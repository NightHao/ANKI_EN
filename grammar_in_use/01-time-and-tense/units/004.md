# Unit 4 — Present continuous and present simple 2

## A

Continuous forms normally describe actions or events that have started but are not
finished. Some verbs describe a state instead, so we do not normally use them in a
continuous form.

Common state verbs include:

- preferences and needs: **prefer, like, need, want**;
- knowledge and opinion: **understand, know, recognise, realise, remember, believe**
  and **suppose**;
- description and connection: **seem, mean, fit, belong, contain** and **consist**.

- “I need some help.” (not “I’m needing”)
- “Do you understand this part?”
- “This jacket doesn’t fit me.”
- “Mina seems tired today.”

## B

The correct form can depend on meaning. When **think** means *believe or have an
opinion*, use the simple form:

- “I think the plan will work.”
- “What do you think of the new design?”

When **think about/of** means *consider*, a continuous form is possible:

- “I’m thinking about your suggestion.”
- “We’re thinking of moving closer to work.”

## C

We normally use simple forms with **see, hear, smell** and **taste** when they describe
perception:

- “Do you hear that noise?” (not normally “Are you hearing?”)
- “The milk smells strange.”
- “This sauce tastes too salty.”

With **look** and **feel**, both forms may be possible for a present condition: “You
look well” or “You’re looking well”; “How do you feel?” or “How are you feeling?”
For a regular state, use the simple: “I usually feel sleepy after lunch.”

## D

We can use **am/is/are being** for temporary, controllable behaviour:

- “Why are you being so impatient? You’re normally calm.”
- “I’m being careful, so please stop worrying.”

Compare “Leo is selfish” (his general character) with “Leo is being selfish” (his
behaviour now). We do not normally use **being** for a condition that is not behaviour:
“She is ill” (not “She is being ill”).

## Exercises

### 4.1

Choose the natural form.

1. I **think / am thinking** this is the right address.
2. We **think / are thinking** about adopting a dog.
3. This soup **tastes / is tasting** delicious.
4. You **are / are being** unusually quiet today.

### 4.2

Correct the sentences where necessary.

1. I’m not understanding the question.
2. Are you feeling better today?
