# Unit 14 — Present perfect and past 2

## A

Study these messages at different times:

> At 10 a.m.: “I’ve written three emails today.”  
> On Saturday: “I wrote three emails yesterday.”

Do not use present perfect with a finished time such as `yesterday`, `last month`, `in 2018`, or `an hour ago`. Use past simple: *We met an hour ago.* / *When did you arrive?* / *What time did the shop close?*

Compare *Has Rina left?* (is she here now?) with *When did Rina leave?* (at what past time?).

## B

Use present perfect for a period that reaches now: *I’ve done a lot today* (today is still continuing), *It hasn’t rained this week*, and *Have you seen the new manager recently?* The period may be life until now: *I’ve never tried surfing.*

Use past simple for a finished period: *I did a lot yesterday.* / *It did not rain last week.* The same phrase can change according to the time of speaking: *Have you called Jo this morning?* is natural in the morning; after lunch, ask *Did you call Jo this morning?*

## C

Duration can show whether the situation still continues:

| Still true now | Finished in the past |
|---|---|
| *I’ve worked at this school for six years.* | *I worked there from 2017 to 2023.* |
| *We’ve been waiting for forty minutes.* | *We waited for forty minutes, then left.* |

The first column uses present perfect because the situation continues. The second uses past simple because it has ended.

## Exercises

### 14.1

Choose the correct form.

1. I **(have bought / bought)** this coat last winter.
2. It is only 9 a.m.; I **(have had / had)** two meetings today.
3. What time **(have you finished / did you finish)** yesterday?
4. We **(have not seen / did not see)** any rain this month.

### 14.2

Complete the sentences.

1. I ___ (live) in this city since 2021 and still live here.
2. I ___ (live) in this city from 2021 to 2024, but then moved.
3. It is now evening. ___ you ___ (speak) to Kian this morning?
4. It is still morning. ___ you ___ (speak) to Kian this morning?

### 14.3

Write the question that fits the answer.

1. “At quarter to nine.” (the train / leave?)
2. “For three weeks.” (how long / you / be / on this project?)
3. “No, never.” (you / ever / climb / a mountain?)
