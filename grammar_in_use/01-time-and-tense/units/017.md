# Unit 17 — `have` and `have got`

## A

Study this exchange:

> “Do you have a charger?”  
> “Yes, I’ve got one in my bag.”

For possession, family relationships, physical features, illnesses, and appointments, `have` and `have got` usually mean the same in the present: *They have a balcony.* / *They’ve got a balcony.* *I have / I’ve got a sore throat.* We do not normally use a continuous form for this meaning: *We have a quiet room* (not *we’re having a quiet room*).

## B

In questions and negatives, use either `do/does + have` or `have/has got`: *Do you have any siblings?* / *Have you got any siblings?* *He doesn’t have a licence.* / *He hasn’t got a licence.* `Have you a licence?` and *I haven’t a licence* exist, but are less usual in modern everyday English.

For the past, use `had`, `did have`, or `didn’t have`, normally without `got`: *Did you have a bike as a child?* / *I didn’t have my wallet.*

## C

Use ordinary `have`, not `have got`, for actions and experiences: *have breakfast, have a shower, have a break, have a chat, have trouble, have a good time.* These can be continuous: *We’re having lunch now.* / *She is having a difficult week.*

Compare *I have sandwiches* (I possess them) with *I have a sandwich for lunch* (I eat one). Only the second is an activity.

## Exercises

### 17.1

Choose the natural form.

1. ___ you ___ (have) any questions? (Use `have got`.)
2. My uncle ___ (not / have got) a car.
3. ___ you ___ (have) a car when you lived in Rome? (Use the past.)
4. We ___ (have got) a large garden.

### 17.2

Choose `have` or `have got` and use the correct form.

1. I usually ___ coffee after lunch.
2. They ___ a great time at the festival right now.
3. Can we ___ a look at the timetable?
4. She ___ an appointment with the dentist tomorrow.

### 17.3

Correct the sentences where necessary.

1. I’m having two brothers.
2. He has got a headache today.
3. Did you got any trouble finding the hotel?
4. We are having a short break.
