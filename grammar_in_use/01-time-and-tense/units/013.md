# Unit 13 — Present perfect and past 1

## A

Study this situation:

> “I can’t open the front door.”  
> “Have you lost your key?”  
> “Yes, but I found it in my coat.”

Present perfect connects a past event to the situation now. *Have you lost your key?* asks whether you do not have it now; *I have found it* says that I have it now. Past simple only reports a past event: *Did you lose your key?* — *Yes, I dropped it on the bus, but I found it later.*

The same contrast works with changing situations: *The wind has stopped, so we can sit outside* means it is calm now. *The wind stopped at noon, then started again* describes a past period.

## B

Use present perfect to announce new or recent information when its result matters now: *I’ve fixed the lamp — try the switch.* / *Our neighbours have adopted a puppy.* Past simple is also possible in many everyday announcements: *I fixed the lamp.*

For people and events wholly in the past, use past simple: *Ada Lovelace wrote influential work on computing.* / *Who designed the first modern bicycle?* These are not current results or new news.

## C

After giving new information with present perfect, we normally use past simple for the story’s details:

> “I’ve cut my hand.”  
> “Oh no. How did you do it?”  
> “I opened a tin without the right tool.”

The question concerns the completed event that caused the current problem. Likewise: *Someone has moved my notebook.* / *Who put it on the shelf?* Do not keep switching to present perfect merely because the news is still relevant.

## Exercises

### 13.1

Choose present perfect or past simple.

1. The screen is working again; I ___ (restart) the computer.
2. The screen ___ (restart) twice during yesterday’s meeting.
3. Where is Miro? He ___ (go) to the pharmacy and will return soon.
4. We ___ (go) to the pharmacy after lunch, then came home.

### 13.2

Complete the short conversations.

1. “I’ve spilled coffee on the report.” “How ___ you ___ (do) that?”
2. “Someone has taken my umbrella.” “When ___ you last ___ (see) it?”
3. “The bus has broken down.” “Where ___ it ___ (break) down?”

### 13.3

Correct the verb form where necessary.

1. Who has discovered penicillin?
2. I have forgotten my password, so I cannot log in.
3. The concert has finished at eleven last night.
