# Unit 12 — `for`, `since`, `when`, and `how long`

## A

Study the two answers:

> “How long have you lived here?”  
> “For eight months — since last November.”

Use `for` with a length of time: *for ten minutes, for years, for a short while.* Use `since` with the starting point: *since 9:30, since Tuesday, since I changed jobs.* Both usually go with a present perfect form when the situation continues now: *The café has been open for three hours.* / *I’ve known it since childhood.*

Do not say *since eight months*. Say *for eight months*. We sometimes omit `for` in affirmative sentences (*They have lived together twelve years*), but normally keep it in negatives: *They haven’t lived together for twelve years.* In a negative sentence, `in` can replace `for`: *I haven’t travelled abroad in two years.*

## B

Do not put `for` before `all`: *I’ve waited all morning* (not *for all morning*). `All morning`, `all week`, and `all my life` already express the whole period.

## C

Compare the question about the beginning with the question about duration:

| Beginning: past simple | Duration: present perfect |
|---|---|
| *When did the workshop start?* — *At noon.* | *How long has the workshop been running?* — *For two hours.* |
| *When did you first meet?* — *At university.* | *How long have you known each other?* — *Since university.* |

`when` asks for a finished point in the past, so use past simple. `how long` asks for the time from then to now.

## D

`It is` and `It has been` can both measure time since a past event: *It’s been six months since I changed my glasses.* This means *I haven’t changed my glasses for six months.* Ask *How long is it since your last lesson?* or *How long has it been since ...?*

## Exercises

### 12.1

Write `for`, `since`, or `all`.

1. I have been awake ___ 5 a.m.
2. The children have been outside ___ two hours.
3. She has worked in that library ___ 2016.
4. It has been windy ___ afternoon.

### 12.2

Choose the appropriate question.

1. Answer: “In 2022.” — **When did you join / How long have you been in** the club?
2. Answer: “For nearly four years.” — **When did you join / How long have you been in** the club?
3. Answer: “At half past eight.” — **When did the lesson begin / How long has the lesson lasted?**

### 12.3

Rewrite using `It’s ... since` or `It has been ... since`.

1. I have not called my uncle for three weeks.
2. We have not eaten at that restaurant for ages.
