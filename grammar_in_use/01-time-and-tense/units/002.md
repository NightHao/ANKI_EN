# Unit 2 — Present simple

## A

We use the present simple for things in general, repeated actions and facts:

- “My brother repairs bicycles.”
- “I normally cook at home.”
- “This road leads to the station.”
- “The library closes at six.”

With **he, she** and **it**, the verb normally ends in **-s** or **-es**: “I teach,
but she teaches”; “they go, but he goes”; “we have, but it has”.

## B

Study this situation:

> Omar is a train driver. He is at home now, so he is not driving. However, he
> **drives** trains for his job.

Use **do/does** to form present-simple questions and negatives:

| question | negative |
|---|---|
| Do you work here? | I do not work here. |
| Does she drive? | She does not drive. |

After **does** or **doesn’t**, use the base form: “Does the shop open on Sundays?”
(not “Does the shop opens?”). When **do** is also the main verb, both forms appear:
“What do you do?” “I design websites.”

## C

The present simple often answers **How often?**

- “I check my email several times a day.”
- “How often does Ava visit her grandparents?”
- “We don’t eat out very often.”

Frequency adverbs normally come before a main verb: “He usually walks.” With **be**,
they come after it: “He is usually early.”

## D

Sometimes speaking is the action itself. For this reason, we normally use the present
simple with verbs such as **promise, apologise, suggest, agree, insist, advise** and
**refuse**:

- “I promise I won’t mention it.” (not “I’m promising”)
- “I apologise for the confusion.”
- “I suggest that we wait until tomorrow.”

## Exercises

### 2.1

Complete the sentences.

1. This app ___ (store) your notes online.
2. Where ___ your cousin ___ (work)?
3. I ___ not ___ (use) cash very often.
4. What ___ this expression ___ (mean)?

### 2.2

Correct the sentences.

1. My sister live in Canada.
2. Does your office opens on Saturday?
3. I’m promising I’ll return the money tomorrow.
4. How often you call your parents?
