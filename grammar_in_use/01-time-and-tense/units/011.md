# Unit 11 — How long have you (been) ...?

## A

Study this exchange:

> “Is Aria still at the clinic?”  
> “Yes. She has been there since Monday.”

Use present perfect when a situation began in the past and continues now. Compare *Aria is at the clinic* (her situation now) with *How long has Aria been at the clinic?* (its duration until now). Say *We’ve known each other for years* and *How long have they had that dog?*, not *How long do they know/have ...?*

## B

For actions and activities, present perfect continuous is often the most natural answer to `how long`: *How long have you been learning Korean?* / *I’ve been learning it since February.* *It has been snowing all day.*

Use present perfect simple for verbs that are not normally continuous, including `know`, `like`, and often `have`: *How long have you liked jazz?* / *I’ve had this number for six months.* With `live` and `work`, either form is possible: *She has lived / has been living nearby for a decade.*

## C

Use simple perfect with `always`: *I’ve always worked from home.* `I’ve always been working from home` usually suggests a temporary recent pattern instead of a lifelong or established fact.

## D

We can also say `haven’t + past participle + for/since` when something has not happened during a period:

> “Do you still talk to your old classmates?”  
> “Not much. I haven’t spoken to Dani since the reunion.”

*He hasn’t taken a holiday for ages* means the last holiday was a long time ago.

## Exercises

### 11.1

Write questions using `how long`.

1. Ellis is in a meeting that began at 10. (Ellis / in the meeting?)
2. Your neighbour owns an old bicycle. (you / have / that bicycle?)
3. A woman says she teaches biology. (you / teach / biology?)

### 11.2

Choose the correct form.

1. I **(know / have known)** Zane since primary school.
2. It **(is raining / has been raining)** since dawn.
3. How long **(do you live / have you lived)** in this area?
4. My parents **(have always lived / have always been living)** near the sea.

### 11.3

Complete with a present perfect form and `for` or `since`.

1. I ___ (not / see) that film ___ last summer.
2. They ___ (wait) ___ nearly an hour.
3. We ___ (be) friends ___ a long time.

11.1 1. How long has Ellis been in the meeting? 2. How long have you had that bicycle? 3. How long have you been teaching biology?

11.2 1. have known 2. has been raining 3. have you lived 4. have always lived.

11.3 1. haven’t seen; since 2. have been waiting; for 3. have been; for.
