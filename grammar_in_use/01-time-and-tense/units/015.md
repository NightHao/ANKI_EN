# Unit 15 — Past perfect

## A

Study this story:

> When we reached the theatre, the doors were locked. The performance had already begun.

`had + past participle` is the past perfect. Start from one past time (*we reached the theatre*); use past perfect for an earlier event (*the performance had begun*). It helps the reader see the order: *When I opened the fridge, somebody had eaten the cake.* / *She did not join us because she had promised to help her sister.*

## B

Present perfect looks back from now, but past perfect looks back from a past time:

| Looking back from now | Looking back from then |
|---|---|
| *I’ve seen that face before, but I cannot place it.* | *I had seen that face before, but I could not place it.* |
| *We’ve just eaten, so we are full.* | *We’d just eaten, so we were full.* |

Choose the tense that matches the time of the surrounding story.

## C

Compare these answers:

> “Was Nadia still there when you arrived?”  
> “Yes, but she left soon afterwards.”  
> “No, she had already left.”

Use past simple for an event after the reference time; use past perfect for an event before it. *When I called, Ren was at work* describes the situation then. *When I called, Ren had just got home* puts the arrival before my call.

## Exercises

### 15.1

Complete with past simple or past perfect.

1. By the time the nurse ___ (arrive), the patient ___ (fall) asleep.
2. I ___ (not / recognise) the building because it ___ (change) so much.
3. When we ___ (get) to the station, the train ___ already ___ (leave).

### 15.2

Choose the better continuation.

1. Mira was not hungry because she **(had eaten / ate)** before the meeting.
2. We saw a window that **(had broken / had been broken)** during the storm. 
3. I read the article after my colleague **(had sent / sent)** it to me.

### 15.3

Put the events in a clear two-sentence past story. Use the prompts.

1. first: the guests / leave; later: we / arrive
2. first: I / visit the gallery; later: my friend / recommend it
