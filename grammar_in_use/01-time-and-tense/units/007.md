# Unit 7 — Present perfect: present result and new information

## A

Study this situation:

> “Why are you standing outside?” “I’ve left my keys in the flat.”

We use **have/has + past participle** when a past event has a result now. “I’ve forgotten her number” means I cannot remember it now. “The road has closed” means it is closed now. The past participle may be regular (*finished*) or irregular (*lost, written, gone*).

## B

The present perfect often introduces news. “There has been an accident” and “The police have arrested a suspect” give the new situation first. We can then give completed details in past simple: “It happened near the bridge at six.”

In informal conversation, past simple is sometimes possible for recent events: “I just ate.” Present perfect is the safer international choice when the present result matters: “I’ve just eaten, so I’m not hungry.”

## C

Compare **gone to** and **been to**:

- “Mina has gone to Rome.” She is there or travelling there now.
- “Mina has been to Rome.” She visited and has returned.

Use **just** for a very recent event, **already** for earlier than expected, and **yet** in questions and negatives: “Have you sent it yet?” “I haven’t sent it yet.” “I’ve already sent it.”

## Exercises

### 7.1

Complete the sentences.

1. I cannot open the file. I ___ (forget) the password.
2. The floor is wet. Someone ___ (spill) water.
3. Is Daniel here? No, he ___ (go) to the pharmacy.

### 7.2

Choose **just**, **already**, or **yet**.

1. I have ___ booked the table.
2. Have you finished the report ___?
3. We have ___ arrived, so give us a moment.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**7.1** 1. have forgotten 2. has spilled 3. has gone. Each past event explains the present situation.

**7.2** 1. already 2. yet 3. just. *Yet* is natural in questions; *already* means earlier than expected.
