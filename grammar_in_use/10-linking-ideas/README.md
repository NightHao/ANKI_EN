# Chapter 10 — Linking ideas

## Use it when

You need an answer to move somewhere: add a reason, show a result, qualify a claim, give an example, or change direction. A Band 7 answer is not a chain of fashionable connectors. It is easy to follow because each sentence has a clear relationship to the last one.

## Core contrast

Use **because / since / as** to give a reason: *I cook at home because it saves time during the week.* Use **so / therefore / as a result** for a result. In conversation, *so* is usually the natural choice: *The shop closes early, so I go on Saturday.*

**Although / though / even though** introduce a contrast inside one sentence: *Although I enjoy the city, I miss having more quiet space.* **But** joins two equal ideas: *I enjoy the city, but I miss quiet space.* Do not stack them: not *Although … but …*.

Use **if** for a real condition and **unless** for “if not”: *I’ll take the train if it rains.* / *I’ll walk unless it rains.* For a concession, **even if** means the result will not change: *I’ll go even if I’m tired.*

For spoken organisation, simple frames are powerful: *The main reason is …; another thing is …; for example …; that said …; in the end …* They help listeners, but they should point to a real idea. Avoid beginning every sentence with *Moreover* or *Furthermore*; those sound written and rehearsed in a casual answer.

## Natural examples

> I like working from home because I can concentrate, but I still go to the office once or twice a week for the social side.

> I didn’t enjoy the course at first. Once we started doing practical projects, though, it became much more useful.

> Public transport is cheaper in my city. That said, it is not always convenient late at night.

> I try to keep my weekends free. Otherwise, work tends to spread into the whole week.

> For example, I started leaving my phone in another room while I study, so I check it far less often.

Notice that *though* can sit at the end of a spoken sentence. It lightly adds a contrast without making the sentence heavy.

## Common failure

**Unclear:** *I like reading. Because it is relaxing. And I learn many things.*  
**Better:** *I like reading because it helps me relax, and I usually learn something new as well.*

A sentence beginning with *because* can work in informal speech when the context is obvious, but do not make fragments your default. Build one complete relationship first.

**Wrong:** *Despite it was expensive, I bought it.*  
**Better:** *Although it was expensive, I bought it.* / *Despite the price, I bought it.*

After **despite / in spite of**, use a noun, pronoun, or *-ing* form—not a full clause unless you add *the fact that*.

## Say it yourself

Answer: “Has technology made daily life better?” Speak for one minute.

1. State your position.
2. Give a reason with *because*.
3. Add a concrete example.
4. Qualify it with *that said* or *although*.
5. End with a result using *so* or *as a result*.

Record it once. On the second attempt, remove any connector that does not express a real link.
