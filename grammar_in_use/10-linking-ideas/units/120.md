# Unit 120 — `by`, `until`, and `by the time`

## A

Study this deadline:

> Please upload the form **by** Thursday. The office needs it on or before that day.

Use **by + time** for a deadline: *by noon; by next week; by now*. It means “not later than”. *The package should be here by now* means it should already have arrived. Use **until/till + time or clause** for something that continues up to an endpoint: *The office is open until six; Wait until I call.*

## B

Compare the meaning:

> I will be away until Friday. (I return on Friday.)  
> I will be back by Friday. (I return on or before Friday.)

Likewise, *I will work until six* describes a continuing activity, while *I will have finished by six* gives a completion deadline.

## C

Use **by the time + clause** when another event will be completed or changed before that time:

> By the time we reach the venue, the doors will have opened.  
> By the time I got home, the guests had left.

For the past, this often combines with the past perfect. **By then/by that time** can replace a repeated clause: *The train finally arrived, but by then we had left.*

## Exercises

### 120.1

Complete with `by` or `until`.

1. The library is open ___ nine tonight.
2. Please reply ___ the end of the day.
3. I will not decide ___ I have read the report.
4. They should have reached the hotel ___ now.

### 120.2

Choose the sentence that matches the meaning.

1. You stop studying at 10.30. (I will study by 10.30. / I will study until 10.30.)
2. Your work is complete no later than 10.30. (I will finish by 10.30. / I will finish until 10.30.)

### 120.3

Complete with `By the time`.

1. We arrived late. The concert had started.
2. We will get through security. Our flight will be boarding.
