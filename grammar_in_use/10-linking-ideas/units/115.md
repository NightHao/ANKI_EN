# Unit 115 — Conditions with `unless`, `as long as`, and `provided`

## A

Study this club rule:

> You cannot borrow equipment **unless** you show your membership card.

**Unless** means “except if” or “if ... not”: *Unless we leave now, we will miss the last bus* = *If we do not leave now, we will miss it.* It often expresses a condition necessary for a result: *I will not mention it unless you ask.* Avoid a second negative after `unless` when the intended meaning is simple.

## B

**As long as**, **so long as**, **provided (that)**, and **providing (that)** all mean “only if this condition is met”.

> You can use the studio as long as you book ahead.  
> We can meet outside, provided that the weather is dry.

They make the condition explicit; `provided/providing` often sound a little more formal than `as long as`.

## C

For future meaning, use a present tense after these words: *I will wait unless the train is cancelled; Providing she is free, we will meet tomorrow.* Do not use *will* in the condition clause. `Unless` can also introduce a preference or exception: *We can take the stairs — unless you would rather use the lift.*

## Exercises

### 115.1

Rewrite with `unless`.

1. If you do not back up the files, you may lose them.
2. I will not sign the form if I do not understand it.
3. We cannot start if the presenter does not arrive.

### 115.2

Complete with `as long as`, `provided`, or `providing`.

1. You may borrow my bicycle ___ you lock it afterwards.
2. The picnic will go ahead ___ the forecast is good.
3. I do not mind which route we take ___ it is safe.

### 115.3

Correct the future condition.

1. We will go out unless it will rain.
2. Providing the room will be free, we will use it tomorrow.
