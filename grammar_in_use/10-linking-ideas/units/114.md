# Unit 114 — Precaution with `in case`

## A

Study this preparation:

> I saved an offline map **in case** my connection disappears.

Use **in case + clause** when you do something now as a precaution against a possible later problem. The action happens whether the problem occurs or not: *Take a charger in case your battery runs low.* **Just in case** suggests that the possibility is small: *I do not expect snow, but I packed gloves just in case.*

Use a present form after `in case` for future meaning: *in case it rains*, not *in case it will rain*.

## B

`In case` and `if` do different jobs.

> I will print two copies in case the printer fails. (I print them now.)  
> If the printer fails, I will use the copy shop. (This action depends on failure.)

Use `in case` to explain the reason for a precaution, not to state a condition for a later decision.

## C

For a past precaution, use **in case + past**: *I left a note in case you arrived before me.* You can also use a past perfect when the earlier event may already have happened: *We rang again in case they had not heard the first call.*

## D

**In case of + noun** is common in notices and instructions: *In case of power failure, use the stairs.* It means “if there is ...”; do not follow it with a full clause.

## Exercises

### 114.1

Complete with `in case` and a suitable verb form.

1. Bring a notebook ___ the Wi-Fi is unavailable.
2. I copied the address ___ I needed it later.
3. I will take a light jacket ___ it gets cold.

### 114.2

Choose `in case` or `if`.

1. I will leave the key with a neighbour ___ you arrive before me.
2. ___ you arrive before me, call this number.
3. We bought extra chairs ___ more guests came.

### 114.3

Rewrite the notice using `in case of`.

1. If there is an earthquake, do not use the lift.
2. If there is a medical emergency, contact reception.

**114.3** 1. In case of an earthquake, do not use the lift. 2. In case of a medical emergency, contact reception.
