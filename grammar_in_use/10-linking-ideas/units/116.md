# Unit 116 — Time, reason, and simultaneous change with `as`

## A

Study this moment:

> As I stepped onto the platform, the train began to move.

Use **as** when two actions happen at the same time: *As we walked, we planned the trip; Please close the gate as you leave.* It can also introduce an action already in progress: *I saw the message as I was getting off the bus.* **Just as** means exactly at that moment: *Just as I opened the door, the lights went out.*

Use `as` for a longer simultaneous change too: *As the evening went on, the room became quieter; I grew more confident as I practised.*

## B

`As` can also mean **because**, often when the reason is already clear or background information:

> As the shops were closed, we cooked at home.  
> Since it is late, let us take a taxi.

`Since` has a similar use. Use **because** when you want to give the reason strong emphasis or make it unmistakable.

## C

Compare `as` and `when`. Use **as** for overlap: *As I drove, I listened to the news.* Use **when** when one event follows another or simply identifies a time: *When I arrived, I called home.*

> As she was on holiday, she missed the meeting. (because)  
> When she was on holiday, she read every day. (during that period)

## Exercises

### 116.1

Join each pair with `as` meaning “at the same time”.

1. I walked through the park. I listened to a voice message.
2. The audience stood up. The musician came on stage.

### 116.2

Join with `as` meaning “because”.

1. The roads were icy. We stayed at home.
2. I had the details already. I did not call her.

### 116.3

Choose `as` or `when`.

1. ___ I finished the report, I sent it to the team.
2. ___ the temperature fell, the lake began to freeze.
3. We saw a deer ___ we were hiking.
