# Unit 119 — `during`, `for`, and `while`

## A

Study these answers:

> “When did the power fail?” “**During** the concert.”  
> “How long was it off?” “**For** twenty minutes.”

Use **during + noun phrase** to say when something happened within a period: *during the meeting; during our visit; during the night*. With morning, night, summer, and similar time words, `in` is often possible too: *in the summer / during the summer*.

Use **for + length of time** to say how long: *for three hours; for a month; for ages*. Do not use `during` with a number of hours or days.

## B

Compare `during` and `while`:

> I heard the news during the meal.  
> I heard the news while we were eating.

`During` is followed by a noun phrase; **while** is followed by a subject and verb: *while I was eating; while the meal was being served*. Both describe something happening within another activity or period.

## C

For future time after **while**, use a present form, not `will`: *I will read while I wait*, not *while I will wait*. `While` can connect two simultaneous actions: *She cooked while I set the table.*

## Exercises

### 119.1

Complete with `during` or `for`.

1. The shop was closed ___ two weeks.
2. I received three calls ___ the presentation.
3. We stayed with them ___ the weekend.
4. It was noisy ___ the night.

### 119.2

Complete with `during` or `while`.

1. Please do not talk ___ the recording is playing.
2. I took notes ___ the lecture.
3. The lights failed ___ we were cooking.

### 119.3

Correct the future-time sentence.

1. I will make coffee while you will unpack.
2. What will you do while you will be in the waiting room?
