# Unit 117 — `like` and `as`

## A

Study this description:

> The frozen path was **like** a mirror, and walking on it felt **like** skating.

Use **like** to mean “similar to”. It is followed by a noun, pronoun, or `-ing` form: *like a mirror; like that; like skating*. We also use it after verbs such as *look, sound,* and *feel*: *It sounds like a baby crying.* For examples, `like` can mean “for example”: *outdoor activities like cycling and climbing*. `Such as` is possible too.

## B

Use **as + subject + verb** for “in the same way/condition as”:

> Leave the files as they are.  
> Please arrange the chairs as I showed you.

In conversation, `like + subject + verb` is also common: *Leave them like they are.* But say **like this**, not *as this*. We also say **as usual**, **as always**, and **the same as**: *Her answer is the same as mine.*

## C

In expressions such as **as you know, as I said, as I expected,** and **do as you like**, `as` means “what/in the way that”. `Like` is not usual there, except *like I said*.

> As you know, the office closes early today.  
> They did as they promised.

## D

`As + noun` can mean “in the role or position of”, not similarity. Compare:

> As a volunteer, I answer visitors’ questions. (I am a volunteer.)  
> I am like a volunteer today. (I am not one, but I have a similar role.)

We also say *use a box as a table* and *the announcement came as a surprise*.

## Exercises

### 117.1

Complete with `like` or `as`.

1. This fabric feels ___ silk.
2. Please leave the lights ___ they are.
3. ___ usual, the first bus was late.
4. She works ___ a translator.

### 117.2

Choose the natural completion.

1. The soup tastes (like water / as water).
2. You can do (as you prefer / like this) with the spare chairs.
3. The new device is the same (as / like) mine.

### 117.3

Rewrite to make the role clear.

1. I was a shop assistant during university. Use `as`.
2. The old warehouse has a similar appearance to a castle. Use `like`.
