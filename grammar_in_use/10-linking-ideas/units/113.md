# Unit 113 — `although`, `though`, `despite`, and `in spite of`

## A

Study this situation:

> Although the café was packed, we found a quiet corner and stayed for hours.

Use **although + subject + verb** to introduce a contrast: *Although the task was difficult, we finished it.* It is similar to *but*: *The task was difficult, but we finished it.* Do not use *but* in the same clause after *although*.

Compare the meaning of **although** and **because**: *Although the road was wet, we drove* contrasts with the expected result; *Because the road was wet, we drove slowly* gives a reason.

## B

After **in spite of** or **despite**, use a noun phrase, pronoun, or `-ing` form:

> In spite of the delay, the team stayed calm.  
> Despite feeling ill, Arun came to work.  
> In spite of this, we continued.

Say **despite** (not *despite of*). For a full clause, use *in spite of the fact that ...* or *despite the fact that ...*.

## C

Compare the structures:

| Clause | Noun phrase / `-ing` |
|---|---|
| Although the wind was strong, we sailed. | Despite the strong wind, we sailed. |
| Although I was tired, I read on. | In spite of being tired, I read on. |

**Because of** introduces a cause, not a contrast: *We cancelled because of the wind.*

## D

**Though** has the same basic meaning as *although*. In conversation it can come at the end: *The room is small. It has great light, though.*

## E

Use **even though** for a particularly striking contrast: *Even though the tickets were expensive, they sold out in an hour.* Do not use *even + clause* without *though*.

## Exercises

### 113.1

Complete with `although`, `despite`, or `in spite of`.

1. ___ the heat, the runners continued.
2. ___ it was late, nobody wanted to leave.
3. We enjoyed the hike ___ getting lost twice.

### 113.2

Rewrite using the word in brackets.

1. The film was long, but I watched it all. (`although`)
2. She had little experience, but she got the job. (`despite`)
3. The traffic was heavy, so we arrived late. (`because of`)

### 113.3

Put `though` at the end of each second sentence.

1. The laptop is old. It still works well.
2. I did not enjoy the exhibition. The building was beautiful.
