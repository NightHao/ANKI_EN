# Unit 68 — `-ing` clauses describing accompanying events

## A

Study this situation:

> A woman hurried across the station platform carrying two bags and talking on her phone.

We can add an `-ing` clause to describe an action happening at the same time: *He sat by the window reading.* *A dog ran across the road barking.* This is a compact way to join two related actions with the same subject.

An `-ing` clause can also describe something happening during the main action: *I hurt my wrist lifting the suitcase.* *Take care crossing the bridge.* `While -ing` and `when -ing` are also possible: *While waiting, I checked my messages.*

## B

Use `having + past participle` for an earlier completed action: *Having printed the tickets, we left for the airport.* `After + -ing` has a similar meaning: *After printing the tickets, we left.* These openings are more usual in writing than in relaxed conversation.

When this type of clause begins a sentence, put a comma after it: *Having checked the address, I sent the parcel.*

## C

An opening `-ing` clause can give a reason: *Feeling unwell, she went home early.* *Not knowing the answer, I said nothing.* For a completed earlier reason, use `having done`: *Having visited the gallery before, we chose a different museum.*

The understood subject must be the same as the subject of the main clause. Say *Walking home, I saw a fox*, not *Walking home, a fox appeared* if the fox was not walking.

## Exercises

### 68.1

Combine each pair using an `-ing` clause.

1. Diego walked through the market. He looked at the fruit stalls.
2. I slipped. I was getting off the train.
3. She had completed the form. Then she handed it to the receptionist.

### 68.2

Choose the best opening.

1. ___ tired after the flight, we booked a room near the airport. (`Feeling` / `Felt`)
2. ___ the route twice, I knew where to turn. (`Having checked` / `Checking`)
3. ___ a bike, Noor takes the metro to work. (`Not having` / `Not have`)

<summary>Answers</summary>

**68.1** 1. *Diego walked through the market looking at the fruit stalls.* 2. *I slipped getting off the train.* 3. *Having completed the form, she handed it to the receptionist.*

**68.2** 1. *Feeling* 2. *Having checked* 3. *Not having*.
