# Unit 54 — Verb + `to`

## A

After missing the last bus, Rina makes a new plan:

> “I’ve decided to leave earlier tomorrow.”

Verbs such as `decide`, `plan`, `agree`, `offer`, `refuse`, `hope`, `promise`, `manage`, `fail`, `afford`, `learn`, and `tend` are followed by `to + base verb`:

> We agreed to share a taxi.  
> He failed to notice the sign.  
> I can’t afford to replace it yet.  
> My neighbour tends to wake up early.

The negative is `not to + verb`:

> They promised not to mention the surprise.

## B

This pattern contrasts with Unit 53 verbs:

> I enjoy reading.  
> I decided to read more.

`Dare` can take an infinitive with or without `to` after ordinary verbs:

> She didn’t dare (to) ask.

After `daren’t` or `dare not`, use the base verb: `I daren’t tell him.`

## C

After `seem`, `appear`, `pretend`, and `claim`, the infinitive can show a state, an action in progress, or an earlier action:

> The café seems to be closed.  
> He pretended to be asleep.  
> She pretended to be reading the notice.  
> I seem to have lost my card.  
> They claimed not to have received the email.

## D

We also use question word + infinitive after verbs such as `know`, `decide`, `remember`, and `ask`:

> I don’t know what to wear.  
> Have you decided where to stay?  
> Could you show me how to connect this printer?

Use `whether + to`: `She is deciding whether to apply.`

## Exercises

### 54.1

Complete the sentences.

1. We managed ___ (find) a quieter table.  
2. I promised ___ (not / be) late.  
3. She seems ___ (forget) the appointment.

### 54.2

Complete with a question word and `to`.

1. I don’t know ___ (put) these boxes.  
2. Can you explain ___ (use) this setting?

### 54.3

Correct: `I decided going by train.`
