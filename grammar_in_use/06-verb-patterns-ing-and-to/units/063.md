# Unit 63 — `There’s no point in`, `It’s worth`, and related patterns

## A

Study this exchange:

> “Should we wait outside the closed shop?”
> “There’s no point in waiting. It will not reopen today.”

Use `there’s no point in + -ing` when an action cannot achieve anything useful. `It’s no use + -ing` and `it’s no good + -ing` express a similar idea: *It’s no use calling; her phone is off.*

For a question, use `the point of + -ing`: *What is the point of saving the file if you delete it later?*

## B

`It’s worth + -ing` means an action has enough value to justify the effort: *The coastal road is worth taking.* Its negative is *It is not worth arguing about.* You can also say *The concert was worth it* or *not worth it*.

Use `worth + -ing` after a noun too: *That is an idea worth exploring.* The action is valuable, not “worth to explore”.

## C

Use `have trouble/difficulty/a problem + -ing` for something hard to do: *I had trouble connecting to the video call.* *Did you have any difficulty finding the building?* We do not say *difficulty to find* in this pattern.

## D

Use `spend` or `waste time + -ing`: *She spent the afternoon organising her notes.* *Do not waste time blaming people.* `be busy + -ing` describes somebody occupied with an activity: *I cannot talk now; I’m busy preparing dinner.*

For many activities, use `go + -ing`: *We went hiking on Saturday.* *He has gone shopping.*

## Exercises

### 63.1

Complete with the `-ing` form.

1. There is no point in ___ the same question again. (ask)
2. The documentary is worth ___. (watch)
3. I had difficulty ___ the label in the dark. (read)
4. They spent two hours ___ for the missing key. (look)

### 63.2

Rewrite using the word in brackets.

1. Taking a taxi is unnecessary; the station is nearby. (`worth`)
2. Trying to persuade him will not change his decision. (`no use`)
3. We went on a short trip in the mountains on foot. (`hiking`)
