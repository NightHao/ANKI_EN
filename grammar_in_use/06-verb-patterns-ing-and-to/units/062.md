# Unit 62 — Verb + preposition + `-ing`

## A

Study this message from Omar:

> “Sorry for replying so late. I finally succeeded in fixing the shared calendar.”

Some verbs need a particular preposition before an object. When that object is an action, use `-ing`: `apologise for doing`, `succeed in doing`, `insist on doing`, `think of/about doing`, and `dream of doing`.

The preposition belongs to the verb. We say *They decided against moving*, not *decided to moving*. We say *Do you feel like going out?*, not *feel like to go out*.

## B

These patterns can include another person before the action: *We are looking forward to Maya joining us.* *I do not approve of people leaving rubbish in the park.*

Some verbs have an object before the preposition: `accuse somebody of`, `congratulate somebody on`, `prevent/stop somebody from`, `suspect somebody of`, and `thank somebody for`.

*The guard stopped visitors from entering.* *Everyone congratulated Amir on winning the race.* `Stop somebody doing` is also possible: *Nothing can stop her following her plan.*

## C

Many of these forms are common in the passive: *He was accused of copying the report.* *The company was suspected of hiding the figures.* Keep the same preposition.

With `apologise`, say who receives the apology: *I apologised to my neighbour for making noise.* (not *I apologised my neighbour*).

## Exercises

### 62.1

Complete with the correct preposition and `-ing` form.

1. We are thinking ___ ___ the meeting online. (hold)
2. Priya insisted ___ ___ for everyone’s lunch. (pay)
3. The team succeeded ___ ___ the deadline. (meet)
4. I would not dream ___ ___ your private messages. (read)

### 62.2

Rewrite each sentence using the word given.

1. Heavy snow meant that the coach could not leave. (`prevent`)
2. “You helped me move; thank you,” said Noor. (`thank`)
3. The police believed that he had used a false name. (`suspect`)

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**62.1** 1. *of holding* 2. *on paying* 3. *in meeting* 4. *of reading*.

**62.2** 1. *Heavy snow prevented the coach from leaving.* 2. *Noor thanked me for helping her move.* 3. *The police suspected him of using a false name.*
