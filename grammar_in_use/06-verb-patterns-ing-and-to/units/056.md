# Unit 56 — Verb + `-ing` or `to`: memory and completed actions

## A

On the way to the airport, Ken turns back:

> “I remembered to bring my passport.”

`Remember + to` means first remembering a duty, then doing it:

> Remember to charge your phone.  
> I forgot to reply, so I wrote this morning.

`Remember + -ing` means having a memory of an earlier action:

> I remember meeting her at a conference.  
> Do you remember locking the door?

Compare: `I remembered to lock it` means the task happened; `I remember locking it` describes the memory now.

## B

`Regret + -ing` looks back with sorrow:

> I regret saying that in anger.  
> She regrets not taking the course.

`Regret to say/tell/inform` is a formal way to introduce bad news now:

> We regret to inform you that the shop will close early.

## C

`Go on + -ing` means continue the same activity:

> He paused, then went on explaining the route.

`Go on + to` means move to a new activity or topic:

> After introductions, she went on to discuss costs.

## D

With `begin`, `start`, `continue`, `intend`, and `bother`, both forms are often possible:

> It started to rain. / It started raining.

After a progressive verb, speakers usually avoid a second `-ing`: `It’s starting to rain` is more natural than `It’s starting raining.`

## Exercises

### 56.1

Choose the correct form.

1. I remember ___ (see) that painting as a child.  
2. Remember ___ (send) the invoice today.  
3. He went on ___ (describe) his first job, then changed topic.

### 56.2

Complete with `regret`.

1. I ___ (not / accept) the offer.  *(a past decision)*  
2. We ___ ___ you that your booking has changed. *(formal notice)*

### 56.3

Correct: `After the safety rules, she went on explaining the budget.` (She began a new topic.)

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**56.1** 1. seeing 2. to send 3. describing

**56.2** 1. regret not accepting 2. regret to inform

**56.3** After the safety rules, she went on to explain the budget. `To explain` introduces a new topic.
