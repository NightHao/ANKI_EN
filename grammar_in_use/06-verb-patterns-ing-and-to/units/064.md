# Unit 64 — Purpose and noun + infinitive patterns

## A

Study this situation:

> Mei went back to the office to collect her charger. She needed it to finish a presentation.

`to + verb` can give the purpose of an action, thing, or arrangement: *I called to check the address.* *This notice is to remind visitors about the rules.* *They hired a guide to show them around.*

## B

After a noun, `to + verb` can describe something available, necessary, or possible: *Is there a plug to use?* *I have three emails to answer.* *We need somebody to ask.*

Keep a necessary preposition at the end: *There is nothing to write with.* *She needs a bench to sit on.* You can also say *time/money/a chance/an opportunity/energy to do something*: *He had no opportunity to explain.*

## C

Compare `for + noun` with `to + verb`:

| Noun: what it is for | Infinitive: why someone acts |
|---|---|
| *We stopped for coffee.* | *We stopped to buy coffee.* |
| *These gloves are for gardening.* | *I bought gloves to work in the garden.* |

Use `for + somebody + to + verb` when you name the person: *There was no room for the children to play.*

## D

`so that` introduces a purpose clause, especially with `can`, `could`, `will`, or `would`: *I shared the link so that you could join later.* *Speak clearly so everyone can hear.*

## Exercises

### 64.1

Complete the sentences.

1. I opened the window ___ some fresh air. (get)
2. Do you have a pen ___ this form ___? (write / with)
3. We need more time ___ a decision. (make)
4. There were no seats ___ us ___. (for / sit on)

### 64.2

Choose `for`, `to`, or `so that`.

1. I wore boots ___ walk through the snow.
2. This shelf is ___ keeping spices.
3. Leave a note ___ your colleague can find the parcel.
4. We stopped ___ a quick meal, then continued driving.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**64.1** 1. *to get* 2. *to write; with* 3. *to make* 4. *for; to sit on*.

**64.2** 1. *to* 2. *for* 3. *so that* 4. *for*.
