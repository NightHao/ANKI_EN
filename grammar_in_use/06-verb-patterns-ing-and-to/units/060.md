# Unit 60 — Preposition + `-ing`

## A

Study this note from a team leader:

> “Thanks for staying late. By checking every number twice, we found the mistake without delaying the launch.”

When a verb follows a preposition, use its `-ing` form: *after lunching*, *before leaving*, *without complaining*, *instead of driving*. This applies whether the preposition gives a reason, time, method, or contrast.

You can put a person before the `-ing` form: *I was annoyed at Sam arriving so late.* *She left without anyone noticing.*

## B

`by + -ing` explains the method: *You can save the file by pressing this icon.* It does not mean the purpose; use `to + verb` for purpose: *I pressed the icon to save the file.*

Use `without + -ing` for an action that did not happen: *He left without saying goodbye.* `Before/after + -ing` is common when the subject is the same: *After finishing the report, I sent it.* You can instead use a full clause: *After I finished the report, I sent it.*

## C

Be careful with `to`. Often it begins an infinitive: *We decided to leave.* But in phrases such as `look forward to`, `be used to`, and `object to`, it is a preposition, so use `-ing`: *I am looking forward to seeing you.* *They objected to paying the fee.*

## Exercises

### 60.1

Complete with the `-ing` form.

1. Thank you for ___ me home. (drive)
2. We solved the problem by ___ the instructions slowly. (read)
3. Before ___ the oven, check that it is empty. (use)
4. She went out without ___ her umbrella. (take)

### 60.2

Choose the correct form.

1. I am not looking forward to (`move` / `moving`) next week.
2. He improved his pronunciation (`by recording` / `to record`) himself every day.
3. We met for coffee instead of (`to go` / `going`) straight home.
4. I went to the library (`to study` / `by studying`) quietly.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**60.1** 1. *driving* 2. *reading* 3. *using* 4. *taking*.

**60.2** 1. *moving* 2. *by recording* 3. *going* 4. *to study*; this states the purpose of going.
