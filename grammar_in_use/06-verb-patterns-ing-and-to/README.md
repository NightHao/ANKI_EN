# Chapter 6 — Verb patterns: `-ing` and `to`

## Use it when

You need a second verb: talking about plans, habits, recommendations, memories, rules, or a change of mind. The choice after the first verb is often a fixed pattern, but several choices also change the meaning. Learn useful chunks, then notice the contrast.

## Core contrast

Some verbs are commonly followed by `-ing`:

> I enjoy **cooking** for friends.  
> She suggested **taking** a taxi.  
> We avoided **driving** in the storm.

Others normally take `to + base verb`:

> I hope **to visit** next year.  
> He decided **to leave** early.  
> They offered **to help**.

After a preposition, use `-ing`, not an infinitive:

> Thank you for **waiting**.  
> Before **leaving**, check the address.  
> I am interested in **learning** Korean.

`To` is not always an infinitive marker. In `look forward to`, `be used to`, and `object to`, it is a preposition, so it is followed by a noun or `-ing`:

> I am looking forward to **seeing** you.  
> She is used to **working** late.

With an object, many verbs use `object + to + verb`:

> My manager encouraged me **to apply**.  
> I reminded Leo **to bring** his ID.

Do not invent `suggest someone to do`. Say `suggest doing`, `suggest that someone do`, or `suggest something to someone`.

## Natural examples

Some verbs allow both forms with little practical difference:

> It started **raining / to rain** just as we left.  
> I like **reading / to read** on the train.

With `like`, `love`, and `hate`, `-ing` often describes a general enjoyment or dislike; `to` can point to a habit or choice.

> I like **cycling**.  
> I like **to check** the route before I drive somewhere new.

Other pairs change the message:

> I remembered **locking** the door. *(memory of a past action)*  
> I remembered **to lock** the door. *(I did not forget the task.)*

> Please stop **talking**. *(end the activity)*  
> We stopped **to get** coffee. *(pause one activity for a purpose)*

> Try **restarting** the app. *(experiment; see whether it helps)*  
> Try **to restart** the app. *(make an effort; perhaps it is difficult)*

> I regret **telling** him. *(I am sorry about a past action.)*  
> We regret **to inform** you ... *(formal announcement now)*

Use `go + -ing` for leisure activities named by an activity: `go swimming`, `go shopping`, `go hiking`. It is not a general replacement for `go to do`.

## Common failure

Do not choose the form by translating one word at a time:

> Wrong: I suggested to meet after work.  
> Better: I suggested meeting after work.  
> Better: I suggested that we meet after work.

Do not confuse a completed task with a remembered experience:

> Wrong for a task: I remembered locking the window, so I went back and locked it.  
> Better: I remembered to lock the window, so I did it before leaving.

When you are unsure in conversation, a full clause is often safer: `I think we should leave now` rather than forcing an unfamiliar verb pattern.

## Say it yourself

1. Say one thing you enjoy doing and one routine you like to do.
2. Complete both meanings: `I stopped ___ because ...` and `I stopped ___ to ...`.
3. Give a friend advice using `try`, then explain whether it is an experiment or an effort.
4. Tell a short story using `remember to` or `forget to`, plus one preposition followed by `-ing`.
