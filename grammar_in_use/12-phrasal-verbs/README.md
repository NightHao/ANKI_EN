# Chapter 12 — Phrasal verbs

## Use it when

You want to understand and produce ordinary expressions such as `find out`, `put
off`, `carry on`, and `work out`. Phrasal verbs are common in conversation, but
memorising long alphabetical lists is inefficient. Learn each expression as a verb
pattern inside a short situation.

## Core contrast

A phrasal verb combines a verb with a particle. The combination often has a meaning
that is not predictable from the two words:

> We **worked out** the answer.  
> I **ran into** an old colleague.

First learn whether the phrasal verb takes an object.

- no object: `The car broke down.`
- object: `They called off the meeting.`

Then learn its word order. Some object-taking phrasal verbs are separable:

> turn off the light  
> turn the light off  
> turn it off

With a pronoun, the middle position is required: `turn it off`, not `turn off it`.
Other combinations are inseparable:

> look after a child  
> come across an interesting article

Do not move the object unless you have learned that the pattern allows it.

## Natural examples

> I found out about the change through a colleague.

> We had to put off the trip because of the weather.

> My laptop froze, but it started working again after I turned it off and back on.

> I came across an old photo while I was clearing out a drawer.

> The first solution did not work out, so we tried a simpler one.

Notice the surrounding grammar. `Put off` is commonly followed by a noun or `-ing`
form: `put off the meeting`; `put off making a decision`. `Find out` can be followed
by a question word or a `that` clause: `find out why`; `find out that the shop had
closed`.

## Common failure

**Wrong:** *I put off to call her.*  
**Better:** *I put off calling her.*

Learn the complement with the phrasal verb, not as a separate afterthought.

**Wrong:** *Please turn off it.*  
**Better:** *Please turn it off.*

The pronoun goes between the verb and particle in a separable phrasal verb.

Do not replace a clear one-word verb merely to sound informal. `Investigate the
problem` and `look into the problem` can both be natural; the appropriate choice
depends on context and register.

## Say it yourself

1. Describe a plan that was delayed. Use `put off` and explain why.
2. Tell a short story about discovering information. Use `find out`.
3. Explain a small technical problem with `turn off`, `start up`, or `break down`.
4. Replace every object with a pronoun and check the word order aloud.
