# Unit 137 — Phrasal verbs: meaning, grammar, and word order

## A

Study this journey:

> The bus was full, so we could not **get on**. A taxi **drove off**, then **came back** for us.

Phrasal verbs combine a verb with a short word such as `on`, `off`, `out`, or `back`. Sometimes the meaning is movement: *turn round, come back, get on*. Often the combination has a special meaning: *break down* (stop working), *find out* (discover), *give up* (stop trying), *get by* (manage with what you have), and *get on* (do/progress).

## B

Some phrasal verbs need another preposition: *look up at the roof; run away from home; keep up with the group; look forward to the holiday.* Learn the whole pattern, not only the first two words.

## C

Many phrasal verbs have an object. With a noun, the object can often go in either position: *turn on the heater / turn the heater on; take off your boots / take your boots off.*

## D

With a pronoun object, put the pronoun in the middle: *turn it on; take them off; wake her up; throw it away.* Do not say *turn on it* or *throw away it*.
