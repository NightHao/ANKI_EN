# Unit 138 — Phrasal verbs with `in` and `out`

## A

Study a move:

> We **moved in** on Saturday, but first we had to **get into** the building with a temporary code.

`In` often means entering: *come in, get in, break in, dive in, check in*. Use `into` when you name the destination: *move into an apartment; dive into the pool*. We say *let yourself in* when you enter using a key or code.

## B

`Out` often means leaving: *get out, move out, check out, look out*. Use **out of** with the place named: *get out of the taxi; climb out of the pool*. **Locked out** means unable to enter.

## C

Learn these specific verbs: **fill in/out** a form, **drop in** (make an unplanned visit), **eat out**, **drop out of** a course, **get out of** an arranged duty, **leave out** (omit), **cross out**, **join in**, **take somebody in** (deceive), and **plug in** a device.

## Exercises

### 138.1

Complete with `in`, `into`, `out`, or `out of`.

1. We checked ___ after arriving at the hotel.
2. The cat climbed ___ the box.
3. Do not get ___ the car until it stops.
4. They are moving ___ their new house next month.

### 138.2

2. We had no food, so we decided to ___ .
3. You can ___ the final paragraph; it is not needed.
4. I was completely ___ by his false story.
