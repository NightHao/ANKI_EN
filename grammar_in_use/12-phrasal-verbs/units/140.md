# Unit 140 — Phrasal verbs with `on` and `off`: starting, continuing, and arranging

## A

Study this evening routine:

> Put the kettle **on**, then turn the kitchen light **off** before you leave.

Use **turn/switch on/off** lights and machines. We also say **put music on** and **put the kettle on**. These are separable: *turn the radio off / turn it off*.

In a different use, **go on** means *happen*: “What’s going on outside?” “A lot went
on while we were away.” Unit 141 covers **go on** meaning *continue*.

## B

Use **put off** something or **put off doing** something to delay it: *They put off the vote; do not put off replying.* **Call off** means cancel: *The match was called off.* **Be off to** a place means be leaving for it: *I am off to the airport.*

## C

Use **put on** clothes, glasses, make-up, or a seat belt; **try on** clothes to test fit; **take off** clothes and accessories. **Put on weight** means become heavier.

## D

Use **set off** to start a journey and **take off** for a plane leaving the ground. **Walk/run/drive/ride/go off** mean go away. **See somebody off** means accompany them to say goodbye.

## Exercises

### 140.1

Complete with the correct phrasal verb.

1. It is dark; please ___ the lamp.
2. The organisers had to ___ the outdoor event.
3. We ___ at dawn to avoid traffic.
4. I need to ___ this jacket before buying it.
5. Can you tell me what is ___ outside?

### 140.2

Complete with `on` or `off`.

1. Do not put ___ making the call.
2. The plane took ___ after a two-hour delay.
3. She put ___ three kilos over winter.
4. We saw our friends ___ at the station.
