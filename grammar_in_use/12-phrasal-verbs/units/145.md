# Unit 145 — Phrasal verbs with `away` and `back`

## A

Study this message:

> “I am going away for the weekend, but I will be back on Monday.”

`Away` means away from home, a place, or a person: *The kite blew away.* *She walked away without speaking.* `Back` means return home or to a previous place: *We came back after lunch.* *Put the files back in the drawer.*

Many object verbs are separable: *give the keys back* / *give them back*; *put the files away* / *put them away*. A pronoun goes between verb and particle.

## B

`Keep away from` means do not go near: *Keep away from the wet paint.* `Get away` means escape, while `get away with something` means do something wrong without being caught or punished: *He copied the answer but did not get away with it.*

`Give something away` means give it to somebody because you no longer want it. `Throw something away` means put it in the rubbish. `Put something away` means return it to its usual place.

## C

`Wave/smile/shout/hit back` means respond in the same way: *I waved, and she waved back.* `Call/ring/phone somebody back` means return a call. `Get back to somebody` means reply later: *I will get back to you tomorrow.*

`Pay back` means return borrowed money: *I will pay you back next month.* `Look back on` means think about the past: *Looking back on that move, I learned a lot.*

## Exercises

### 145.1

Complete with the correct phrasal verb.

1. Please ___ the tools ___ when you finish using them.
2. The thief ran through the crowd and ___ .
3. I will ___ you ___ after my meeting. (`return your call`)
4. We should ___ this broken chair ___. (`put in the rubbish`)
5. She borrowed €30 and ___ me ___ the next day.

### 145.2

Choose the best completion.

1. You may borrow my guidebook, but please (`give it back` / `give back it`) tomorrow.
2. He cheated, but he did not (`get away` / `get away with it`).
3. I emailed the supplier, but they have not (`got back to me` / `got me back to`).
4. Correct: *Put away them after the game.*
