# Unit 40 — `if I had known` and `I wish I had known`

## A

Study this regret:

> “If I had known about the exhibition, I would have gone.”

Use `if + past perfect` for an unreal past condition, and `would have + past participle` for its imagined past result. The speaker did not know and did not go. *If the roads had been clear, we would have arrived on time.* Do not put `would have` in the `if` clause.

## B

Use `wish + past perfect` to regret a past event: *I wish I had taken your advice.* / *I wish I had not spent so much.* The past cannot be changed.

## C

The result can refer to the present or the past: *If I had gone to bed earlier, I would be less tired now.* / *If I had gone to the conference, I would have met the editor.*

## D

`Could have` and `might have` can replace `would have` with different meanings: *If we had left sooner, we could have caught the train* (we would have been able to); *we might have caught it* (perhaps we would have).

## Exercises

### 40.1

Complete the sentences.

1. If you ___ (tell) me, I ___ (help) you.
2. I wish I ___ (not / forget) my passport.
3. If the restaurant ___ (not / be) full, we ___ (eat) there.

### 40.2

Choose the correct result.

1. If I had charged my phone, I **(would be / would have been)** able to call you last night.
2. If I had charged my phone, I **(would be / would have been)** less worried now.
3. If we had booked earlier, we **(could have found / might have found)** a room. (ability)

### 40.3

Write a sentence beginning `I wish`.

1. You said something rude yesterday.
2. You did not learn to swim as a child.

40.1 1. had told; would have helped 2. had not forgotten 3. had not been; would have eaten.

40.2 1. would have been 2. would be 3. could have found.

40.3 1. I wish I had not said something rude yesterday. 2. I wish I had learned to swim as a child.
