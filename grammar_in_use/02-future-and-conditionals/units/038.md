# Unit 38 — `if I do` and `if I did`

## A

Study two possible travel plans:

> “If we take the ferry, it will be cheaper.”  
> “We are taking the train. If we took the ferry, it would be cheaper, but slower.”

Use `if + present` for a real future possibility: *If the shop is open, I’ll buy a charger.* We think the condition may happen. Use `if + past` for an imagined or unlikely situation: *If the shop were open at midnight, I’d buy a charger.* The past form expresses distance from reality, not past time.

## B

Do not normally use `would` in the `if` clause: *If I had a car, I would drive there* (not *if I would have*). Put `would/wouldn’t` in the result clause. `Could` and `might` are also possible there: *If I found a cheaper flat, I could move.* / *If I won a prize, I might travel.*

## Exercises

### 38.1

Choose the better form.

1. If it **(snows / snowed)** tonight, we’ll cancel the picnic.
2. If I **(own / owned)** a cottage, I’d spend every weekend there.
3. If you **(see / saw)** Sami tomorrow, will you give him this note?
4. What **(will you do / would you do)** if the lift stopped between floors?

### 38.2

Complete the sentences.

1. If I ___ (have) more energy, I ___ (join) the team.
2. If the alarm ___ (go) off, everyone ___ (leave) the building.
3. If we ___ (not / take) a map, we ___ (get) lost.

### 38.3

Correct the sentence where necessary.

1. If I would win the competition, I would buy a new laptop.
2. If the weather improves, we can eat outside.
3. If I were taller, I could reach that shelf.
