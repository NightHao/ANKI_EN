# Unit 22 — `will` and `shall` 2

## A

Study this conversation:

> “Do you think Rafi will enjoy the new job?”  
> “Yes, I’m sure he’ll settle in quickly.”

Use `will/won’t` for a prediction or what we believe about the future: *The road will be busy tomorrow.* / *I think prices will fall.* / *I don’t think it will snow.* Common words with this use include `probably`, `I’m sure`, `I think`, `I wonder`, and `do you think`.

Do not use `will` merely to describe somebody’s existing arrangement: *Nora is travelling on Friday* if this is already planned; *I think Nora will travel on Friday* is a prediction about what she will decide or do.

## B

After `hope`, we usually use a present form: *I hope the train arrives on time.* /
*I hope it does not rain tomorrow.* A form with `will` is also possible—*I hope it
will rain*—but the present form is the usual teaching pattern here.

## C

`Will` can describe a likely situation now: *Do not call Jun; she’ll be in a meeting.* It means “I expect she is in a meeting now,” not “she will be later.”

## D

`shall` is normally used only with `I` and `we`: *I shall be late* / *We shall probably stay nearby.* In conversation, `I’ll` and `we’ll` are much more usual. The negative is `shall not` or `shan’t`, though `won’t` is also common. Do not normally use `shall` with `he`, `she`, `you`, or `they`.

## Exercises

### 22.1

Complete with `will` or `won’t`.

1. I am sure you ___ enjoy the concert.
2. The forecast is clear, so I do not think it ___ rain.
3. You ___ believe what happened at the market!
4. I wonder where we ___ be this time next year.

### 22.2

Choose the better form.

1. I hope the package **(arrives / will arrive)** tomorrow.
2. Do not phone Mateo now; he **(will be / is going to be)** asleep.
3. Lara has bought her ticket, so she **(is travelling / will travel)** on Saturday.
4. I think Lara **(is travelling / will travel)** if the weather is good.

### 22.3

Correct the sentence where necessary.

1. We shall probably need more chairs.
2. She shall be pleased with the result.
3. I shan’t be at the office tomorrow.
