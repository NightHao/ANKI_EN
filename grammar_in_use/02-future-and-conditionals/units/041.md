# Unit 41 — `wish`

## A

Study these good wishes:

> “Good luck with the interview.”  
> “Thanks. I hope it goes well.”

Use `wish + person + noun` for goodwill: *We wish you every success.* / *I wished her luck before the race.* Do not use this pattern before a full future clause. Say *I hope you enjoy the trip*, not *I wish you enjoy the trip.*

## B

`Wish + past` expresses a present regret: *I wish I knew what to choose.* `Wish + past perfect` expresses a past regret: *I wish I had arrived earlier.* `Wish + could` regrets a present inability: *I wish I could remember his name.* `Wish + could have` regrets a past inability: *I wish I could have joined you.*

## C

Use `I wish ... would ...` when you want something to happen or change: *I wish the rain would stop.* It often expresses a complaint: *I wish somebody would answer the door.* With repeated annoying behaviour, use `wouldn’t`: *I wish you would not leave wet towels on the floor.*

## D

Do not use `wish + would` just to describe a desired state. Say *I wish Nia were here* (a state now), but *I wish Nia would come* (I want her to come).

## Exercises

### 41.1

Choose `wish` or `hope`.

1. I ___ you a safe journey.
2. I ___ you arrive safely.
3. We ___ them all the best in their new home.

### 41.2

Complete the wishes.

1. I cannot reach the top shelf. I wish I ___ (be) taller.
2. I missed the concert because I was ill. I wish I ___ (can / go).
3. The alarm has been ringing for ten minutes. I wish somebody ___ (turn) it off.
4. You keep borrowing my pen without asking. I wish you ___ (not / do) that.

### 41.3

Choose the better form.

1. I wish my friend **(was / would be)** here now.
2. I wish my friend **(came / would come)** to the party.
3. I wish I **(had / would have)** a bicycle.
