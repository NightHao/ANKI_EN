# Unit 20 — `be going to`

## A

Study this situation:

> “What are you going to do with the spare room?”  
> “I’m going to turn it into a study.”

Use `be going to + base verb` for an intention already decided: *I’m going to apply for the course.* / *Are they going to sell their flat?* The plan may not yet be arranged in detail.

## B

Compare an intention with an arranged event:

> “I’m going to clean my bike.” (I have decided.)  
> “I’m taking it to the repair shop at 11.” (an appointment is fixed.)

`I’m doing` often implies a clearer schedule or arrangement, while `I’m going to do` stresses the decision. Sometimes either form is possible. Compare *I do not know what I’m doing tomorrow* (my schedule) with *I do not know what I’m going to do about the leak* (my decision).

## C

Use `going to` for a prediction based on evidence visible or known now: *Look at that overloaded shelf — it’s going to fall.* / *I feel awful; I’m going to be ill.* The present situation makes the future result seem clear.

## D

`was/were going to` describes a past intention that did not happen, or a future event expected from a past viewpoint: *We were going to camp, but the storm changed our plans.* / *I thought the parcel was going to arrive today, but it did not.*

## Exercises

### 20.1

Complete with `be going to`.

1. I have decided to learn Italian. I ___ (join) an evening class.
2. Be careful — you ___ (spill) that paint.
3. What ___ you ___ (wear) to the ceremony?

### 20.2

Choose the better form.

1. We **(are going to visit / are visiting)** the gallery at 2:00; the tickets are booked.
2. I **(am going to contact / am contacting)** the landlord, but I have not chosen a time yet.
3. I do not know what I **(am doing / am going to do)** about this broken laptop.

### 20.3

Rewrite with `was/were going to`.

1. I intended to phone Rosa, but I sent a message instead.
2. We expected the weather to improve, but it stayed cloudy.
