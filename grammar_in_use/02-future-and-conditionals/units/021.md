# Unit 21 — `will` and `shall` 1

## A

Study this moment:

> The doorbell rings while you are cooking. “I’ll get it.”

Use `will` (`I’ll`) for a decision made as you speak: *I forgot to reply. I’ll do it now.* / *I think I’ll order soup.* We do not use present simple for that immediate decision. `I don’t think I’ll go out` and `I won’t stay long` are common ways to state a decision not to do something.

## B

`Will` also expresses an offer, agreement, or promise: *That box looks awkward. I’ll carry it.* / *Sure, I’ll email you the notes.* / *I’ll return the keys tomorrow.* `won’t` can express refusal: *He won’t discuss it.* / *My scooter won’t start.*

`Will you ...?` can be a request: *Will you hold this for a second?*

## C

Do not use `will` for an arrangement made earlier: *I’m working late tomorrow* (not *I’ll work late*), but *I’ll meet you at the entrance at six* can be a decision made in the conversation now.

## D

Use `shall I ...?` to offer or ask permission, and `shall we ...?` to ask for a suggestion: *Shall I close the window?* / *What shall we cook tonight?* Compare *Shall I lock the door?* (do you want me to?) with *Will you lock the door?* (please do it).

## Exercises

### 21.1

Complete with `I’ll` and a suitable verb.

1. “I cannot reach that shelf.” “___ you a chair.” (get)
2. “Did you book the taxi?” “No, I forgot. ___ now.” (book)
3. “Tea or juice?” “___ tea, please.” (have)

### 21.2

Choose the better form.

1. I have a dentist appointment tomorrow, so I **(am leaving / will leave)** work early.
2. “I cannot lift this.” “I **(am helping / will help)** you.”
3. I asked Farah, but she **(doesn’t / won’t)** tell me the password.
4. “___ you pass the salt?” (Will / Shall)

### 21.3

Write a question with `shall`.

1. You want to offer to make some tea. (I / make / tea?)
2. You and a friend need to choose a restaurant. (where / we / eat?)
