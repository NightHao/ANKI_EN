# Unit 24 — `will be doing` and `will have done`

## A

Study this schedule:

> At 3 p.m. tomorrow, the runners will be crossing the finish line.

`will be + verb-ing` is future continuous. It shows an activity in progress at a future time: *This time next month, I’ll be travelling around Norway.* / *Do not call at eight; we’ll be putting the children to bed.* Compare *We’ll be eating at eight* (in the middle of a meal) with *We’ll eat at eight* (the planned action or decision).

## B

Future continuous can also report a future event in a neutral, expected way: *The director will be speaking after the break.* / *Our goalkeeper will not be playing on Sunday.* In this use it is often close in meaning to `will` or `going to`.

## C

`will have + past participle` is future perfect. Use it for something complete before a stated future time: *By the time you arrive, we’ll have packed.* / *The show will already have started at nine.*

It can also measure a period up to a future date: *In October, they will have lived together for ten years.*

## Exercises

### 24.1

Complete with future continuous or future perfect.

1. At noon tomorrow, I ___ (sit) in the dentist’s waiting room.
2. By noon tomorrow, I ___ (finish) the first draft.
3. Do not come at six; we ___ (have) dinner then.
4. By the end of the trip, she ___ (drive) over 2,000 kilometres.

### 24.2

Choose the better form.

1. The host **(will announce / will be announcing)** the winners later in the programme.
2. Let’s wait for Niko, then we **(will eat / will be eating)**.
3. At 4:30, the meeting **(will finish / will have finished)**.

### 24.3

Write a sentence using the prompt.

1. next Friday / this time / we / fly / over the Atlantic
2. by 2032 / I / work / here / twenty years
