# Unit 25 — `when I do`, `when I’ve done`, `if`, and `when`

## A

Study this message:

> “I’ll send you the address when I get home.”

In a future time clause after `when`, use a present form, not `will`: *I’ll call before I leave.* / *Wait here until I come back.* The same rule applies after `while`, `after`, `as soon as`, and `until`: *What will you do while I’m away?*

## B

After `when`, `after`, `until`, and `as soon as`, present perfect can show that the first action must finish before the next one: *When I’ve checked the figures, we can publish them.* / *Do not speak until the manager has left.*

Use present simple when the two actions happen together: *When I speak to Zara, I’ll mention it.* Often either form is possible: *I’ll rest after I finish / I’ve finished this chapter.*

## C

After `if`, use present forms for a possible future condition: *If the shop is open, I’ll buy batteries.* (not *if it will be open*). Use `if` when the event may not happen: *If I see Amir tomorrow, I’ll ask him.* Use `when` when it is expected or certain: *When I see Amir tomorrow, I’ll ask him.*

## Exercises

### 25.1

Complete with the correct form.

1. I’ll unlock the door when I ___ (arrive).
2. We can start as soon as she ___ (finish) the call.
3. If it ___ (rain) tonight, we ___ (stay) in.
4. I’ll return the book after I ___ (read) it.

### 25.2

Choose `if` or `when`.

1. ___ the delivery arrives, I’ll sign for it. (It is definitely due today.)
2. ___ the delivery arrives today, I’ll sign for it. (It may be delayed.)
3. I’ll make coffee ___ you are ready.

### 25.3

Correct the sentence where necessary.

1. I’ll text you as soon as I will know more.
2. If the repair takes too long, we’ll cancel it.
3. When I’ve finished the meeting tomorrow, I’ll send you the notes.
