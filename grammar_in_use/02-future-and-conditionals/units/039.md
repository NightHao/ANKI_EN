# Unit 39 — `if I knew` and `I wish I knew`

## A

Study this problem:

> “I’d message Elio if I knew his address.”

Use `if + past` with `would` to imagine a different present situation: *If I had more free time, I’d learn pottery.* / *If she did not need the car, she would lend it to us.* The speaker knows the condition is not true or does not expect it to be true.

`Wish + past` expresses regret about a present reality: *I wish I knew the answer.* / *I wish I did not have to work tomorrow.* Compare *I am glad I live here* with *I wish I lived here.*

## B

After `if` and `wish`, `were` can replace `was`, especially in *If I were you*: *If I were you, I would apologise.* / *I wish the room were brighter.* `Was` is also common in conversation.

## C

Do not normally use `would` after `if`, or use `would` after `wish` merely to describe a state: *If he were here, he would help*; *I wish he were here* (not *would be here*). `I wish ... would ...` has a different meaning; see Unit 41.

## D

`Could` can mean “would be able to” or “were able to”: *I could work abroad if I could speak Japanese.* / *I wish I could help.*

## Exercises

### 39.1

Complete with the correct form.

1. If I ___ (know) the answer, I ___ (tell) you.
2. I wish I ___ (have) a quieter workspace.
3. If we ___ (be) nearer, we ___ (visit) more often.
4. I wish I ___ (can) play the piano.

### 39.2

Choose the better form.

1. If I **(was / were)** you, I would not accept it.
2. I wish the weather **(would be / were)** warmer.
3. If the museum **(would open / opened)** late, we could go after work.

### 39.3

Write a wish about the situation.

1. You do not understand the instructions.
2. Your neighbour is away, but you want to talk to her.
