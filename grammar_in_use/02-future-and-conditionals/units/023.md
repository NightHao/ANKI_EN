# Unit 23 — `will` and `be going to`

## A

Study these replies:

> “The clinic has been trying to reach you.”  
> “Really? I’ll call them now.”  
> “Yes, I know. I’m going to call them after lunch.”

Use `will` for a new decision made at the moment of speaking. Use `be going to` when the decision already existed before the conversation. *I’ll make some tea* can be a new response; *I’m going to make some tea* tells someone about an earlier plan.

## B

Both forms can express a prediction: *I think the new café will be popular.* / *I think the new café is going to be popular.* For a prediction based on evidence now, use `going to`: *Look at the cracked branch — it’s going to break.* The evidence makes the outcome visible now.

Compare *Maya will miss the bus; she is always late* (my knowledge of her habits) with *Maya is going to miss the bus; it leaves in two minutes and she is still at home* (the present facts show it).

## Exercises

### 23.1

Complete with `will` or `be going to`.

1. “I cannot open this file.” “I ___ (show) you how.”
2. I have decided to repaint the kitchen. I ___ (choose) a pale colour.
3. Watch out! That glass ___ (fall).
4. I think the concert ___ (be) excellent.

### 23.2

Choose the better reply.

1. “Why are you carrying those boxes?” “I **(will move / am going to move)** them upstairs.”
2. “We have no bread.” “I **(will buy / am going to buy)** some on my way home.”
3. “I forgot to bring a pen.” “I **(will lend / am going to lend)** you mine.”

### 23.3

Write one prediction based on the prompt.

1. The pavement is icy; a cyclist is turning quickly. (fall)
2. Your colleague regularly misses deadlines. (miss / this one too)
