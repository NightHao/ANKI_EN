# Unit 111 — `still`, `any more`, `yet`, and `already`

## A

Study this update:

> “Does the old bakery still open at six?” “No, it does not open that early **any more**.”

Use **still** for a continuing situation or action: *I still need your address; Is it still snowing?* It can also mean “despite this”: *The task was difficult, but we still finished on time.*

Use **not ... any more/any longer** for a changed situation. These usually go at the end: *They do not live here any more.* **No longer** has the same meaning, but goes in the middle: *They no longer live here.* Do not use *no more* in this meaning.

## B

Use **already** when something happens before expected or before now:

> “Should I print the tickets?” “No, I have already downloaded them.”

It usually goes in the middle, but can come at the end: *The meeting has started already.* It is also possible with a present situation: *It is only noon and I am already tired.*

## C

Use **yet** mostly in questions and negatives for something expected by now: *Have you chosen a date yet? We have not received a reply yet.* It normally goes at the end. `Yet` is especially common with the present perfect, but it is not limited to it: *Is dinner ready yet?*

## D

Compare **still** and **yet**:

> Is the printer still broken?  
> Has the printer been fixed yet?

Both ask about an expected change. In negatives, **still not** often sounds more impatient or surprised: *I sent the files two weeks ago, and they still have not replied.* *They have not replied yet* is more neutral.

## Exercises

### 111.1

Complete with `still`, `any more`, or `no longer`.

1. I ___ use that password; I changed it yesterday.
2. The clinic ___ accepts walk-in patients.
3. We are ___ waiting for the final decision.

### 111.2

Complete with `already` or `yet`.

1. Have you checked the address ___?
2. The guests have ___ arrived, so we can begin.
3. It is ___ dark outside, and it is only five o’clock.

### 111.3

Choose the more natural sentence for the situation.

1. You expected a reply last week and are annoyed. (They have not replied yet. / They still have not replied.)
2. You simply want an update about an expected repair. (Is it still broken? / Has it been repaired yet?)

<summary>Answers</summary>

**111.1** 1. no longer (or *do not ... any more*)  2. no longer  3. still.  
**111.2** 1. yet  2. already  3. already.  
**111.3** 1. They still have not replied. 2. Has it been repaired yet?
