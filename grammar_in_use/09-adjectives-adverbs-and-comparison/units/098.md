# Unit 98 — Adjectives ending in `-ing` and `-ed`

## A

Study this situation:

> Lina watched a documentary about deep-sea animals. “It was amazing,” she said. “I was amazed by the photographs.”

We use **-ing** adjectives for the person, thing, or situation that produces a feeling: *an amazing documentary; a tiring journey; confusing instructions*. We use **-ed** adjectives for the person who has the feeling: *amazed viewers; tired passengers; confused users*.

> The lecture was boring, so several students were bored.
>
> The news was worrying. My parents were worried.

## B

The difference is about meaning, not time. A film can be *interesting* before you watch it; you become *interested* when it catches your attention. Compare:

| Cause or quality | Person’s reaction |
|---|---|
| The job is satisfying. | I am satisfied with the job. |
| The noise is annoying. | We are annoyed by the noise. |

We say *I am interested in photography* (not *I am interesting in photography*) when photography attracts you. *I am interesting* means other people may find you interesting.

## C

Some **-ed** adjectives describe a lasting state, so they can follow *feel, look,* and *seem*:

> You look exhausted. Did you sleep badly?
>
> I felt relieved after the interview.

Use **-ing** when you describe the experience itself: *an exhausting week; a relieving result* is unusual—normally say *a result that was a relief*.

## Exercises

### 98.1

Choose the correct word.

1. The directions were confusing / confused.
2. I was surprising / surprised by the price.
3. It is an exhausting / exhausted commute.

### 98.2

Complete the reply: “The workshop was really ___, but I was too ___ to stay for the final discussion.” (*interesting / tired*)

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**98.1** 1. *confusing*; the directions cause confusion. 2. *surprised*; the speaker has the feeling. 3. *exhausting*; the commute causes exhaustion.

**98.2** *interesting; tired.* The workshop has the quality; the speaker experiences tiredness.
