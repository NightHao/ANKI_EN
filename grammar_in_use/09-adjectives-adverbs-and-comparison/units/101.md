# Unit 101 — Adjectives and adverbs 2

## A

**Good** is an adjective; its usual adverb is **well**:

- “Your pronunciation is good.”
- “You pronounce the names well.”
- “Eva is a good pianist.” / “Eva plays the piano well.”

We also use **well** before some past participles: “a well-known actor”, “a
well-designed app”, “well-paid work”. **Well** can be an adjective meaning *in good
health*: “I wasn’t well yesterday, but I’m fine now.”

## B

**Fast, hard** and **late** have the same form as adjectives and adverbs:

- “It’s a fast train.” / “The train travels fast.”
- “This is hard work.” / “They worked hard.”
- “The flight was late.” / “We arrived late.”

**Lately** means *recently*: “Have you spoken to Jun lately?”

## C

**Hardly** does not mean *with effort*. It means *very little* or *almost not*:

- “We hardly know each other.” (= we know each other very little)
- “I can hardly hear you.” (= hearing you is almost impossible)
- “She tried hard, but I hardly tried.”

**Hardly** normally comes before the main verb, but after an auxiliary: “He hardly
spoke”; “I could hardly walk.”

## D

We can use **hardly** with **any, anybody, anyone, anything** and **anywhere**:

- “There’s hardly any milk left.”
- “Hardly anyone answered the survey.”
- “She said hardly anything.”

**Hardly ever** means *almost never*: “I hardly ever watch live television.”
Sometimes **hardly** means *certainly not*: “It’s hardly surprising that he’s tired;
he worked all night.”

## Exercises

### 101.1

Choose the correct word.

1. Mei speaks English **good / well**.
2. We worked **hard / hardly** to meet the deadline.
3. I have not seen him **late / lately**.
4. I could **hard / hardly** read the sign.

### 101.2

Complete with **hardly any, hardly anyone** or **hardly ever**.

1. The hall was almost empty. ___ came.
2. We have almost no time. We have ___ time.
3. She almost never drives. She ___ drives.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**101.1** 1. well 2. hard 3. lately 4. hardly.

**101.2** 1. Hardly anyone 2. hardly any 3. hardly ever.
