# Unit 107 — `as ... as` and comparative clauses

## A

Study this situation:

> Leo can carry the box, but he cannot carry it **as easily as** Nia can. Nia is stronger.

Use **as + adjective/adverb + as** to say that two things are equal: *The train is as fast as driving; Please reply as soon as you can.* In positive sentences and questions, use **as**, not *so*. We can also say *as much as, as many as,* and *as long as*: *Take as many copies as you need.*

## B

Use **not as ... as** when the first thing has less of a quality. **Not so ... as** is also possible, especially in conversation.

> This year is not as busy as last year.  
> I did not sleep as well as I hoped.

**Less than** can express a similar idea: *The room was less bright than it looked online.* It is often useful with quantities: *We spent less than our budget.*

## C

For multiples, use **twice/three times etc. + as ... as**: *The new battery lasts twice as long as the old one.* We can also say *three times the size of*, but not *three times as big than*.

Use **the same as** for identical things or features: *My jacket is the same colour as yours.* Do not use *the same like*.

## D

After **than** and the second **as**, everyday English allows an object pronoun: *She is taller than me; I cannot type as quickly as him.* A fuller verb phrase is also correct: *She is taller than I am; I cannot type as quickly as he can.* Avoid a bare subject form such as *taller than I* in ordinary conversation.

## Exercises

### 107.1

Complete with `as` or `not as`.

1. The walk was ___ difficult ___ we expected.
2. You may stay ___ long ___ you need.
3. I do not know ___ many people here ___ Rina does.

### 107.2

Rewrite using the word in brackets.

1. The hall was less full than usual. (`as`)
2. My desk and yours have identical widths. (`same`)
3. The latest model costs double the old model. (`twice`)

### 107.3

Choose the natural ending.

1. Amir is more patient than (me / I).
2. The children ran as quickly as (we could / us could).

**107.1** 1. not as, as  2. as, as  3. as, as.  
**107.2** 1. The hall was not as full as usual. 2. My desk is the same width as yours. 3. The latest model costs twice as much as the old one.  
**107.3** 1. me (or *I am*)  2. we could.
