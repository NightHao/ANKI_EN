# Unit 106 — Modifying comparatives

## A

Study this review:

> The new interface is **much easier** to navigate, but the subscription is **a little more expensive**.

Put degree words before a comparative: **much, a lot, far** for a large difference; **a bit, a little, slightly** for a small one. We say *far more useful*, *a lot better*, and *slightly quieter* (not *very better*).

## B

Use **any** or **no + comparative** to stress that there is no extra amount of change.

> Is the headache any better?  
> I cannot stay any longer.  
> This screen is no wider than mine.

*No wider than* and *not any wider than* have nearly the same meaning here: not even a little wider. We can also say *The cheaper plan is no less secure.*

## C

Repeat a comparative with **and** for continuous change: *The evenings are getting darker and darker.* With longer words, repeat **more and more + adjective/adverb**: *The instructions became more and more confusing.* We also use *more and more + noun*: *More and more residents cycle to work.*

## D

Use **the + comparative, the + comparative** when one change is connected to another.

> The earlier we leave, the less traffic we will meet.  
> The more carefully you plan, the easier the move becomes.

For a preference, use **the + comparative, the better**: *The fewer meetings, the better.*

## E

The normal comparative of *old* is **older**: *My cousin is older than I am.* **Elder** is used before a family noun, especially *elder brother/sister/son/daughter*: *her elder sister*. Do not say *My sister is elder than me*.

## Exercises

### 106.1

Complete with a suitable modifier.

1. This keyboard is ___ quieter than the last one. (small difference)
2. The final version is ___ more reliable than the draft. (large difference)
3. Could you drive ___ more slowly?

### 106.2

Complete with `any` or `no`.

1. I cannot wait ___ longer.
2. Is the second route ___ shorter than the first?
3. Their garden is ___ bigger than ours.

### 106.3

Rewrite using the pattern in brackets.

1. As we waited, we became increasingly impatient. (`more and more`)
2. If you pack less, travelling is easier. (`the ... the ...`)
3. My sister is older than I am. Use `elder` correctly.
