# Unit 103 — `enough` and `too`

## A

Study this situation:

> Mina wants to cycle to work, but her bike is not safe **enough**. It is **too** old and the brakes are weak.

Put **enough** after an adjective or adverb: *safe enough, early enough, clearly enough* (not *enough safe*). Put it before a noun: *enough batteries, enough patience*. It can stand alone when the noun is understood: “Do we need more food?” “No, we have enough.”

## B

**Too + adjective/adverb** means more than is acceptable or useful: *The screen is too bright to use at night.* Compare *not + adjective/adverb + enough*, meaning less than necessary:

> The screen is too bright.  
> The text is not large enough.

With nouns, use **too much** for uncountable nouns and **too many** for plurals: *too much salt; too many tabs; not enough space*.

## C

Use **enough/too … for + person or thing** when you name who or what is affected: *Is this table large enough for six people?* Use **enough/too … to + verb** for an action: *She was experienced enough to lead the meeting.*

> The password is too short for the system.  
> The password is too short to be secure.

## D

After **too + adjective + to**, do not repeat the object: *The soup is too hot to eat* (not *to eat it*). The same applies to *enough*: *This shelf is strong enough to stand on*, not *to stand on it*.

## Exercises

### 103.1

Complete with `enough` in the correct position.

1. The room is not ___ for two desks. (large)
2. Do we have ___ cups for everyone? 
3. Please speak ___ for the people at the back. (loudly)

### 103.2

Choose `too`, `not ... enough`, `too much`, or `too many`.

1. This suitcase is ___ heavy for me to lift.
2. There are ___ notifications on my phone; I cannot focus.
3. The print is ___ big to read without glasses.

### 103.3

Rewrite as one sentence using `too` or `enough` and `to`.

1. The gate was very low. The delivery truck could not pass under it.
2. Omar is sixteen. He can apply for the course.
