# Unit 100 — Adjectives and adverbs 1

## A

Study these sentences:

> “The traffic was slow, and every driver was moving slowly.”

**Slow** is an adjective; it describes the traffic. **Slowly** is an adverb; it
describes how the drivers were moving. Many adverbs are formed with **adjective +
-ly**: **careful → carefully, serious → seriously, heavy → heavily, terrible →
terribly**.

Not every word ending in **-ly** is an adverb. **Friendly, lively, elderly, lonely**
and **lovely** are adjectives: “We had a lovely evening.”

## B

Adjectives describe nouns. Adverbs describe verbs:

- “Mara is a careful driver.” / “Mara drives carefully.”
- “There was heavy rain.” / “It was raining heavily.”
- “He speaks fluent Spanish.” / “He speaks Spanish fluently.”

We say “a quick reply” (not “a quickly reply”) because **reply** is a noun. We say
“She replied quickly” because **quickly** describes the verb.

## C

Use an adjective after **be** and after linking verbs such as **look, feel, sound,
smell** and **taste**:

- “Please be quiet.”
- “You look serious.”
- “I feel happy.”
- “Dinner smells good.”

Compare “She looked nervous” (her appearance) with “She looked nervously at the
clock” (the manner of looking).

An adverb can also modify an adjective or another adverb: “reasonably cheap”,
“extremely useful”, “surprisingly easy”, “incredibly quickly”.

## D

We use adverbs before past participles when they describe degree or manner:

- “Two passengers were seriously injured.”
- “The event was badly organised.”
- “It is a beautifully written novel.”

## Exercises

### 100.1

Choose the correct word.

1. Please speak **quiet / quietly**.
2. The children were **quiet / quietly**.
3. It was raining **heavy / heavily**.
4. This fabric feels **soft / softly**.

### 100.2

Complete with an adjective or adverb from the word in brackets.

1. The exam was surprisingly ___. (easy)
2. She explained the process very ___. (clear)
3. It is a ___ designed website. (careful)
