# Unit 110 — Adverb position with verbs

## A

Study this conversation:

> “Do you take the early train?” “I **usually take** it, but today I walked.”

With a one-word main verb, put adverbs such as **always, usually, often, never, also, already,** and **hardly ever** before it: *I rarely cook; We also need a receipt.* We say *I always have to leave early*, not *I have always to leave early*.

## B

After **be**, put these adverbs after the verb: *The shop is usually open; They were both nervous.* With two or more verb words, put the adverb after the first one: *She has already called; We can probably help; I have never seen it.*

**Probably** normally comes before a negative: *The parcel probably will not arrive today* or, more naturally, *The parcel probably won’t arrive today*.

## C

**All** and **both** also take a middle position: *We all agreed; My cousins are both doctors; They have all left.* This position makes clear who or how many the statement concerns.

## D

Sometimes we use *is, do, will,* or another auxiliary to avoid repeating words: “Does she like it?” “I think she does.” Put frequency adverbs before that short verb:

> He says he will call, but he never does.  
> I have not tried it, and I never will.

## Exercises

### 110.1

Put the adverb in the correct place.

1. I check my calendar before breakfast. (`usually`)
2. The seats are available. (`still`)
3. We have met their manager. (`already`)

### 110.2

Complete the sentences with the words in brackets.

1. My parents ___ work from home. (`both / can`)
2. The train ___ be delayed. (`probably / will`)
3. I ___ remember where I left it. (`never / can`)

### 110.3

Correct the adverb position.

1. I have often to explain this rule.
2. She says she will help, but she does rarely.
3. We all have arrived before noon.
