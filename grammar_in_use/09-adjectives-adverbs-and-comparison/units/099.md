# Unit 99 — Adjectives: a nice new house, you look tired

## A

We sometimes put two or more adjectives before a noun:

- “They rented a comfortable new flat.”
- “The café has a beautiful large wooden counter.”

Adjectives such as **comfortable** and **beautiful** express an opinion. Adjectives
such as **new, large** and **wooden** give facts. An opinion adjective normally comes
before a fact adjective: “an interesting old building”, “delicious hot soup”.

## B

When several fact adjectives occur together, this is the usual order:

| size | age | colour | origin | material | noun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| small | old | black | Japanese | metal | box |

You will rarely need every position. Natural combinations include “a tall young man”,
“an old Turkish song” and “a small blue plastic case”. Size or length normally comes
before shape or width: “a large square room”, “a long narrow path”.

Use **and** between two colours: “a blue and white shirt”. We do not normally use
**and** between other adjectives before a noun: “a long black coat” (not “a long and
black coat”).

## C

Adjectives also follow **be, get, become** and **seem**:

- “I’m tired, and I’m getting hungry.”
- “The explanation became clearer.”
- “Your new colleague seems friendly.”

They can describe how something **looks, feels, sounds, tastes** or **smells**: “You
look tired”; “The idea sounds sensible”; “This tea tastes strange”. Use an adverb for
the way somebody performs an action: “Drive carefully” (not “Drive careful”).

## D

We say **the first two**, **the next few** and **the last ten**, with the sequence word
before the number or quantity:

- “I missed the first two classes.”
- “We’ll be busy for the next few weeks.”
- “Nothing happened during the last ten minutes.”

## Exercises

### 99.1

Put the adjectives in a natural order.

1. a / leather / beautiful / Italian / bag
2. a / round / large / table
3. an / cotton / old / white / shirt

### 99.2

Choose the natural form.

1. The plan sounds **realistic / realistically**.
2. Please explain it **clear / clearly**.
3. She bought a **red and green / red green** scarf.

### 99.3

Correct: “I didn’t understand the two first chapters.”
