# Unit 109 — Word order: verb, object, place, and time

## A

Study this report:

> We delivered the samples to the laboratory this morning.

The usual order is **verb + object + place + time**. Keep a verb next to its object: *I read the message on the train* (not *I read on the train the message*). The same rule applies when an adverb describes the action: *The guide explained the map clearly*, not *explained clearly the map*.

> I lost my keys near the entrance.  
> Did you buy anything at the market?

## B

After the verb (and its object), place usually comes before time: *They stayed in Seoul for a week; Please put the glasses on the shelf.* We say *go home* without *to*.

> I met a former colleague at the conference yesterday.  
> The children walk to school every weekday.

Put a time expression first when it sets the scene or is the main link to the previous sentence: *After lunch, we checked the results.* This changes emphasis, not the basic order.

Some adverbs such as *always, usually,* and *never* go in the middle of the sentence, not at the end; see Unit 110.

## Exercises

### 109.1

Put the words in the usual order.

1. yesterday / at the library / I found / an old photograph
2. to the airport / our neighbour / drove / us / early this morning
3. quickly / the technician / the problem / solved

### 109.2

Correct the word order where necessary.
