# Unit 123 — `in`, `at`, and `on` for position: basic uses

## A

Study this scene:

> There is a notebook **in** the drawer and a label **on** the drawer.

Use **in** for an enclosed space or area: *in a box, in the kitchen, in a town, in the sea*. We also say *in your hand* and *in your mouth*. Think of something inside a space or area.

## B

Use **at** for a point, stop, entrance, or position: *at the traffic lights; at the door; at reception; at the next stop*. Compare *There are people in the bookstore* (inside it) with *Turn left at the bookstore* (a point on the route).

> Meet me in the lobby, not at the entrance.  
> Leave your pass at reception.

## C

Use **on** for a surface, attached mark, page, or island: *on the floor; on the ceiling; on your sleeve; on page twelve; on an island*. Compare *water in a bottle* with *a sticker on the bottle*.

`At` and `on` also contrast: *Someone is at the door* means near it; *a notice is on the door* means attached to it.

## Exercises

### 123.1

Complete with `in`, `at`, or `on`.

1. There is a crack ___ the screen.
2. I will wait for you ___ the corner by the café.
3. They were swimming ___ the lake.
4. Turn right ___ the roundabout.

### 123.2

Choose the natural phrase.

1. The keys are (in my pocket / on my pocket).
2. We meet (in the hotel / at the entrance to the hotel).
3. The timetable is (at page six / on page six).

### 123.3

Complete the contrast.

1. There is someone ___ the window, but there is a fingerprint ___ the window.
2. There are books ___ the table, but the remote is ___ the table.
