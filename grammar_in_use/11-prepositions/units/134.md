# Unit 134 — Verb + preposition: `about` and `of`

## A

Study these questions:

> Did you hear **about** the power cut? Have you heard **of** that author?

**Hear about** means receive news about an event. **Hear of** means know that a person or thing exists. **Hear from** means receive communication from someone: *I heard from my cousin yesterday.*

## B

Use **think about** when considering something carefully: *I will think about your offer.* Use **think of** to produce an idea or ask/give an opinion: *Can you think of a name? What did you think of the film?* For future possibilities, both can work: *She is thinking of/about moving.*

## C

Use **dream about** for dreams during sleep and **dream of/about** for hopes or imagined situations. **Would not dream of** means “would never consider”: *I would not dream of sharing it.*

## D

Use **complain to somebody about** something unsatisfactory, but **complain of** a pain or illness. **Remind somebody about** an obligation; **remind somebody of** something that causes a memory.

## Exercises

### 134.1

Complete with `about`, `of`, or `from`.

1. Have you heard ___ the traffic accident?
2. I have never heard ___ that company.
3. It was good to hear ___ you.

### 134.2

Complete with `think about` or `think of`.

1. I need a few days to ___ the proposal.
2. Can you ___ a reason to refuse?
3. What did you ___ the exhibition?

### 134.3

Choose the correct preposition.

1. She complained (about / of) a severe headache.
2. Please remind me (about / of) renewing my passport.
3. This song reminds me (about / of) school.
