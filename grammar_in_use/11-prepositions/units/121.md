# Unit 121 — `at`, `on`, and `in` for time

## A

Study these diary notes:

> The call is **at** 3.15, **on** Tuesday, **in** October.

Use **at** for a clock time and some fixed points: *at midnight, at lunchtime, at sunset*. Use **on** for days and dates: *on Friday; on 9 March; on my birthday*. Use **in** for longer periods: *in June; in 2030; in winter; in the 1990s*.

## B

Learn these common expressions: **at the moment/present**, **at the same time**, **at weekends**, and **at Christmas** (but *on Christmas Day*). We say **at night** for nights in general, but **in the night** for a particular night: *I dislike driving at night; I heard a noise in the night.*

## C

Use **in the morning/afternoon/evening**, but **on** with a specific day: *in the morning; on Thursday morning; on Sunday evenings*. Do not use `at/on/in` before **last, next, this,** or **every**: *next Monday; last winter; every weekend*. `On` is often optional before a day: *I will see you (on) Friday.*

## D

**In + period from now** means “after that amount of time”: *The technician will arrive in ten minutes.* `In` can also say how long an activity took: *She learned to swim in six weeks.*

Complete with `at`, `on`, or `in`.

1. The market closes ___ 6 p.m.
