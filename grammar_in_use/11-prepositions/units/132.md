# Unit 132 — Verb + preposition: `to` and `at`

## A

Study this call:

> I spoke **to** the technician and asked her to explain the error **to** me.

Use **talk/speak/listen to** somebody or something. But say **ask somebody** a question, **phone/call/email/text somebody**, and **thank somebody** (no `to`). Use **apologise to somebody for** something.

Use **explain something to somebody** and **describe (to somebody) what/how/why ...**: *Could you explain the rule to us?* Do not say *explain us the rule*.

## B

Use **look/stare/glance at**, **laugh at**, **aim/point at**, and **shoot/fire at**. Say **answer** a person, question, or email, but **reply to** an email or letter.

## C

Some verbs change meaning with `at` or `to`:

> She shouted **at** him. (angrily)  
> She shouted **to** him. (so he could hear)

Throw something **at** a target when you intend to hit it; throw it **to** someone when they should catch it.

## Exercises

### 132.1

Complete with `to`, `at`, `for`, or no preposition.

1. Please listen ___ this message carefully.
2. I thanked Maya ___ her advice.
3. Can you explain the delay ___ me?
4. Nobody answered ___ my question.

### 132.2

Choose the natural phrase.

1. He was angry and shouted (at / to) the waiter.
2. I called (the doctor / to the doctor) this morning.
3. Do not point that laser (at / to) anyone.

### 132.3

Complete with `at` or `to`.

1. The child threw the ball ___ her brother, who caught it.
2. Someone threw paint ___ the wall.
3. I glanced ___ the clock.
