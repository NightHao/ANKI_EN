# Unit 122 — `on time`, `in time`, `at the end`, and `in the end`

## A

Study this travel update:

> The ferry left **on time**, and we reached the terminal **in time** for the last bus.

**On time** means punctual: at the planned time, not late. *The class began on time.* **In time (for something / to do something)** means soon enough: *We reached the gate in time for boarding.* The opposite is **too late**. **Just in time** means almost too late: *I caught the glass just in time.*

## B

Use **at the end of + thing/period** for the point when it finishes: *at the end of the meeting; at the end of May; at the end of the film*. The opposite is **at the beginning of**. Do not say *in the end of May*.

> At the end of the workshop, everyone exchanged contacts.

## C

Use **in the end** for a final result after a process, problem, or decision: *We considered several flats and, in the end, chose the smaller one.* It often means “finally”. It is not used for the final point of an event.

> At first, the software seemed confusing. In the end, it became easy to use.

## Exercises

### 122.1

Complete with `on time` or `in time`.

1. The lecture started exactly at two: it started ___.
2. I ran to the pharmacy and got there ___ before it closed.
3. Please be ___ for the interview.

### 122.2

Complete with `at the end` or `in the end`.

1. ___ of the race, the runners thanked the volunteers.
2. We could not repair the washing machine, so we replaced it ___.
3. ___ of the week, I will send the report.

### 122.3

Correct the phrase.

1. In the end of the course, we receive a certificate.
2. At the end, we decided not to travel.
