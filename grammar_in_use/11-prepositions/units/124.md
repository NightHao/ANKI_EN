# Unit 124 — `in`, `at`, and `on` for position: conventional locations

## A

Study a theatre plan:

> Our seats are **in** the front row, but the exit is **at** the back of the building.

Use **in** with lines, rows, queues, pictures, publications, departments, and broad areas: *in a queue; in a photo; in a magazine; in the sales department; in the sky; in the country*. These are conventional expressions rather than literal containers.

## B

Use **on** for sides, floors, maps, menus, lists, websites, roads, rivers, and coasts: *on the left; on the third floor; on a map; on the menu; on the coast*. We also say **on the way**: *I bought bread on the way home.*

## C

Use **at the top/bottom/end (of ...)**: *Sign at the bottom of the page; a café at the end of the street.* This is different from `on the top of` for a physical surface.

## D

For vehicles, say **in the front/back of a car**, but **at the front/back of** a building, theatre, or group. Use **on the front/back of** paper or an envelope. Say **in the corner of a room**, but **at/on the corner of a street**.

## Exercises

### 124.1

Complete with `in`, `at`, or `on`.

1. Your name is not ___ the list.
2. The apartment is ___ the fourth floor.
3. We stopped for coffee ___ the way to the airport.
4. Write the date ___ the top of the form.

### 124.2

Choose the natural phrase.

1. The children are sitting (in the back of the car / at the back of the car).
2. The emergency exit is (in the back of the theatre / at the back of the theatre).
3. I wrote the address (on the back of the envelope / at the back of the envelope).

### 124.3

Complete the location.

1. There is a lamp ___ the corner of the room.
2. The bakery is ___ the corner of Oak Street.
3. She is standing ___ a queue.
