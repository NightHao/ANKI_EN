# Unit 126 — `to`, `at`, `in`, and `into`

## A

Study this journey:

> We travelled **to** the airport, then waited **at** the airport before flying **to** Canada.

Use **to** for movement towards a place or event: *go to work; return to Spain; come to my house; a visit to the museum*. Compare movement with position: *They are going to Chile, but they live in Chile.* We say **have been to** for experience: *I have been to that gallery twice.*

## B

Say **get to** a place. With `arrive`, use **arrive in** for a city or country and **arrive at** for a building, event, or airport: *arrive in Rome; arrive at the hotel*. Do not say *arrive to*. With **home**, use no preposition: *go home; get home; arrive home; on the way home*.

## C

Use **into** for movement from outside to inside: *walk into the office; pour juice into a glass*. With *go, get,* and *put*, `in` is often possible: *get in/into the car*. The opposite is **out of**.

## D

For buses, trains, and planes, usually say **get on** and **get off**: *get on the bus; get off at Central Station*. Do not normally use `into` for these forms of transport.

## Exercises

### 126.1

Complete with `to`, `at`, `in`, `into`, or no preposition.

1. We got ___ home after midnight.
2. The visitors arrived ___ Dublin yesterday.
3. Please put the receipt ___ your bag.
4. What time did you arrive ___ the conference?

### 126.2

Choose the natural phrase.

1. I have never been (to / in) Mexico.
2. We are driving (to / at) the coast.
3. I met him on my way (home / to home).

### 126.3

Complete with a form of `get`.

1. The taxi stopped, so we ___ the car.
2. At the next stop, please ___ the train.
3. The doors opened and everyone ___ the plane.
