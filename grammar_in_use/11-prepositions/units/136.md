# Unit 136 — Verb + preposition: `in`, `into`, `with`, `to`, and `on`

## A

Study these beliefs:

> I believe **in** sharing useful information, but I do not believe that rumour.

**Believe in** means believe something exists or is right; **believe somebody/something** means accept it as true. Other `in` patterns include **specialise in**, **succeed in**, and **concentrate on**.

## B

Use **break into + building** for entering illegally: *Someone broke into the office
overnight.* Use **crash/drive/run into** for a collision. Other patterns include
**divide/cut/split into** and **translate from one language into another**. `Into`
can therefore show entry, collision, or division into parts.

## C

Use **collide with** and **fill somebody/something with**: *The van collided with a barrier; fill the bottle with water.*

## D

Use **happen to**, **provide/supply somebody with**, **prefer one thing to another**, and **invite somebody to** an event. *What happened to your old laptop?*

## E

Use **spend money on** and **insist on + `-ing`**: *He insisted on paying; we spend little on transport.*

## Exercises

### 136.1

Complete with the correct preposition.

1. I do not believe ___ ghosts.
2. The company specialises ___ medical software.
3. The car crashed ___ a tree.
4. The book was translated ___ Korean.
5. Thieves broke ___ the warehouse.

### 136.2

Complete the paired phrase.

1. Divide the class ___ groups; fill each bottle ___ water.
2. Provide each guest ___ a name tag; invite them ___ lunch.
3. Prefer tea ___ coffee; spend money ___ food.

### 136.3

Correct the preposition.

1. The team succeeded on finding a solution.
2. She insisted to paying.
3. A cyclist collided to the car.
