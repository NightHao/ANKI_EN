# Unit 128 — Uses of `by`

## A

Study this explanation:

> I took your folder **by mistake**, not **on purpose**.

Use **by mistake, by accident,** and **by chance** for unplanned events. These use `by` without `a/the`. In contrast, **on purpose** means deliberately.

## B

Use **by** for a general method of travel: *by bus, by car, by train, by bike*. But use **in** with a particular car or taxi and **on** with a particular bus, train, plane, bike, or ship: *in my car; on the 8.20 train*. Say **on foot**, not usually *by foot*.

`By` also shows method: *send it by post; pay by card; wash it by hand*. Say *pay cash* or *pay in cash*, not normally *by cash*.

## C

In passive sentences, **by** names the agent: *The mural was painted by a local artist.* **With** names the tool: *The door was opened with a key.* We also say *a novel by an author*.

## D

`By` can mean beside: *Sit by the window.* It can show an amount of change or a margin: *Prices rose by 6%; the team won by two points.*

## Exercises

### 128.1

Complete with `by`, `in`, or `on`.

1. We travelled ___ bus but returned ___ a taxi.
2. I left my coat ___ the train.
3. She came ___ foot.

### 128.2

Choose `by` or `with`.

1. The portrait was painted ___ my uncle.
2. The parcel was opened ___ scissors.
