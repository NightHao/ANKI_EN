# Chapter 11 — Prepositions

## Use it when

You are talking about time, place, movement, relationships, or everyday actions. These small words cause many errors because English often chooses a different pattern from your first language. Aim for reliable high-frequency phrases, not a giant list.

## Core contrast

For time, **at** marks a point (*at 6, at night*), **on** marks a day or date (*on Monday, on my birthday*), and **in** marks a longer period (*in July, in 2026, in the morning*). Say *at the weekend* in British English; *on the weekend* is common in American English. Pick the variety you hear most, but understand both.

For place, **at** often means a point or activity (*at the station; at work*), **in** means inside an area (*in a room; in Taipei*), and **on** means touching a surface or part of a route (*on the table; on the bus*). Movement usually takes **to**: *go to school*. But say *go home, arrive home,* and *come home*—no *to*.

Learn verb + preposition as one unit: *depend on, worry about, listen to, apply for, pay for, belong to.* The preposition is not decoration; changing it changes the phrase. Learn adjective and noun patterns in the same way: *interested in, responsible for, a reason for, an increase in*.

## Natural examples

> I usually work best in the morning, so I try to deal with difficult tasks before lunch.

> We met at the entrance and walked through the park to the museum.

> I’m looking for a quieter flat, but it depends on what I can afford.

> She apologised to the team for arriving late.

> There has been a noticeable increase in rent this year.

## Common failure

**Wrong:** *I discussed about the problem with her.*  
**Better:** *I discussed the problem with her.* / *I talked about the problem with her.*

Some verbs take no following preposition: *discuss, enter, reach, mention, approach.* Check the whole phrase rather than translating word by word.

**Wrong:** *I’m interested on photography.*  
**Better:** *I’m interested in photography.*

When you notice an error, save the useful chunk: *be interested in + noun / -ing*, not merely “in, not on.”

## Say it yourself

Describe how you get ready for an important day. Use:

- three time or place phrases: *at, on, in*;
- two verb + preposition chunks;
- one adjective + preposition chunk.

Then describe one change in your city using a noun + preposition combination such as
*an increase in*, *a demand for*, or *the effect on*.
