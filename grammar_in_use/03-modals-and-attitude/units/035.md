# Unit 35 — `had better` and `it’s time`

## A

Study this warning:

> “The battery is at two percent.”  
> “We’d better save the file now.”

`Had better + base verb` gives advice in a particular situation where a problem may follow: *You’d better leave soon or you will miss the last train.* / *We’d better stop for fuel.* The negative is `had better not`: *You’d better not mention the surprise yet.*

Although `had` looks past, `had better` refers to the present or future. Say *You’d better call* (not *better to call*).

## B

`Had better` and `should` are not identical. Both work for immediate advice: *It is late; you’d better go* / *you should go.* But `had better` is for a specific risk or situation; for general advice use `should`: *You should stretch more often* (not normally *you’d better stretch more often*).

## C

Use `It’s time (for someone) to + verb`: *It’s time for us to leave.* We also say `It’s time + subject + past form`: *It’s time we left.* The past form has a present meaning: the action should happen now.

This pattern often criticises delay: *It’s time he took responsibility.* / *It’s about time they fixed the lift.*

## Exercises

### 35.1

Complete with `’d better` or `’d better not`.

1. The road is icy. You ___ drive too fast.
2. The restaurant is popular. We ___ reserve a table.
3. You have an early flight. You ___ set an alarm.

### 35.2

Choose `had better` or `should`.

1. You ___ take an umbrella; the storm is approaching.
2. People ___ drink enough water every day.
3. We missed the final bus. We ___ get a taxi.

### 35.3

Complete with the correct past form.

1. It is midnight. It’s time we ___ (go) home.
2. This argument has continued for months. It’s about time they ___ (make) a decision.
3. It is time for the children ___ (be) in bed.
