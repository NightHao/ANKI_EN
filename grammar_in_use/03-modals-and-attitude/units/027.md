# Unit 27 — `could (do)` and `could have (done)`

## A

Study this discussion:

> “What shall we do with the extra tickets?”  
> “We could give them to the neighbours.”

`Could + base verb` can suggest a possible action now or in the future. It is less certain than `can`: *We could take the early train* means it is one option. *I can stay with my brother* sounds like a firm, realistic arrangement; *Maybe I could stay with my brother* is less certain.

Use `could`, not `can`, for deliberately unreal or exaggerated possibilities: *I’m so hungry, I could eat two pizzas.*

## B

`Could` can also mean that a particular situation is possible now or later: *The rumour could be true, although I doubt it.* / *The temperature could drop overnight.* Compare *Traffic can be awful here* (a general fact) with *Traffic could be awful tomorrow* (one possible future situation).

## C

`Could have + past participle` looks back at a past possibility: *You could have stayed with us instead of paying for a hotel.* It was possible, but did not happen. *The cyclist could have been badly hurt, but she was not* describes a danger that did not become real.

## D

`Couldn’t` can mean “would not be possible now”: *I couldn’t live without music.* It can also be the past of `can`: *I couldn’t run yesterday because my ankle hurt.* Context shows which meaning is intended.

For the past impossible or best-possible result, use `couldn’t have`: *The trip was perfect; it couldn’t have been better.*

## Exercises

### 27.1

Complete with `can` or `could`.

1. We ___ meet at the library tomorrow, if that suits you.
2. The path is wet; it ___ become slippery later.
3. This device ___ overheat if you cover the vents. (general possibility)
4. I am exhausted. I ___ sleep for a day.

### 27.2

Complete with `could have` or `couldn’t have`.

1. You ___ (ask) me for help; I was at home.
2. The crash was serious, but it ___ (be) much worse.
3. The view was amazing. It ___ (be) better.

### 27.3

Choose the meaning of `couldn’t`.

1. I couldn’t carry that piano now. (past inability / not possible now)
2. I couldn’t attend the meeting yesterday because I was ill. (past inability / not possible now)
