# Unit 36 — `would`

## A

Study this imagined choice:

> “Would you tell the whole team?”  
> “No, I wouldn’t say anything until we knew more.”

Use `would` (`’d`) and `wouldn’t` for an unreal or imagined present/future situation: *I’d love to live near a forest.* / *It would be useful to have another screen.* We often use it with `if`: *I’d buy the jacket if it were cheaper.*

Use `would have + past participle` for an unreal past: *I would have called, but I did not have your number.* / *You would have enjoyed the exhibition.*

## B

Compare `will` with `would`: *I’ll stay because I have time* reports a real decision. *I’d stay, but I have to leave* imagines the alternative, which is not possible. In reported past speech, `would` can be the past of `will`: *Nora said she would email.*

## C

`Wouldn’t` can mean refusal: *I asked him to slow down, but he wouldn’t listen.* / *The old engine wouldn’t start.* It can describe a person or thing that refuses to cooperate.

## D

`Would` can describe repeated past actions: *Every summer, we would cycle to the lake.* / *Whenever the dog heard thunder, it would hide under the table.* Use it for repeated actions, not a past state; say *We used to live by the lake*, not normally *we would live by the lake*.

## Exercises

### 36.1

Complete with `would` or `would have`.

1. I ___ (join) you, but I have a deadline.
2. I ___ (join) you yesterday, but I was ill.
3. Without her help last week, we ___ (not / finish) the project.

### 36.2

Choose the better form.

1. When I was young, every winter we **(would go / would live)** skiing.
2. I asked Kim to explain, but she **(wouldn’t / won’t)** answer.
3. He promised that he **(would call / will call)** the next day.

### 36.3

Write a sentence using `would` for the prompt.

1. imagined present: I / love / travel / by train / more often
2. repeated past action: whenever / we / visit / my uncle / he / make / soup
