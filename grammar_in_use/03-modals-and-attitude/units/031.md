# Unit 31 — `have to` and `must`

## A

Study this rule from a workplace:

> “I have to wear a badge to enter the lab.”

`Have to` expresses necessity or obligation. It works like an ordinary verb phrase in questions and negatives: *Do I have to register?* / *She does not have to work on Fridays.* For the past, use `had to`: *We had to leave before the final talk.*

Other forms are possible: *I’ll have to replace the screen.* / *We might have to change the date.*

## B

`Must` often expresses the speaker’s own judgement, a strong recommendation, or an immediate need: *I must remember to send that form.* / *You must try the bakery near here.* For an external fact or routine, `have to` is more usual: *I have to start at 7:30 every day.*

Use `must` in written rules and instructions: *Visitors must show identification.* Do not normally use `must` for past necessity; use `had to`.

## C

`Mustn’t` means prohibition: *You mustn’t share this password.* `Don’t have to` means no necessity: *You don’t have to stay; I can finish alone.* They do not mean the same thing.

## D

`Have got to` is an informal alternative to `have to` in the present: *I’ve got to catch the early train.* / *When have you got to leave?* It is not normally used for the past.

## Exercises

### 31.1

Complete with a form of `have to`.

1. We ___ (wear) helmets on this site.
2. Yesterday I ___ (take) a different route because the bridge was closed.
3. ___ you ___ (pay) to enter the museum?
4. I ___ (not / get up) early tomorrow.

### 31.2

Choose the better form.

1. I ___ call my grandmother tonight; it is her birthday. **(must / have to)**
2. Nurses ___ work shifts at this hospital. **(must / have to)**
3. Applications ___ arrive by 5 p.m. **(must / had to)**
4. You ___ tell anyone about this. **(mustn’t / don’t have to)**

### 31.3

Write a sentence using `have got to`.

1. I / leave / now
2. what time / you / be / at the airport?
