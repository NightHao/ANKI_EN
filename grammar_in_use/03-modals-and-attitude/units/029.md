# Unit 29 — `may` and `might` 1

## A

Study this uncertainty:

> “Where is the curator?”  
> “She might be in the archive, but I’m not sure.”

Use `may` or `might` for a possibility, not a certainty: *The parcel may arrive today.* / *He might know the answer.* Both can be followed by `be`, `be -ing`, or another base verb: *They may be waiting outside.* / *I might need your help.* Negatives are `may not` and `might not`: *The key may not fit.*

`may be` is two words (`may + be`); `maybe` is one adverb: *Maybe the key fits.*

## B

For an uncertain past explanation, use `may/might have + past participle`: *Nia may have missed the train.* / *They might not have heard the announcement.* We are saying “perhaps”, not reporting a fact.

## C

`Could` can also express possibility: *The story could be true.* / *You could have put the receipt in your bag.* But `couldn’t have` means impossible, while `might not have` means possibly not:

> “Why has no one answered?”  
> “They might not have heard us.” (perhaps they did not)  
> “They couldn’t have heard us from that distance.” (impossible)

## Exercises

### 29.1

Complete with `may` or `might`.

1. I am not sure where Yusuf is. He ___ be having lunch.
2. The answer ___ not be correct; check it again.
3. Ask Ren. She ___ know the code.
4. They ___ arrive late because of the strike.

### 29.2

Complete with a possible past explanation.

1. I cannot find my headphones. (leave / them / at work)
2. Nora did not answer her phone. (be / asleep)
3. The guests were absent. (not / receive / the address)

### 29.3

Choose the better form.

1. “Why did he not reply?” “He **(might not have seen / couldn’t have seen)** the email.”
2. “Could he hear us from the roof?” “No, he **(might not have heard / couldn’t have heard)** us.”
3. **(May be / Maybe)** the gallery is closed today.
