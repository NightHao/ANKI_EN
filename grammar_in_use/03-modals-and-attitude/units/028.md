# Unit 28 — `must` and `can’t`

## A

Study the evidence:

> “You have been unpacking boxes since dawn.”  
> “You must be exhausted.”

Use `must` when we make a strong conclusion that something is certainly true: *That must be the delivery driver; nobody else was expected.* / *You must be joking.* Use `can’t` when we conclude that something is impossible: *You cannot be hungry already; we have just eaten.* / *They can’t know the route; they arrived yesterday.*

These are deductions, not obligations. Negative deduction is `can’t`, not `mustn’t`.

## B

For a deduction about an earlier situation, use `must have` or `can’t have` plus a past participle: *Someone must have left the window open.* / *He can’t have received the invitation, or he would have replied.*

The form can be `have been + -ing`: *The road is wet; it must have been raining.* / *She can’t have been listening when I explained it.* `Couldn’t have` has the same deduction meaning as `can’t have`.

## Exercises

### 28.1

Complete with `must` or `can’t`.

1. That suitcase is enormous. It ___ be very heavy.
2. The shop is dark and locked. It ___ be open.
3. You got here in five minutes. You ___ have taken a taxi.
4. He has not read the instructions; he ___ know the answer.

### 28.2

Write a deduction using the prompt.

1. The printer has no paper. (somebody / use / it)
2. Lena is in another country today. (she / attend / the meeting yesterday afternoon)
3. The grass is wet, but it is dry now. (it / rain / earlier)

### 28.3

Correct the sentence where necessary.

1. You mustn’t be the new designer; I was told she is arriving tomorrow.
2. They must have been waiting a long time; the queue has not moved.
3. I can’t have to remember his name.
