# Unit 32 — `must`, `mustn’t`, and `needn’t`

## A

Study this safety notice:

> “You must keep the gate closed. You mustn’t leave it unlocked.”

`Must` says that an action is necessary. `Mustn’t` says it is forbidden: do not do it. *You must bring photo ID.* / *You mustn’t touch the exposed wire.*

## B

`Needn’t` means “not necessary”; the action is allowed but not required: *You needn’t wait for me.* / *We needn’t hurry; there is plenty of time.* We can also say `do not need to`: *You don’t need to print the email.*

Compare *You needn’t tell Sora* (you may tell her, but it is unnecessary) with *You mustn’t tell Sora* (do not tell her).

## C

`Needn’t have + past participle` means someone did an action but later learned it was unnecessary: *You needn’t have booked a taxi; I had already arranged a lift.*

`Didn’t need to` or `didn’t have to` means there was no necessity. It does not say whether the action happened: *I didn’t need to bring lunch, but I did anyway.*

## D

Compare the time reference: *You needn’t worry* means no need now or in the future. *You needn’t have worried* means you worried in the past, but it turned out to be unnecessary.

## Exercises

### 32.1

Choose `mustn’t` or `needn’t`.

1. You ___ feed the animals; the staff will do it.
2. You ___ enter this room without protective clothing.
3. We have plenty of chairs, so you ___ bring one.
4. You ___ forget the emergency number.

### 32.2

Complete with `needn’t have` or `didn’t need to`.

1. I bought a map, but the hotel gave us one for free. I ___ (buy) one.
2. The event was cancelled, so we ___ (go). We stayed home.
3. There was no requirement to bring a gift, but she brought one. She ___ (bring) one.

### 32.3

Correct the sentence where necessary.

1. You don’t need bring an umbrella.
2. You needn’t to speak loudly.
3. You needn’t have printed the form; a digital copy was enough.
