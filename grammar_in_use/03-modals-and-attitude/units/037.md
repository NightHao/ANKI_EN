# Unit 37 — Requests, offers, permission, and invitations

## A

Study this request:

> “Could you hold the door for a moment, please?”

Use `can you ...?` or `could you ...?` to ask somebody to do something. `Could` is often more polite: *Can you send the receipt?* / *Could you tell me where the lift is?* We can also say *Do you think you could help me with this box?*

To ask for a thing, use *Can/Could I have ...?* or *Can I get ...?*: *Could we have two glasses of water, please?* `May I have ...?` is also possible and more formal.

## B

Use `Can I ...?` or `Could I ...?` to ask permission to do something: *Could I use this seat?* / *Can I speak to the supervisor?* `May I ...?` is formal. Other polite patterns are *Do you mind if I open the window?* and *Is it OK if I sit here?*

## C

Use `Can I ...?` to offer help: *Can I carry that for you?* / *Can I get you some water?* To invite or offer something, use `Would you like ...?`: *Would you like some fruit?* / *Would you like to join us for dinner?* Do not use *Do you like ...?* for a one-time invitation.

## D

`I’d like` is a polite way to say what you want: *I’d like to book a table for four.* / *I’d like some information about day trips, please.*

## Exercises

### 37.1

Match the purpose to the sentence: request, permission, offer, or invitation.

1. Could you check this email for me?
2. Do you mind if I use your charger?
3. Can I get you a chair?
4. Would you like to come to the exhibition?

### 37.2

Complete the polite question.

1. You want the bill in a restaurant. (could / we / have / bill?)
2. You want to use a colleague’s printer. (could / I / use / printer?)
3. You want someone to lower loud music. (could / you / turn / it down?)

### 37.3

Choose the correct form.

1. **(Would you like / Do you like)** to try this dessert?
2. **(Can I / Do I)** carry those bags for you?
3. I ___ like to try on these shoes, please. (would / will)
