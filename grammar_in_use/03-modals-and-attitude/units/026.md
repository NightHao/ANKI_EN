# Unit 26 — `can`, `could`, and `(be) able to`

## A

Study this exchange:

> “I cannot find a screwdriver.”  
> “You can use mine.”

Use `can + base verb` for ability, possibility, permission, or an offer of help: *We can see the harbour from here.* / *Can you speak Portuguese?* / *You can come with us if you want.* The negative is `can’t` (`cannot`): *I’m sorry, I can’t stay.*

`Be able to` has a similar meaning, but `can` is more usual where it is available.

## B

`Can` has only present and past forms (`can`, `could`), so use `be able to` with other forms: *I haven’t been able to sleep lately.* / *She may be able to help.* / *Applicants must be able to write clearly.*

## C

Use `could` for general past ability and especially with perception verbs: *My uncle could repair almost anything.* / *From the balcony, we could hear the band.* It can also mean past permission: *During the holiday, we could stay out late.*

## D

For one particular successful action in the past, use `was/were able to` or `managed to`, not normally `could`: *Despite the smoke, everyone was able to leave safely.* / *I managed to find the right platform.* But `couldn’t` is possible for a particular failure: *I searched everywhere, but I couldn’t find the receipt.*

## Exercises

### 26.1

Complete with `can` or a form of `be able to`.

1. I ___ (not / sleep) well recently.
2. After the course, you should ___ (use) the software confidently.
3. We ___ (see) the lighthouse from our room.
4. She might ___ (join) us later.

### 26.2

Choose the better form.

1. When he was young, my father **(could / was able to)** speak three languages.
2. After several attempts, we **(could / managed to)** open the safe.
3. I tried to contact her, but I **(couldn’t / wasn’t able to)** reach her. 
4. I sat at the back and **(could / was able to)** not hear the speaker clearly.

### 26.3

Make a sentence using the prompt.

1. the firefighters / escape / the building (one successful event)
2. I / not / understand / the instructions (past failure)
