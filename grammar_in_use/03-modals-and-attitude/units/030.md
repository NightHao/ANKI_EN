# Unit 30 — `may` and `might` 2

## A

Study this undecided plan:

> “Have you chosen a holiday destination?”  
> “Not yet. I may go hiking in Slovenia.”

Use `may` or `might` for a possible future action or event: *We might need more chairs.* / *The ferry may not run tomorrow.* Compare *I’m going to buy a laptop* (decided) with *I might buy a laptop* (only possible).

In unreal situations, use `might`, not normally `may`: *If I had a larger kitchen, I might cook more often.*

## B

`May/might be -ing` expresses a possible future activity or plan: *Do not call at nine; I might be putting the baby to bed.* It is uncertain. *I’ll be putting the baby to bed at nine* describes a known plan or expected future activity.

## C

`Might as well` (or `may as well`) means “this is a sensible choice because there is no better alternative”: *The queue is not moving, so we might as well come back later.* / *You are already at the station; you may as well buy the tickets now.*

## Exercises

### 30.1

Choose the better form.

1. We have not decided yet, so we **(might rent / are going to rent)** a car.
2. They have booked a table, so they **(might eat / are going to eat)** there.
3. If I earned more, I **(may move / might move)** closer to town.

### 30.2

Complete with `might`.

1. Take a jacket; it ___ (get) cold later.
2. I cannot promise to attend. I ___ (work) late.
3. We ___ (have to / wait) for a table.
4. The technician ___ (be able to / fix) it tomorrow.

### 30.3

Rewrite with `might as well`.

1. The last bus has gone. Walking is the only reasonable option.
2. You are already making soup; add enough for us too.

30.1 1. might rent 2. are going to eat 3. might move.

30.2 1. might get 2. might be working 3. might have to wait 4. might be able to fix.

30.3 1. We might as well walk. 2. You might as well make enough for us too.
