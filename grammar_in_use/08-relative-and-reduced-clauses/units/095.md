# Unit 95 — Relative clauses 4: extra information

## A

Compare these two types of relative clause:

> “The neighbour **who owns the grey car** is a doctor.”  
> “My neighbour Luis, **who owns the grey car**, is a doctor.”

In the first sentence, the clause identifies which neighbour we mean. It is essential
information, so there are no commas.

In the second, **my neighbour Luis** already identifies the person. The relative
clause only adds extra information, so it is separated by commas.

The distinction changes meaning:

- “My brothers who live abroad call every week.”  
  (only the brothers who live abroad)
- “My brothers, who live abroad, call every week.”  
  (all my brothers live abroad)

## B

In an identifying relative clause, we can use **that**:

- “Do you know anyone who/that speaks Thai?”
- “We stayed at the hotel which/that you recommended.”

When the relative word is the object, it can be omitted: “the hotel you recommended”.

In an extra-information clause, do not use **that**, and do not omit **who/which**:

- “The Grand Hotel, which a colleague recommended, was full.”  
  (not “that”; not simply “The Grand Hotel, a colleague recommended”)
- “I met Noah, who I hadn’t seen for years.”

Formal **whom** is possible when the person is the object: “Noah, whom I hadn’t seen
for years”.

## C

Use **who** for people and **which** for things in extra-information clauses. **Whose**
and **where** work in both types:

- “A cyclist whose helmet was damaged spoke to police.”
- “Mina, whose helmet was damaged, spoke to police.”
- “We visited the town where my father grew up.”
- “Kyoto, where my father grew up, is beautiful in autumn.”

## Exercises

### 95.1

Add commas where the relative clause gives extra information.

1. I own one laptop. My laptop which I bought last month has stopped working.
2. The laptop that is on the kitchen table belongs to Kai.
3. Professor Lin is already identified by name. Professor Lin who taught me statistics has retired.

### 95.2

Choose all possible relative words.

1. The café ___ we usually visit is closed. (**that / which / —**)
2. The Blue Café, ___ we usually visit, is closed. (**that / which / —**)
3. My aunt, ___ lives in Perth, is visiting. (**who / that / —**)

### 95.3

Explain the likely difference: “The students who passed celebrated” / “The students,
who passed, celebrated.”

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**95.1** 1. I own one laptop. My laptop, which I bought last month, has stopped
working. 2. No commas: the clause identifies the laptop. 3. Professor Lin is already
identified by name. Professor Lin, who taught me statistics, has retired.

**95.2** 1. that, which or no relative word 2. which only 3. who only.

**95.3** Without commas, only the successful students celebrated. With commas, the
sentence implies that all the students passed and adds this as extra information.
