# Unit 94 — Relative clauses 3: `whose`, `whom` and `where`

## A

Study this situation:

> We stopped to help a driver. Her car had broken down.

We can say:

> “We stopped to help a driver **whose car had broken down**.”

Use **whose** to show that the following person or thing belongs or relates to the
person before it:

- “I met someone whose sister works with you.”
- “A widower is a man whose spouse has died.”

Compare “the man who knows Tara” with “the man whose daughter knows Tara”.
**Who’s** means **who is** or **who has**; it is not the same as **whose**.

## B

**Whom** can replace **who** when it is the object:

- “Ravi is someone whom I respect.”
- “These are colleagues with whom I can speak openly.”

**Whom** is formal and uncommon in relaxed conversation. We usually say “someone I
respect” or “colleagues I can speak openly with”. After a fronted preposition, however,
formal English uses **whom** for a person.

## C

Use **where** in a relative clause about a place:

- “This is the neighbourhood where I grew up.”
- “We found a café where we could work quietly.”
- “I want to live somewhere where public transport is reliable.”

**Where** means *in/at which place*, so do not add **there**: “the town where I grew
up” (not “where I grew up there”).

## D

With **day, year, time** and similar time nouns, the relative word is often omitted:

- “Friday is the day I leave.”
- “I remember the first time we met.”

**That** is also possible: “the year that I graduated”.

We say **the reason I’m calling**, **the reason that I’m calling** or **the reason why
I’m calling**. Do not normally combine both: not “the reason why that I’m calling”.

## Exercises

### 94.1

Complete with **who, whose, whom** or **where**.

1. I know a designer ___ work has won several awards.
2. The village ___ we stayed had no supermarket.
3. She is the person to ___ you should send the form.
4. I have a friend ___ is learning Arabic.

### 94.2

Rewrite in ordinary spoken English.

1. The consultant to whom I spoke was helpful.
2. The people with whom we travelled were friendly.

### 94.3

Correct: “That’s the restaurant where we had lunch there.”

<summary>Answers</summary>

**94.1** 1. whose 2. where 3. whom 4. who.

**94.2** 1. The consultant I spoke to was helpful. 2. The people we travelled
with were friendly.

**94.3** “That’s the restaurant where we had lunch.”
