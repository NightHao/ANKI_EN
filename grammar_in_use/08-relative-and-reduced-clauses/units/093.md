# Unit 93 — Relative clauses 2: leaving out the relative word

## A

Look at this sentence:

> “The engineer **who designed the bridge** won an award.”

**Who** is the subject of **designed**. When **who, that** or **which** is the subject
of a relative clause, we cannot leave it out:

- “The bus that stops here goes downtown.”
- “Where are the documents which were on my desk?”

We cannot say “the engineer designed the bridge won an award”.

## B

Sometimes the relative word is the object:

> “The engineer **who I called** was unavailable.”  
> I called **the engineer**.

Here **I** is the subject and **who** is the object of **called**, so we can omit the
relative word:

- “The engineer I called was unavailable.”
- “Did you find the documents you lost?”
- “The film we watched was excellent.”
- “Is there anything I can do?”

Do not repeat the object: “the documents you lost” (not “the documents you lost
them”).

## C

In everyday relative clauses, a preposition commonly stays at the end:

- “Do you know the person Mina is talking to?”
- “The chair I was sitting on was uncomfortable.”
- “Are these the files you were looking for?”

The relative word can be omitted because it is the object of the preposition. It can
also be included: “the person who/that Mina is talking to”. Do not add another object
after the preposition.

## D

Use **that** after **everything, something, anything, all** or a noun:

- “Everything (that) they promised was delivered.”
- “I spent all the money (that) I had.”

Use **what** when there is no noun before it: “What they promised was delivered.”
**What** means *the thing(s) that*.

## Exercises

### 93.1

Can the relative word be omitted? Write both versions when possible.

1. the woman who manages the shop
2. the woman who I met yesterday
3. the phone that rang during the meeting
4. the phone that I bought last week

### 93.2

Join the sentences.

1. I slept in a room. The room was very cold.
2. You asked for some information. Here it is.
3. We travelled with a guide. Do you remember him?

### 93.3

Correct: “The laptop I told you about it has been sold.”
