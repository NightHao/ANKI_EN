# Unit 97 — `-ing` and past-participle clauses after nouns

## A

Study these examples:

> “Who’s the man speaking to Elena?”  
> “The cyclist injured in the crash is recovering.”

The clauses **speaking to Elena** and **injured in the crash** give information about
the nouns before them.

We use an **-ing clause** when the noun performs the action. It can describe an action
in progress at a particular time:

- “The people waiting outside are here for the interview.”  
  (= the people who are waiting outside)
- “I was woken by a dog barking.”  
  (= a dog was barking)

It can also describe a regular function or permanent connection:

- “The path leading to the lake is closed.”  
  (= the path that leads to the lake)
- “We booked a room overlooking the harbour.”

## B

A past-participle clause normally has a passive meaning. The noun receives the action:

- “The paintings displayed upstairs are for sale.”  
  (= the paintings that are displayed upstairs)
- “Most phones made here are exported.”
- “The documents sent yesterday have not arrived.”

Although many past participles end in **-ed**, irregular forms are common: **made,
built, stolen, written, chosen**. The important contrast is active versus passive, not
the spelling:

| active noun | passive noun |
|---|---|
| “the technician repairing the lift” | “the lift repaired last week” |

## C

We also use these clauses after **there is/are/was/were**:

- “There’s someone waiting at reception.”
- “Were there many passengers injured?”
- “There was a bicycle left outside.”

In this pattern, **left** often means *remaining or still available*: “There are only
two seats left.”

## D

An **-ing** or past-participle clause often corresponds to a relative clause, as the
expansions above show. Keep the full relative clause if you need an explicit modal,
tense or negative meaning: “the forms that must be signed”; “the guests who did not
receive an invitation”.

Infinitival modifiers such as **the first person to arrive**, **the next train to
leave** and **the only one to notice** are taught in
[Unit 65](../../06-verb-patterns-ing-and-to/units/065.md).

## Exercises

### 97.1

Join each pair using an **-ing** clause.

1. A road connects the two towns. It is often flooded.
2. A woman was carrying a red folder. She asked for you.

### 97.2

Complete with a suitable past participle.

1. The meals ___ in this kitchen contain no nuts. (prepare)
2. The bicycle ___ from the station has been found. (steal)
3. We replaced the windows ___ in the storm. (damage)

### 97.3

Expand the clause in bold.

1. the students **waiting downstairs**
2. the bridge **opened by the mayor last month**
