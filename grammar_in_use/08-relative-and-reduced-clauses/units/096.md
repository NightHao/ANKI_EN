# Unit 96 — Relative clauses 5: prepositions, quantities and whole ideas

## A

In formal English, a preposition can come before **whom** for people or **which** for
things:

- “Dr Shah, **to whom** I sent the report, has replied.”
- “We had a map, **without which** we would have been lost.”

In conversation, the preposition usually stays at the end:

- “Dr Shah, who I sent the report to, has replied.”
- “She works for a company which I had never heard of.”

When the preposition is at the end, use **who**, not **whom**, in ordinary speech—or
omit the object relative word in an identifying clause.

## B

After a number or quantity, use **of whom** for people and **of which** for things:

- “Ella has four cousins, two of whom live nearby.”
- “They asked ten questions, most of which I could answer.”
- “Three applicants arrived, none of whom had the correct form.”
- “The house has two balconies, neither of which faces the road.”

Other useful patterns include **many/some/all/both/one of whom/which**. We can also
say “a hotel, the name of which I have forgotten” or, more naturally in conversation,
“a hotel whose name I have forgotten”.

## C

**Which** can refer to the whole fact expressed by the previous clause:

> “The train was cancelled, **which** caused a long delay.”

Here **which** means *the fact that the train was cancelled*, not the train itself.
Use **which**, not **what**, in this pattern:

- “Marta offered to help, which was kind of her.”
- “Nobody warned us, which I found surprising.”

Compare “What she offered was useful”, where **what** means *the thing that she
offered*.

## Exercises

### 96.1

Rewrite in the other style.

1. Mr Wu, to whom I spoke, is the project manager. (conversational)
2. The data came from one survey, which we know little about. (formal)

### 96.2

Complete with a quantity + **whom/which**.

1. I invited six friends. Three of them came.
2. The course has eight modules. Two of them are optional.
3. We interviewed four people. None of them had used the software.

### 96.3

Join with **which**.

1. The shop refunded the full amount. This surprised me.
2. Leo missed the last bus. This meant he had to walk home.
