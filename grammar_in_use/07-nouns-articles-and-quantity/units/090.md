# Unit 90 — `all`, `every`, and `whole`

## A

Study this report:

> Everyone left safely. All the doors were locked, and everything was quiet.

Use `everyone/everybody` for people and `everything` for things: *Everybody has a seat.* *Everything looks ready.* Do not normally use bare `all` in this position. But use `all + noun`, `all of + pronoun`, or `we/they ... all`: *All guests arrived.* *All of us agreed.* *They all agreed.*

`All I need is a charger` means the only thing I need.

## B

`Whole` means complete. Use it mainly with singular countable nouns and a determiner: *I read the whole article.* *She ate a whole mango.* Use `all` with uncountable nouns: *all the information*, *all the water* (not *the whole information*).

## C

Use `every` for frequency: *I call my parents every Sunday.* *A train comes every fifteen minutes.* Use `all day/all week` or `the whole day/week` for one complete period: *It rained all day.*

Compare *I see him every time I visit* (on each occasion) with *She talks all the time* (continuously).

## D

`Every`, `everyone`, and `everything` take singular verbs, but singular `they/them/their` is natural for people: *Every passenger has their own ticket.*

## Exercises

### 90.1

Choose the correct form.

1. (`Everyone` / `All`) was ready to leave.
2. I spent (`the whole` / `all`) afternoon answering emails.
3. Did you read (`the whole` / `all`) report?
4. The shop delivers (`every` / `all`) Friday.
5. We have been waiting (`all day` / `every day`).

### 90.2

Complete the sentences.

1. ___ I want is a quiet evening. (`all`)
2. Every student ___ a name badge. (`have`)
3. I check the forecast ___ morning. (`every`)
4. ___ of them knew what to do. (`all`)

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**90.1** 1. *Everyone* 2. *the whole* or *all* 3. *the whole* 4. *every* 5. *all day*.

**90.2** 1. *All* 2. *has* 3. *every* 4. *All*.
