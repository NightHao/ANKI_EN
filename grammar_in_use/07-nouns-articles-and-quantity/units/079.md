# Unit 79 — Singular and plural nouns

## A

Study this comment:

> “These scissors are blunt. I need a new pair of scissors.”

Some things with two parts have plural names: `trousers`, `jeans`, `shorts`, `pyjamas`, `glasses`, `binoculars`, and `scissors`. They take plural verbs: *My glasses are on the desk.* Use `a pair of` for one item: *a pair of trousers*.

## B

Some nouns ending in `-ics` are usually singular: *Economics is difficult for me.* *The news is encouraging.* Other words have the same singular and plural form: *one series / two series*, *a species / many species*, *one means / several means*.

## C

Group nouns such as `team`, `family`, `staff`, `committee`, and `government` may take singular or plural verbs. Use singular when treating the group as one unit; plural when thinking about its members: *The committee has reached a decision.* / *The committee are arguing among themselves.*

`Police` is always plural: *The police are investigating.* For one person, say *a police officer*. `People` is the usual plural of `person`.

The same singular/plural choice is possible with company and team names: *Orion Ltd has announced its results.* / *Orion Ltd have announced their results.*

## D

Amounts of money, periods, and distances often take a singular verb as one total: *Twenty minutes is enough.* *Five hundred euros was missing.*

## Exercises

### 79.1

Choose the correct form.

1. These jeans (`is` / `are`) too loose.
2. Mathematics (`is` / `are`) my best subject.
3. Two kilometres (`is` / `are`) not far by bike.
4. The police (`has` / `have`) spoken to the witnesses.
5. This is the third TV (`series` / `serieses`) I have watched.

### 79.2

Complete or correct the sentences.

1. I need ___ of sunglasses. (`pair`)
2. My family ___ planning a reunion. (Treat the family as one unit.)
3. The staff ___ disagreeing about the schedule. (Think of individuals.)
4. There was a police at the gate.

**79.1** 1. *are* 2. *is* 3. *is* 4. *have* 5. *series*.

**79.2** 1. *a pair* 2. *is* 3. *are* 4. *There was a police officer at the gate.*
