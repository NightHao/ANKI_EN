# Unit 88 — `all`, `most`, `none`, and `of`

## A

Study these two statements:

> Most cafés accept cards. Most of the cafés on this street accept cards.

Use `all`, `most`, `some`, `many`, `no`, and similar words directly before a noun for a general meaning: *All birds have feathers.* *Most weekends are quiet.* Do not say *most of weekends* or *some of people*.

## B

Use `of` before a specific group introduced by `the`, `this/these`, `my`, `those`, or a possessive name: *most of the afternoon*, *some of my notes*, *none of those tickets*. `All` and `half` may omit `of` before these forms: *all (of) my friends*, *half (of) this cake*.

Compare *Most films are too long* with *Most of the films we saw were too long*.

## C

Use `of` before object pronouns: *all of us*, *none of them*, *half of it*, *any of you*. We do not say *all us* or *half it*.

## D

These words can stand alone when the noun is already known: *Some trains stop here; most do not.* *A few of the lights work, but none of them is bright enough.*

## Exercises

### 88.1

Add `of` where necessary.

1. Most ___ my work is online.
2. All ___ animals need water.
3. Have you read any ___ these articles?
4. None ___ us knew the address.
5. Half ___ the cake has disappeared.

### 88.2

Choose the correct phrase.

1. (`Most students` / `Most of students`) need a break sometimes.
2. (`Most of the students` / `Most the students`) passed the test.
3. I invited ten people. Some came, but (`most` / `most of`) did not.
4. I have read only half (`it` / `of it`).

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**88.1** 1. *of* 2. No `of` 3. *of* 4. *of* 5. *of*.

**88.2** 1. *Most students* 2. *Most of the students* 3. *most* 4. *of it*.
