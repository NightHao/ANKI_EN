# Unit 84 — Existential `there` and referential `it`

## A

Study this conversation:

> “There’s a new bakery near the bridge.”
> “Really? Is it open on Sundays?”

Use `there + be` to introduce something or say that it exists: *There was a delay on the motorway.* `It` refers back to a known thing, fact, place, or situation: *The bakery is excellent. It opens at seven.*

`There` can also mean “in/at that place”: *The flat is empty; there is nobody living there.*

## B

Use `there` with modal and other verb forms: *There might be a later train.* *There must have been a mistake.* *There is likely to be a queue.* *There used to be a bookshop here.*

Compare *There is sure to be a bus* (a bus exists) with *The bus is sure to be late* (a known bus).

## C

Use `it` as an introductory subject in common patterns: *It is dangerous to cycle without lights.* *It took us an hour to arrive.* *It is a pity that they cannot come.*

We also use `it` for weather, time, and distance: *It is snowing.* *How far is it to the harbour?* Compare *It was cold* with *There was a cold wind*.

## Exercises

### 84.1

Complete with `there` or `it`.

1. ___ was a power cut last night.
2. This new phone is expensive, but ___ is very reliable.
3. ___ must be an easier way to solve this.
4. ___ is difficult to park near the stadium.
5. ___ used to be a fountain in this square.

### 84.2

Correct `it` or `there` where necessary.

2. There is very windy today.
3. It is a long way from here to my office.
4. There was a lot of noise, so I closed the window.
