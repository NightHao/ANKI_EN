# Unit 85 — `some` and `any`

## A

Study this exchange:

> “I bought some fruit, but I did not buy any bread.”

In general, use `some` in positive statements and `any` in negative ones: *We have some time.* *We do not have any time.* The same pattern applies to `somebody/someone/something/somewhere` and `anybody/anyone/anything/anywhere`.

Negative ideas also take `any`: *She left without any warning.* *Hardly anyone understood.* *He refused to say anything.*

## B

In questions, use `some` when you expect something exists, or when offering and requesting: *Are you waiting for someone?* *Could I have some water?* *Would you like something to eat?*

Use `any` in open questions when you do not know whether something exists: *Is there anyone at home?* *Do you have any spare batteries?*

## C

Use `any` after `if` and in similar conditional meanings: *Tell me if you need anything.* *Please contact anyone who saw the accident.* *I apologise for any inconvenience.*

## D

`Any` can mean “it does not matter which”: *Choose any seat.* *You can call me any time.* *Anybody can learn the basics.*

`Someone/anyone` are grammatically singular, but singular `they/them/their` is natural after them: *If anyone calls, tell them I am out.*

## Exercises

### 85.1

Add `some`, `any`, or a related compound word.

1. We have not received ___ reply yet.
2. Would you like ___ more soup?
3. Is ___ waiting outside, or was that the wind?
4. If you find ___ useful, save the link.
5. You can sit ___ you like.

### 85.2

Choose the meaning or correct form.

1. *Take any route; they all lead to the lake.* What does `any` mean?
2. Correct: *I do not know somebody here.*
3. *Can I borrow some paper?* Why `some`, not `any`?
4. Complete: *If anyone needs help, ___ can ask me.*

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**85.1** 1. *any* 2. *some* 3. *anyone* 4. *anything* 5. *anywhere*.

**85.2** 1. “It does not matter which route.” 2. *I do not know anybody/anyone here.* 3. It is a request for something expected to be available. 4. *they*.
