# Unit 71 — Countable nouns with `a/an` and `some`

## A

Study this conversation:

> “Do you need an umbrella?”
> “No, but I need some batteries.”

Use `a/an` before a singular countable noun: *a key, an hour, a useful idea*. A singular countable noun cannot normally stand alone: *She is wearing a coat* (not *wearing coat*).

Use `a/an` to say what kind of thing or person someone is: *A dolphin is a mammal.* *My cousin is an architect.* It also appears in exclamations: *What a strange question!*

## B

Plural countable nouns can stand alone: *Those houses are old.* Use `some + plural noun` when you mean a number or an unspecified group: *I bought some batteries.* *Some colleagues are visiting tomorrow.*

Do not add `some` when you mean all things of a kind: *I like novels.* *Birds build nests.*

## C

`Some` can also mean “some, but not all”: *Some customers paid by card; others used cash.* This is different from *some apples* when you simply mean an unspecified quantity.

Use `a/an` with body parts in patterns such as *He has a long beard* and plural forms without an article in *She has green eyes*.

## Exercises

### 71.1

Add `a/an`, `some`, or no word.

1. We saw ___ owl in ___ tree.
2. I need ___ new socks for the trip.
3. ___ teachers work very long hours.
4. My sister is ___ engineer.
5. What ___ beautiful photo!

### 71.2

Identify the meaning of `some`, or correct the sentence.

1. *Some guests have already arrived, but others are still travelling.*
2. *Could you buy some oranges?*
3. Correct: *I enjoy some films.* (Meaning: films in general.)
4. Correct: *He has blue eye.*

<summary>Answers</summary>

**71.1** 1. *an; a* 2. *some* 3. No word 4. *an* 5. *a*.

**71.2** 1. “Some but not all.” 2. An unspecified number. 3. *I enjoy films.* 4. *He has blue eyes.*
