# Unit 70 — Countable and uncountable nouns: meaning changes

## A

Study this order at a café:

> “Two coffees and a tea, please.”

`coffee` and `tea` are normally uncountable when they mean the drink in general: *I do not drink much coffee.* In an order, `a coffee` usually means a cup or serving of coffee. Other nouns can change similarly: *a noise* is one particular sound, but *noise* is sound in general; *a hair* is one strand, but *hair* is all the hair on someone’s head.

## B

Compare these meanings:

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| *We found a room in a guesthouse.* | *There is no room for another chair.* |
| *It was an unusual experience.* | *She has experience in design.* |
| *We had a wonderful time.* | *I do not have time today.* |

`work` is generally uncountable when it means employment or effort: *He is looking for work.* But `a job` is countable: *He is looking for a job.*

## C

Some common nouns are normally uncountable: `advice`, `information`, `furniture`, `luggage`, `scenery`, `traffic`, `weather`, and `news`. Say *some advice*, *a piece of information*, and *a suitcase*, not *an advice*, *informations*, or *luggages*.

`News` looks plural but takes singular grammar: *The news is encouraging.* `travel` means travelling generally; use `trip` or `journey` for one occasion.

## Exercises

### 70.1

Choose the correct form.

1. There was (`a light` / `light`) in the upstairs window.
2. I need (`paper` / `a paper`) to make notes on.
3. We had (`a great time` / `great time`) at the festival.
4. My brother has very short (`hair` / `hairs`).

### 70.2

Correct the noun phrase where necessary.

1. She gave me an advice about renting a flat.
2. The traffic were terrible this morning.
3. We carried all our luggages upstairs.
4. They made excellent progress in six months.
