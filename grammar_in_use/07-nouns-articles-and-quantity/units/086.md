# Unit 86 — `no`, `none`, `any`, `nothing`, and `nobody`

## A

Study this exchange:

> “Are there any seats left?”
> “None. There are no seats left.”

Use `no + noun`: *There is no signal here.* `No` means “not a” or “not any”. Use `none` without a following noun: *How many messages arrived? None.* Use `none of` before a specified group: *None of the answers was correct.* / *None of the answers were correct.*

## B

`Nothing`, `nobody/no-one`, and `nowhere` can stand alone or appear in a sentence: *Nothing happened.* *Nobody answered the door.* *We have nowhere to sit.* They mean `not anything`, `not anybody`, and `not anywhere`.

Do not use another negative verb with them: *I said nothing* or *I did not say anything* (not *I did not say nothing*).

## C

Use singular `they/them/their` after `nobody` and `no-one`: *Nobody left their coat, did they?* *No-one remembered to bring their ticket.*

## D

Compare `no-` with `any-` when `any` means “it does not matter which”: *Nobody can lift that cabinet.* *Anybody can use this simple app.* *I want nothing.* *I could eat anything.*

## Exercises

### 86.1

Complete with `no`, `none`, or `any`.

1. There were ___ buses after midnight.
2. I do not have ___ change.
3. “How much coffee is left?” “___.”
4. ___ of these passwords works.
5. We had ___ reason to cancel the booking.

### 86.2

Correct the sentence or complete it.

1. I did not see nobody at the station.
2. “What did you buy?” “___.”
3. There is not nowhere to park.
4. Nobody brought ___ laptop, did ___?
5. The task is easy; ___ can finish it.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**86.1** 1. *no* 2. *any* 3. *None* 4. *None* 5. *no*.

**86.2** 1. *I did not see anybody at the station.* / *I saw nobody at the station.* 2. *Nothing.* 3. *There is nowhere to park.* 4. *their; they* 5. *Anybody*.
