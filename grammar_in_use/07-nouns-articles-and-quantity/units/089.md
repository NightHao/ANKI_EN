# Unit 89 — `both`, `neither`, and `either`

## A

Study this choice:

> “Should we take the stairs or the lift?” “Either is fine.”

Use `both`, `neither`, and `either` for two things. `Both` means the two together; `neither` means not one and not the other; `either` means one or the other: *Both routes are scenic.* *Neither route is short.* *You can take either route.*

They can stand alone: *Which colour do you prefer? Both.* *Tea or juice? Either.*

## B

Use `both of/neither of/either of` before a specific group: *both of these keys*, *neither of the answers*, *either of my sisters*. Before `us/you/them`, use `of`: *Both of us were tired.* *Can either of you drive?*

After `neither of`, a singular or plural verb is possible: *Neither of them is/are ready.*

## C

Use the paired patterns `both ... and`, `neither ... nor`, and `either ... or`: *Both rain and wind delayed us.* *Neither the manager nor the assistant replied.* *Either you call today or I will cancel.*

## D

For more than two, use `all`, `none`, and `any`, not `both`, `neither`, and `either`: *All of the doors were locked.* *Any of the five buses will do.*

## Exercises

### 89.1

Complete with `both`, `neither`, or `either`.

1. I have tried two passwords, but ___ works.
2. You may sit on ___ side of the table.
3. The twins speak ___ French and Arabic.
4. “Is the meeting on Tuesday or Wednesday?” “___; it is on Thursday.”

### 89.2

Rewrite using the word given.

1. Maya and Olek were absent. (`both`)
2. The restaurant did not have vegan food, and the café did not either. (`neither ... nor`)
3. You can send an email, or you can call. (`either ... or`)
4. Two visitors arrived; I did not recognise either. (`of them`)

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**89.1** 1. *neither* 2. *either* 3. *both* 4. *Neither*.

**89.2** 1. *Both Maya and Olek were absent.* 2. *Neither the restaurant nor the café had vegan food.* 3. *Either you send an email or you call.* 4. *I did not recognise either of them.*
