# Unit 83 — `a friend of mine`, `my own`, and `on my own`

## A

Study this invitation:

> A neighbour of mine is holding a barbecue on Saturday.

`A friend of mine` means one of my friends. Use the possessive pronoun after `of`: *a colleague of yours*, *some relatives of ours*, *an idea of hers*. Do not say *a friend of me*.

You can also say *a friend of Maya’s* or *a suggestion of the manager’s*.

## B

`my/your/his/her/our/their own + noun` means belonging only to that person or group, not borrowed or shared: *I would like my own desk.* *The apartment has its own entrance.*

The noun can be omitted: *Why use my laptop? Use your own.* You can also say *a study of my own* or *problems of their own*.

## C

`Own` can stress that a person does something rather than someone else doing it: *Kai repairs his own bicycle.* *We grow our own herbs.* This differs from a general possessive such as *his bicycle*.

## D

`On my own` and `by myself` both mean “alone”: *I live on my own.* / *I live by myself.* `By myself` is also common for doing something without help: *I carried the box by myself.*

## Exercises

### 83.1

Rewrite with the structure in brackets.

1. One of her cousins works in Brazil. (`of hers`)
2. I want a room that is not shared with anyone. (`my own`)
3. This plan is one of our ideas. (`of ours`)
4. He travelled alone. (`by`)

### 83.2

Choose the correct form.

1. We decorated the flat on (`ourselves` / `our own`).
2. You should make (`your own` / `yourself`) decision.
3. She sews (`her own` / `herself`) clothes.
4. A friend (`of me` / `of mine`) recommended this book.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**83.1** 1. *A cousin of hers works in Brazil.* 2. *I want my own room.* 3. *This is an idea of ours.* 4. *He travelled by himself.*

**83.2** 1. *our own* 2. *your own* 3. *her own* 4. *of mine*.
