# Unit 91 — `each` and `every`

## A

Study the instructions:

> Each participant receives a number. Every form must be signed.

`Each` looks at people or things separately, one by one: *Each page has a heading.* It is especially natural for a small number and can be used for two: *Each hand has five fingers.*

`Every` views members as a group, similar to `all`: *Every window was open.* It is common for larger numbers. Use `every`, not `each`, for frequency: *every day*, *every ten minutes*.

## B

Use `each` alone or with `of`: *Each is different.* *Each of the cards has a symbol.* *Each of them was checked.*

Use `every` before a noun. Do not use it alone; say `every one`: *I read every one of the messages.*

## C

`Each` can appear after the subject or object: *The runners were each given a medal.* We also say *€2 each*, meaning “for one person or item”.

## D

`Everyone` (one word) means everybody and is only for people: *Everyone enjoyed the talk.* `Every one` (two words) means every single person or thing: *She answered every one of my questions.*

## Exercises

### 91.1

Choose `each` or `every`.

1. ___ of the two doors has a different code.
2. The bus comes ___ twenty minutes.
3. I checked ___ item on the long list.
4. The players were ___ given a water bottle.
5. ___ child in the class has a locker.

### 91.2

Choose `everyone` or `every one`.

1. ___ in the office received the message.
2. I opened ten envelopes and read ___ .
3. She invited all her neighbours, and ___ came.
4. He solved ___ of the puzzles.
