# Unit 69 — Countable and uncountable nouns: basic contrast

## A

Study this shopping list:

> We need three tomatoes, a loaf of bread, some rice, and some water.

Countable nouns have singular and plural forms: *one tomato, three tomatoes*. We can use a number with them. A singular countable noun needs a determiner: *a tomato*, *the tomato*, *my tomato* (not *I bought tomato*).

Uncountable nouns describe material, substance, or an idea as a mass: *rice, water, music, money, luck, furniture*. They normally have no plural and do not take `a/an`: *We need water* (not *a water*).

## B

To count an uncountable noun, use a container, unit, or piece: *a bottle of water*, *two bowls of rice*, *a piece of furniture*, *three songs*. The countable word, not the uncountable noun, becomes plural: *two bottles of water*.

Plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns can both stand alone in general statements: *Cars cause pollution.* *Music helps me concentrate.*

## C

Use `some/any` with both plural countables and uncountables: *some messages / some advice*; *any questions / any information*. Use `many/few` with plural countables and `much/little` with uncountables: *few chairs* but *little space*.

## Exercises

### 69.1

Classify each noun as countable or uncountable, then complete the phrase.

1. `umbrella`: ___ umbrella / two ___
2. `equipment`: some ___ / a piece of ___
3. `coin`: a ___ / several ___
4. `traffic`: much ___ / a lot of ___

### 69.2

Choose the correct word.

1. We do not have (`many` / `much`) chairs.
2. Could I have (`a` / `some`) information about the course?
3. She bought two (`loaves` / `breads`) of bread.
4. There is (`a` / `some`) sand in my shoes.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**69.1** 1. *an; umbrellas* (countable) 2. *equipment; equipment* (uncountable) 3. *coin; coins* (countable) 4. *traffic; traffic* (uncountable).

**69.2** 1. *many* 2. *some* 3. *loaves* 4. *some*.
