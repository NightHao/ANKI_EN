# Unit 52 — Question tags

## A

At the end of a training day, one colleague says:

> “The session was useful, wasn’t it?”

`Wasn’t it?` is a question tag: a mini-question added after a statement. Use the statement’s auxiliary and normally reverse the polarity:

> You’ve met Noor, haven’t you?  
> The bus isn’t late, is it?  
> They can join us, can’t they?

With present or past simple, use `do/does/did`:

> Your brother lives nearby, doesn’t he?  
> You didn’t call them, did you?

## B

Voice changes the purpose. With a falling voice, a tag usually invites agreement:

> It’s a beautiful view, isn’t it?  
> The food was expensive, wasn’t it?

With a rising voice, it is a real check:

> You haven’t seen my keys, have you?

After a negative statement, `yes` confirms the positive fact and `no` confirms the negative fact:

> “You aren’t leaving yet, are you?” — “Yes, I am.” / “No, I’m not.”

## C

Negative statement + positive tag can make a request or ask for information politely:

> You couldn’t give me a hand, could you?  
> You don’t know the Wi-Fi password, do you?

Some fixed patterns are worth learning whole:

> Let’s take a taxi, shall we?  
> Don’t forget your passport, will you?  
> I’m next, aren’t I?

## Exercises

### 52.1

Add a tag.

1. The restaurant is open today, ___?  
2. You weren’t at the meeting, ___?  
3. Let’s sit outside, ___?

### 52.2

Choose the best reply.

“You haven’t paid yet, have you?”  
a. Yes, I haven’t.  b. No, I haven’t.

### 52.3

Ask a colleague politely to help move a box. Start: `You couldn’t ...`
