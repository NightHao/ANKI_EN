# Unit 51 — Auxiliary verbs, short responses, and `I think so`

## A

Two colleagues are leaving late:

> “Have you saved the file?”  
> “Yes, I have.”

`Have` replaces `have saved the file`. We use an auxiliary when repeating the whole verb phrase would be unnecessary:

> I wasn’t worried, but Maya was.  
> Theo can drive, but I can’t.  
> The new café will open soon, and the library will too.

Present and past simple need `do/does/did`:

> Do you enjoy hiking? — Yes, I do.  
> She lived here once, but she doesn’t now.

We can also correct someone with an auxiliary:

> “You didn’t reply.” — “Yes, I did.”

## B

A short auxiliary question shows interest or surprise:

> “I’ve just changed jobs.” — “Have you? How is it?”  
> “It rained all weekend.” — “Did it? That’s unlucky.”

For agreement, put the auxiliary before the subject:

> “I’m exhausted.” — “So am I.”  
> “I don’t eat meat.” — “Neither do I.”  
> “I can’t swim.” — “Nor can my brother.”

We do not say `So I am` for agreement; that word order has a different emphasis.

## C

`I think so`, `I suppose so`, `I expect so`, `I hope so`, and `I’m afraid so` replace a likely positive clause:

> “Will the parcel arrive today?” — “I think so.”

Negative forms differ:

> I don’t think so. / I don’t expect so.  
> I hope not. / I’m afraid not.  
> I suppose not. / I guess not.

We do not normally say `I don’t hope so` for this meaning.

## Exercises

### 51.1

Complete with an auxiliary.

1. I haven’t been to Seoul, but my sister ___.  
2. “You never listen.” — “Yes, I ___!”  
3. I usually take the bus, but yesterday I ___ not.

### 51.2

Respond naturally.

1. “I’m starting a course next week.” (show interest)  
2. “Do you think it will snow?” (you do not want snow)
