# Chapter 4 — Passive and reported meaning

## Use it when

The action, result, or information matters more than the person who did it—or when you need to relay what someone said without pretending the exact words are yours.

## Core contrast

Use the **active** voice when the actor is useful and direct: “The council closed the road.” Use the **passive** when the result is the topic, the actor is unknown, or naming the actor adds nothing: “The road was closed for repairs.” Make a passive with **be + past participle**, keeping the relevant tense: *is produced, was repaired, has been announced, will be replaced*.

Do not hide responsibility behind the passive. “Mistakes were made” is grammatically possible but vague. If accountability matters, say who acted: “The company made an error.”

Use **get + past participle** mainly for informal events or changes, often unexpected: “My phone got stolen.” For formal explanations, standard *be* passive is safer: “My phone was stolen.”

With reporting, choose between direct words and a report of meaning. Direct: Ana said, “I’m tired.” Reported: “Ana said (that) she was tired.” When the report is later, a tense often moves one step back: *am → was; will → would; have done → had done*. This is called backshifting, but it is not automatic. If the fact remains current, keep it current: “The guide said that the museum is closed on Mondays.”

For requests and advice, report the action pattern: “He asked me to wait”; “She told us not to worry”; “My tutor suggested practising aloud.” *Say* usually does not take a person directly (*say to me*), while *tell* normally does (*tell me*).

## Natural examples

- “Most payments are made by card now, so I rarely carry cash.”
- “The apartment was renovated last year, but the original wooden floor was kept.”
- “I got invited to a small networking event through a colleague.”
- “My manager said that the deadline had moved to Friday.”
- “She told me she’d call once she had spoken to the supplier.”
- “The doctor advised me to take a proper break, not just work from the sofa.”

In IELTS answers, passive voice can make a general process concise: “A lot of waste is produced by packaging.” Then add an active agent if your point needs responsibility: “Manufacturers could reduce it by changing the design.”

## Common failure

**Building a passive without *be*.**

> “The new station completed last year.”

Say: “The new station was completed last year.” If it is an adjective-like state, use *be* too: “I’m interested in architecture.”

**Reporting with the wrong verb pattern.**

> “She said me to wait.”

Say: “She told me to wait,” or “She said that I should wait.”

**Backshifting a permanent fact into something no longer true.** Keep the present where it preserves meaning: “Our teacher explained that water boils at 100°C.”

## Say it yourself

1. Describe how something in your daily life is made, delivered, or organised. Use two passives and name the actor once if it matters.
2. Retell a useful piece of advice someone gave you using *told*, *asked*, or *advised*.
3. Report a recent announcement. Decide whether the reported fact is still true, then choose the tense deliberately.
4. Turn “Someone has cancelled my appointment” into an active and a passive sentence. Which one fits a phone call to the clinic?
