# Unit 45 — `It is said that ...` and `he is said to ...`

## A

Study this report:

> “It is reported that the tunnel is unsafe.”  
> “The tunnel is reported to be unsafe.”

Both forms report what people say without presenting it as the speaker’s own knowledge. We can use verbs such as `said`, `believed`, `known`, `reported`, `thought`, `understood`, `alleged`, and `expected`.

The second pattern is `subject + is/are + reporting verb + to + infinitive`: *The singer is believed to live abroad.* / *The ferry is expected to arrive late.*

## B

Use the infinitive that matches the reported time: *The hikers are believed to be waiting near the summit.* / *The driver is alleged to have ignored the warning.* / *Three people are reported to have been injured.*

We can also use `there`: *There is said to be a private tunnel below the house.*

## C

`Supposed to` can mean “people say”: *That restaurant is supposed to be excellent.* / *The painting is supposed to have been found in Italy.*

## D

It can also mean intended, arranged, expected, or allowed: *You are supposed to be on holiday this week.* / *The guests were supposed to arrive at eight.* `Not supposed to` means not allowed or not advised: *You are not supposed to park here.*

## Exercises

### 45.1

Rewrite using a subject-reporting passive.

1. It is believed that the missing dog is near the river.
2. It is reported that the factory closed last month.
3. It is expected that the train will arrive soon.

### 45.2

Complete with `supposed to`.

1. This app ___ (be) easy to use.
2. I ___ (meet) Jo at six, but she did not come.
3. You ___ (not / leave) bags in this corridor.

### 45.3

Choose the correct form.

1. The actor is said **(to be living / to have lived)** in Berlin now.
2. The sculpture is believed **(to be made / to have been made)** in the 1700s.
3. There is reported **(to be / being)** a power cut nearby.
