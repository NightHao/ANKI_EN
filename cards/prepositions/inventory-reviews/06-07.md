# Phase 2 review — 06 Adjective and Participle + Preposition / 07 Noun + Preposition

**Scope reviewed:** `inventory-slices/06-07.tsv` and its integrated proposed
rows in `coverage.tsv` only. No card source or import TSV was generated.

**Evidence used for disputed AmE forms:** [Cambridge: disappointed](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/disappointed), [Cambridge: opinion](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/opinion), [Cambridge: access](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/access), [Cambridge Grammar: different](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/different-from-different-to-or-different-than), and [Merriam-Webster: based on/off](https://www.merriam-webster.com/words-at-play/based-on-vs-based-off). Cambridge also gives `control over` as a standard form.

## Blocking

None. The slice has the required schema and both assigned source filenames.

## Major

### M1 — Overlapping certainty pairs cannot be fair `contrast` cards

**Items:** `adj-009`, `adj-010`, `adj-011`, `adj-012`.

`certain of/about` and `sure of/about` overlap substantially with noun phrases and
questions. The rows themselves acknowledge that overlap, but two reciprocal
`contrast` cards would require a distinction that ordinary AmE does not reliably
make. This would teach acceptable answers as wrong and duplicates the decision.

**Action:**

- **Exclude** `adj-009` and `adj-011`.
- **Edit** `adj-010` to:
  - `target_form`: `certain about + noun/wh-clause`
  - `meaning_or_distinction`: `confident about a matter, decision, or fact`
  - `construction`: `be certain about + noun/wh-clause`
  - `confusables`: `certain of; certain to + verb`
  - `card_type`: `chunk`
  - `notes`: `Certain of is also standard, especially for a specific fact; do not frame it as wrong.`
- **Edit** `adj-012` analogously: `target_form` `sure about + noun/wh-clause`; `card_type` `chunk`; note that `sure of` is also standard and must not be silently rejected.

### M2 — Accepted variants are incorrectly framed as exclusive contrasts

**Items:** `adj-032`, `adj-033`, `adj-037`, `adj-044`, `adj-045`, `adj-046`, `adj-047`.

`different than` is common in AmE, although `different from` remains the most
frequent general form. `based off` is a newer, less formal AmE alternative, not
an error. Cambridge lists both `disappointed in` and `disappointed with` for
people and things, and lists multiple reactions with `at/by`. The current
`contrast` labels would create unfair one-answer cards.

**Action:**

- **Edit** `adj-032`: `card_type` `chunk`; set `notes` to `Use different from as the neutral default. Different than is also common in AmE and is especially useful before a clause.`
- **Exclude** `adj-033`; it is an accepted alternative, not a separate retrieval decision.
- **Edit** `adj-037`: `card_type` `chunk`; `confusables` `based off / based off of`; `notes` `Based on is the neutral and formal default. Based off and based off of are newer, informal AmE alternatives with the same meaning.`
- **Edit** `adj-044` to `target_form` `disappointed in/with + noun`; `meaning_or_distinction` `unhappy because a person, thing, or result did not meet expectations`; `construction` `be disappointed in/with + noun`; `card_type` `chunk`; `notes` `Both are accepted. Use a later card context only if it can teach a real intended nuance.`
- **Edit** `adj-045`, `adj-046`, and `adj-047`: change `card_type` from `contrast` to `chunk`; retain their combined `at/by` target forms and state in `notes` that both forms are accepted, with `by` often foregrounding the cause.

### M3 — The anger rows duplicate and overstate a non-exclusive distinction

**Items:** `adj-048`, `adj-049`, `adj-050`, `adj-051`, `adj-052`.

`angry with` and `angry at` can both take a person; `angry at` can also refer to
an act. `annoyed at` is another normal alternative. Five reciprocal `contrast`
items would create several cards for the same family while implying false
boundaries.

**Action:**

- **Edit** `adj-048` to `target_form` `angry about + issue/action`; `construction` `be angry about + noun/V-ing`; `card_type` `chunk`; `notes` `Use for the issue or action that causes anger.`
- **Edit** `adj-049` to `target_form` `angry with/at + person`; `construction` `be angry with/at + person`; `card_type` `chunk`; `notes` `Both are normal in AmE; with is especially common for an interpersonal conflict.`
- **Exclude** `adj-050` as covered by the revised `adj-049` and `adj-048`.
- **Edit** `adj-051` to `target_form` `annoyed with/at + person`; `construction` `be annoyed with/at + person`; `card_type` `chunk`; `notes` `Both are accepted for a person.`
- **Edit** `adj-052` to `target_form` `annoyed about + issue/action`; `construction` `be annoyed about + noun/V-ing`; `card_type` `chunk`.

### M5 — Infinitival `to` has been routed as a noun-preposition decision

**Items:** `noun-024`, `noun-026`.

In `opportunity to + verb` and `ability to + verb`, *to* marks an infinitive,
not a preposition. The plan gives prepositional versus infinitival *to* a
specific home in 09. Leaving these in 07 breaks one-home routing and makes the
subdeck label misleading.

**Action: reroute** both to `09 No Preposition and Grammar Traps`:

| Current | Replacement `item_id` | `target_form` | `meaning_or_distinction` | `construction` | `confusables` | `card_type` | `source_file` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `noun-024` | `TRAP-OPPORTUNITY-TO-INF` | `opportunity to + verb` | `To is infinitival after opportunity when it names the action possible.` | `opportunity to + base verb` | `opportunity for + noun/V-ing` | `contrast` | `09-no-preposition-grammar-traps.md` |
| `noun-026` | `TRAP-ABILITY-TO-INF` | `ability to + verb` | `To is infinitival after ability when it names the action possible.` | `ability to + base verb` | `ability for + noun` | `repair` | `09-no-preposition-grammar-traps.md` |

For both: set `subdeck` to `09 No Preposition and Grammar Traps`, `register` to
`neutral`, `card_id` empty, `status` `proposed`, and note `Infinitival to, not
a noun + preposition combination.`

### M6 — Opinion and control rows need a real context boundary, not a categorical contrast

**Items:** `noun-028`, `noun-029`, `noun-051`, `noun-052`.

Cambridge records both *opinion about* and *opinion on*. The slice omits the
very common *opinion about* while giving `of` and `on` reciprocal contrast
cards. Likewise, *control of* and *control over* overlap; the latter commonly
focuses on power over a person, outcome, or variable, while the former is very
common for management or possession of an organization or system. Neither pair
supports a bare one-answer contrast.

**Action:**

- **Edit** `noun-028` to `target_form` `opinion of + person/thing`; `meaning_or_distinction` `a judgment of a person or thing`; `construction` `(a/the) opinion of + noun`; `card_type` `chunk`; `confusables` `opinion about/on + issue`; `notes` `Use of for the person or thing evaluated.`
- **Edit** `noun-029` to `target_form` `opinion about/on + issue`; `meaning_or_distinction` `a view about a topic or issue`; `construction` `(a/the) opinion about/on + noun`; `card_type` `chunk`; `confusables` `opinion of + person/thing`; `notes` `About and on are both accepted; on is common in issue-focused formal contexts.`
- **Edit** `noun-051`: keep `control over + outcome/person`; change `meaning_or_distinction` to `power to direct or influence an outcome or person`; `construction` `control over + noun`; `card_type` `chunk`; `notes` `Control of is also possible in some contexts but is not the default target here.`
- **Edit** `noun-052`: keep `control of + organization/system`; change `meaning_or_distinction` to `management or possession of an organization, territory, or system`; `construction` `control of + noun`; `card_type` `chunk`; `notes` `Context must establish management/possession; do not present of and over as strict opposites.`

### M7 — `access for` is not a productive core noun pattern

**Item:** `noun-054`.

Contemporary learner and business dictionary evidence consistently gives *access
to* for the object or right obtained. Phrases such as *access for visitors* are
possible only in narrow institutional wording and do not provide a stable
general-English contrast to *access to*.

**Action: exclude** `noun-054`. Preserve `noun-023` `access to` as the only core
access item in this subdeck.
