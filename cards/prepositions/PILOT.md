# AmE Preposition Deck — Phase 3 Pilot

**Review status:** Accepted by the learner on 2026-07-30.

These 24 cards are the approved style baseline, not canonical card sources and
not an Anki import. They sample all nine subdecks and all four card types for
full-deck authoring.

The metadata shown above each card is for review only. An imported card will
show exactly two fields: Front and Back.

## 01 Time

### Pilot 01

`TIME-BY-DEADLINE` · contrast · neutral

**Front**

1. The research team must submit its revised proposal ___ 6:00 p.m. Friday.
   (completion deadline)
2. The technical support desk will remain open ___ 6:00 p.m. Friday.
   (continuation)

**Back**

1. The research team must submit its revised proposal **by 6:00 p.m. Friday**.
2. The technical support desk will remain open **until 6:00 p.m. Friday**.

**by** means no later than the endpoint. **until** means that an action or
state continues up to the endpoint.

---

### Pilot 02

`TIME-IN-TIME` · contrast · neutral

**Front**

1. Despite the storm, the flight departed ___, exactly as scheduled.
2. We reached the gate just ___ to board before the doors closed.

**Back**

1. Despite the storm, the flight departed **on time**, exactly as scheduled.
2. We reached the gate just **in time** to board before the doors closed.

**on time** = at the scheduled time.  
**in time to + verb** = early enough for the action still to be possible.

---

### Pilot 03

`TIME-NO-NEXT` · repair · neutral/formal

**Front**

Fix if wrong:

The revised data-protection policy will take effect next month.

**Back**

**Correct as written.**

The revised data-protection policy will take effect **next month**.

Use no preposition before **next + time noun**: `next week`, `next quarter`,
`next summer`.

## 02 Place

### Pilot 04

`PLACE-ON-FLOOR` · context · neutral

**Front**

The neuroscience laboratory is located ___ the seventh floor of the medical
sciences building.

**Back**

The neuroscience laboratory is located **on the seventh floor** of the medical
sciences building.

Use **on** for a particular floor or level of a building.

---

### Pilot 05

`PLACE-AT-INTERSECTION` · context · neutral

**Front**

The new public health clinic stands ___ the intersection of Grant Avenue and
Pine Street.

**Back**

The new public health clinic stands **at the intersection of Grant Avenue and
Pine Street**.

Use **at** when an intersection is treated as an exact point.

---

### Pilot 06

`PLACE-AT-ADDRESS` · context · neutral

**Front**

The orientation session will be held ___ 425 Lexington Avenue.

**Back**

The orientation session will be held **at 425 Lexington Avenue**.

Use **at** before a complete numbered address. Compare **on Lexington Avenue**
when only the street is named.

## 03 Movement

### Pilot 07

`MOVE-ARRIVE-AT-IN` · contrast · neutral

**Front**

1. The delegation arrived ___ Singapore late Tuesday.
2. They arrived ___ the research campus shortly before the opening ceremony.

**Back**

1. The delegation arrived **in Singapore** late Tuesday.
2. They arrived **at the research campus** shortly before the opening
   ceremony.

Use **arrive in** with a city, country, or other large area. Use **arrive at**
with a point, building, venue, or smaller destination.

---

### Pilot 08

`MOVE-GO-HOME` · repair · neutral

**Front**

Fix if wrong:

After the final presentation, most participants went to home to prepare for
the next day's workshop.

**Back**

After the final presentation, most participants **went home** to prepare for
the next day's workshop.

When **home** is a destination adverb, use `go/come/get/drive/walk home`
without **to**.

---

### Pilot 09

`MOVE-THROUGH` · contrast · neutral

**Front**

1. We walked ___ the open courtyard to reach the library.
2. We passed ___ a narrow covered passageway and emerged on the other side of
   the old building.

**Back**

1. We walked **across the open courtyard**.
2. We passed **through a narrow covered passageway**.

Use **across** for movement from one side of an open area or surface to the
other. Use **through** for movement within an enclosed or bounded space.

## 04 Abstract Relationships

### Pilot 10

`AR-AGENT-BY` · contrast · neutral/formal

**Front**

The encrypted archive was opened ___ an external auditor ___ a temporary
access key.

**Back**

The encrypted archive was opened **by an external auditor with a temporary
access key**.

In a passive clause, **by** names the agent who performs the action. **with**
names the tool or instrument used. The one contrast here is **agent by** versus
**instrument with**.

---

### Pilot 11

`AR-PURPOSE-FOR` · contrast · neutral/formal

**Front**

1. This dashboard is used ___ tracking real-time emissions.
2. Analysts open it ___ identify unusual patterns in the data.

**Back**

1. This dashboard is used **for tracking** real-time emissions.
2. Analysts open it **to identify** unusual patterns in the data.

Use **for + V-ing** for the function of a thing. Use **to + base verb** for
the purpose of an agent's action. The second **to** is an infinitive marker,
not a preposition.

---

### Pilot 12

`AR-CONCESSION-DESPITE` · contrast · neutral/formal

**Front**

1. ___ strong objections from residents, the council approved the
   redevelopment plan.
2. ___ several residents objected, the council approved the redevelopment
   plan.

**Back**

1. **Despite strong objections** from residents, the council approved the
   redevelopment plan.
2. **Although several residents objected**, the council approved the
   redevelopment plan.

Use **despite + noun/V-ing**. Use **although + clause** with its own subject
and verb.

**Also OK for sentence 1:** **In spite of strong objections**.

## 05 Verb + Preposition

### Pilot 13

`VP-RESULT-IN` · contrast · neutral/formal

**Front**

1. Prolonged underinvestment can result ___ serious infrastructure failures.
2. Many of the current delays result ___ an outdated procurement process.

**Back**

1. Prolonged underinvestment can **result in** serious infrastructure
   failures.
2. Many of the current delays **result from** an outdated procurement
   process.

**result in** points from a cause to its outcome. **result from** points from
an outcome back to its cause.

---

### Pilot 14

`VP-ACCOUNT-FOR-SHARE` · chunk · neutral/formal

**Front**

`account ___ + percentage/share + of + noun`

**Back**

`account for + percentage/share + of + noun`

Here, **account for** means constitute or represent a stated proportion.

- Renewable sources now **account for 42 percent of** the region's
  electricity generation.
- International students **account for nearly a third of** enrollment in the
  program.
- Two suppliers **account for the majority of** the company's semiconductor
  purchases.

---

### Pilot 15

`VP-PROTECT-FROM` · chunk · neutral

**Front**

`protect + person/thing ___ + harm or an unwanted result`

**Back**

`protect + person/thing from + harm or an unwanted result`

Use **protect ... from** for keeping someone or something safe from harm or
preventing an unwanted effect.

- Stronger labor laws can **protect temporary workers from exploitation**.
- The coating **protects the equipment from being damaged** by salt water.
- Regular backups **protect the organization from permanent data loss**.

**Also OK:** **protect ... against** is natural with many threats, especially
risks, attacks, and diseases. Do not treat the two forms as strict opposites.

## 06 Adjective and Participle + Preposition

### Pilot 16

`ADJ-032` · chunk · neutral

**Front**

`different ___ + noun` (neutral default)

**Back**

`different from + noun`

Use **different from** as the neutral general-purpose form.

- The revised policy is substantially **different from the original
  proposal**.
- Remote collaboration is **different from working in the same office**.
- The outcome was **different from what the researchers had predicted**.

**Also OK in AmE:** **different than** is common, especially before a clause:
`The outcome was different than we had predicted.`

---

### Pilot 17

`ADJ-021` · chunk · neutral/formal

**Front**

`relevant ___ + topic, decision, problem, or situation`

**Back**

`relevant to + topic, decision, problem, or situation`

Use **relevant to** when information or a consideration is directly connected
to the matter being discussed.

- The committee excluded evidence that was not **relevant to the current
  investigation**.
- Cost remains highly **relevant to the decision** about whether to expand the
  service.
- Please include only the technical details **relevant to diagnosing the
  failure**.

## 07 Noun + Preposition

### Pilot 18

`NOUN-030` · chunk · neutral/formal

**Front**

`confidence ___ + person, institution, ability, or outcome`

**Back**

`confidence in + person, institution, ability, or outcome`

Use **confidence in** for the person, institution, ability, or result that is
trusted.

- Public **confidence in local government** has declined.
- She has complete **confidence in her ability to lead** the negotiations.
- Early clinical results increased investors' **confidence in the treatment's
  long-term potential**.

---

### Pilot 19

`NOUN-013` · contrast · neutral/formal

**Front**

Applications rose from 20,000 to 25,000.

1. The university reported a 25 percent increase ___ applications.
2. This represents an increase ___ 5,000 applications.

**Back**

1. The university reported a 25 percent **increase in applications**.
2. This represents an **increase of 5,000 applications**.

**increase in + variable** names what changed.  
**increase of + amount** names the size of the change.

## 08 Fixed and Multiword Expressions

### Pilot 20

`FIXED-CAUSE-BECAUSE-VS-BECAUSE-OF` · contrast · neutral

**Front**

1. The outdoor session was canceled ___ the air-quality warning.
2. The outdoor session was canceled ___ air-quality levels had become unsafe.

**Back**

1. The outdoor session was canceled **because of the air-quality warning**.
2. The outdoor session was canceled **because air-quality levels had become
   unsafe**.

Use **because of + noun/pronoun/V-ing**. Use **because + clause** with its own
subject and verb.

---

### Pilot 21

`FIXED-STANCE-FROM-MY-PERSPECTIVE` · chunk · neutral/formal

**Front**

`___ my perspective`

**Back**

`from my perspective`

Use this frame to evaluate an issue from a person's, group's, or discipline's
viewpoint.

- **From my perspective**, the revised timeline is ambitious but realistic.
- **From the regulator's perspective**, transparency matters as much as
  efficiency.
- **From an environmental perspective**, renovating the building may be
  preferable to replacing it.

For a current personal stance, prefer **from my perspective** or **in my
view** to **according to me**. The broader pattern also permits a possessive
or another determiner: `from the regulator's perspective`, `from an
environmental perspective`.

## 09 No Preposition and Grammar Traps

### Pilot 22

`TRAP-DIRECT-DISCUSS` · repair · neutral/formal

**Front**

Fix if wrong:

The panel discussed about several alternatives before recommending a change
to the admissions policy.

**Back**

The panel **discussed several alternatives** before recommending a change to
the admissions policy.

**discuss + direct object** takes no **about**. Compare **talk about several
alternatives**, where **about** is required.

---

### Pilot 23

`TRAP-QUESTION-STRANDED-PREPOSITION` · contrast · conversational/formal

**Front**

Complete both questions:

1. Who were you speaking ___ after the conference? (ordinary conversation)
2. ___ whom should the formal complaint be addressed? (formal writing)

**Back**

1. Who were you speaking **to** after the conference?
2. **To** whom should the formal complaint be addressed?

Ordinary English commonly leaves the preposition at the end of a question.
Formal writing may place it before **whom**. A sentence-final preposition is
not an error merely because it is final.

---

### Pilot 24

`TRAP-TO-USED-TO-VS-BE-USED-TO` · contrast · neutral

**Front**

1. Before relocating, Elena used to ___ to the office five days a week.
   (past habit)
2. She is now used to ___ with colleagues across several time zones.
   (familiarity)

**Back**

1. Elena **used to commute** to the office five days a week.
2. She is now **used to collaborating** with colleagues across several time
   zones.

**used to + base verb** describes a past habit or state.  
**be/get used to + noun/V-ing** describes familiarity. In the second pattern,
**to** is a preposition, so a following verb takes the **-ing** form.
