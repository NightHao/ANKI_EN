## Must-fix

### M1 — C09-041 teaches a second, separately inventoried decision

**Card/item:** `C09-041` / `CMP-TOO-MUCH-NOUN-TO-INF`.

The Front/Back contrasts **too much** with **not enough**. The card's item is
the `too much + mass noun + to-inf` construction, while the second side is an
independent enough/insufficiency decision already covered by
`CMP-ENOUGH-NOUN-TO-INF` (`C09-046`). Replace sentence 2 with a count-noun
`too many` contrast only if the inventory is intentionally a count/mass
contrast, or better make this a bounded chunk/context for *too much background
noise to conduct a reliable hearing*. Do not use `not enough` as its paired
answer.

### M2 — C09-042 duplicates the count/mass contrast instead of isolating its item

**Card/item:** `C09-042` / `CMP-TOO-MANY-NOUN-TO-INF`.

This card contrasts **too many** and **too much**, duplicating the mass/count
decision that M1 should not teach. Make it a `too many + plural count noun +
to-inf` context/chunk with a clear consequence, or intentionally merge the
two inventory items before validation. As written, C09-041/042 jointly spend
two cards on one decision and still leave neither item atomic.

### M3 — C09-065 has an explicitly open blank

**Card/item:** `C09-065` / `CMP-COMPARED-WITH-TO`.

The Front's blank accepts both **Compared with** and **Compared to**, and the
Back says both are standard. That violates the closed-answer rule. Change the
Front to recognition/retrieval of the completed paired frame, for example:

> `Compared with/to + explicit baseline, main clause`  
> Which two accepted forms can frame this analytical comparison?

Or print two completed alternatives on the Front and ask for the register/use
note. Do not retain a single blank that gives one answer while declaring two
correct.

### M4 — C09-064’s explanation overstates a rule

**Card/item:** `C09-064` / `CMP-AS-OPPOSED-TO`.

The pattern is right, but “the **to** is a preposition, so a verb after it
takes **-ing**” is incomplete in a way that encourages a false ban: the
construction can also contrast NPs and other parallel units. Replace with:

> Use `as opposed to + NP/V-ing`; use V-ing when the contrasted alternatives
> are actions.

The existing two sentences are good and already close the answer space.

## Should-fix

- **C09-003 / `CMP-LESS-THAN-NP`:** sentence 2 teaches countability, while
  C09-017 and C09-018 already do that more directly. Keep the first adjective
  sentence only as `less + adjective + than`, or explicitly label this card
  as a two-part adjective/count contrast and remove duplicate teaching later.
- **C09-009 / `CMP-COMPARATIVE-MUCH-FAR`:** the Front says “very or much” but
  the Back introduces **far** as another valid comparative intensifier. Add
  `Also OK: far more accurate` in a suitable rewritten sentence, or change
  the explanation to state that this particular choice is much versus very,
  while far is another available comparative intensifier.
- **C09-013 / `CMP-COMPARATIVE-TIMES`:** excellent precise-ratio choice;
  retain the usage warning, but phrase it as “can be interpreted differently”
  rather than implying *four times faster than* is always unusable.
- **C09-016 / `CMP-DOUBLE-COMPARATIVE`:** correct repair and grammar. Add a
  short line that *more quickly* is valid with the adverb **quickly**, so the
  learner does not generalize the repair to all `more + word` forms.
- **C09-025 / `CMP-NOT-SO-AS`:** keep only as a recognition/formal-option
  context. Its AmE status is more formal/less common than `not as ... as`; do
  not later convert it to a bare production blank.
- **C09-038–040 and C09-044–047:** the subject-control distinctions are
  mostly good. In final copy, state that `for + NP` names a separate infinitive
  subject; do not say it is merely “affected” when it is actually the logical
  performer. C09-040 and C09-047 already demonstrate this well.
- **C09-043 / `CMP-TOO-NOT-DOUBLE-NEGATIVE`:** the intended meaning and repair
  are sound. Keep the special positive meaning of *too careful not to verify*
as a completed comparison, never a competing blank.
- **C09-062/063:** both responsibly acknowledge overlap. The printed contexts
  are closed because only **point** is blank; preserve that design and do not
  make `that/where` the blank in later revisions.
- **C09-068/069:** strong pragmatic contexts. Retain the final caveat that
  `no/not more/less than` can overlap if context does not fix minimizing or
  emphatic force; do not turn them into correction cards.
