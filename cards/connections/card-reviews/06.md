## Verdict

**Do not validate Module 06 yet.** The grammar core is strong and generally
natural contemporary AmE, and source-to-inventory mapping is exact. However,
**23 of 41 current cards have a blocking retrieval, closure, or duplication
defect**. The largest problems are:

- many Fronts print the requested marker or even the complete reduced form;
- `C06-002`/`C06-037` and `C06-016`/`C06-041` repeat the same decisions;
- the neutral `when` card omits normal defining `that` and zero alternatives;
- the `reason why` card omits formal `reason for which`;
- adjective-phrase and prepositional-phrase noun postmodifiers have no
  inventory item or card; and
- the infinitive cards show subject-control and object-gap readings separately
  but never teach the active/passive interpretation decision explicitly.

The module should remain proposed until the items below are repaired and
independently re-audited.

## Must fix before validation

### M1 — C06-002 / REL-002 and C06-037 / REL-040: exact duplicate decision

Both cards contrast a selected subset with an already identified whole group
and retrieve the same comma/no-comma reference decision. The analyst and
consultant settings do not create different knowledge.

Retain one item/card. A compact closed Front can preserve both meanings:

> Punctuate each sentence so the relative clause matches the stated group.
> 1. Of twelve consultants, only the five licensed consultants may sign.
> 2. The firm has five consultants; all five are licensed and may sign.

The Back should retain the two completed sentences and explicitly say that the
commas encode a reference distinction, not a pause preference.

### M2 — C06-016 / REL-016 and C06-041 / REL-044: exact duplicate decision

Both cards retrieve a defining people-relative with neutral preposition
stranding (`who ... with/to`) versus formal fronting (`with/to whom ...`).
Changing **with** to **to** does not make a new atomic decision.

Retain one defining card with two bounded examples if both prepositions are
useful. Keep `C06-021` separately because its non-defining commas and
prohibition of **that** and zero add a real boundary. Keep `C06-017` only as
the people-versus-things morphology decision after a fronted preposition.

### M3 — C06-004 / REL-004: the Front supplies **which**

“Use **which** as the subject” reveals the answer. Retrieve the
subject-relative marker instead:

> Complete each thing-relative. Do not omit the relative-clause subject.
> 1. The Aurora satellite, ___ monitors coastal erosion, failed overnight.
> 2. Select the sensor ___ detects the smallest pressure changes.

Back: **which** in both; **that** is also standard in sentence 2, but not in
the comma-delimited sentence 1. Zero is impossible because the marker is the
subject.

### M4 — C06-005 / REL-005: the Front supplies **that**

The card currently asks the learner to use the already printed answer.
Replace it with two marker blanks and the cue “defining subject relatives:
one person, one thing.” Back: **that** in both, with **who** and **which**
under `Also OK:`. Retain the non-omission rule.

### M5 — C06-006 / REL-006: the Front gives away the who/whom mapping

“Use **who** in sentence 1 and **whom** in sentence 2” eliminates retrieval.
Ask only for the requested registers:

> Complete the same defining object relative in neutral conversation and in a
> deliberately formal report.

Use blanks for the marker. Back: neutral **who**, formal **whom**, plus
defining-only **that** and zero alternatives. The following subject **we**
must remain visible so the object role is objectively recoverable.

### M6 — C06-007 / REL-007: the Front supplies **which**

Use a bounded recognition/acceptance card rather than “Use **which**.”
For example:

> Complete the defining thing-object relative with every standard neutral
> marker, including omission:
> `the resilience plan ___ local researchers had developed`

Back: **which**, **that**, or zero. This directly aligns with the inventory
item and does not falsely privilege one accepted defining form.

### M7 — C06-010 / REL-010: the Front supplies **whose**

Blank the possessive marker and retain the person-versus-organization pair:

> Complete both possessive relatives with the same marker.
> `the applicant ___ field experience ...`
> `the hospital ___ emergency wing ...`

Back: **whose** in both. This retrieves the useful fact that contemporary
English permits inanimate and organizational antecedents.

### M8 — C06-011 / REL-011: the Front supplies **where**

Replace “Use **where**” with a location cue and a marker blank. The Back may
retain formal **in which** as an accepted alternative. Do not make this a
second neutral/formal contrast; `C06-034` already owns that comparison.

### M9 — C06-012 / REL-012: the Front over-specifies the reduction

“With the preposition stranded/fronted” plus the source facts performs most
of the placement decision. Make the learner choose placement:

> Write a neutral version and a deliberately formal version of the database
> relative. Preserve **in** in both.

Back: `which/that/zero ... in` for neutral defining use and **in which** for
formal fronting. Keep **where** under `Also OK:` but state that it belongs to
the equivalent location construal taught by `C06-034`.

### M10 — C06-013 / REL-013: the Front supplies **when**

Blank the marker instead of saying “Use **when**.” The completed Back must
retain **during which**, **that**, and zero as accepted defining alternatives:
`the winter when/during which/that the river froze` and `the winter the river
froze`. The exact sentence is defining, so all four need acknowledgement.

### M11 — C06-014 / REL-014: the neutral answer space is not closed

For `the date ___ the revised standard took effect`, neutral **when** is not
the only answer: **that** and zero are also standard defining relatives.
“Neutral” does not exclude them.

Print the completed neutral variants and ask which form specifically packages
time as a relative adverb, then contrast it with formal **on which**. Do not
leave a supposedly unique neutral blank.

### M12 — C06-015 / REL-015: the Front supplies **why** and the Back omits
formal **for which**

Blank the marker. The Back should give:

> `the reason why/that the clinics struggle`
> `the reason the clinics struggle`
> formal: `the reason for which the clinics struggle`

Do not force **why** as the sole neutral answer. **Because** remains a
conjunction introducing a reason clause and is not interchangeable inside this
noun phrase.

### M13 — C06-023 / REL-023: the Front prints **all of which**

“Join ... with **all of which**” makes Front and Back repetitions. Replace it
with:

> Join the sentences with one non-defining relative that refers to every
> member of the four-filter set.

Back: **all of which**. Retain the explanation that **all of them** would need
independent-clause punctuation.

### M14 — C06-025 / REL-025 and C06-026 / REL-026: the complete reduced
answers appear on the Front

Each Front prints the exact reduced sentence immediately before asking for
that sentence. Restore retrieval:

> Reduce the bold relative without changing its active meaning:
> `Students who are living abroad may attend online.`

> Reduce the bold passive relative without losing the deadline:
> `Only the documents that were submitted before noon were included.`

Backs: `Students living abroad ...` and `documents submitted before noon ...`.
The active noun must be the understood actor; the passive noun must be the
affected entity.

### M15 — C06-031 / REL-031: the requested pattern is printed verbatim

“Use `the only option + to-infinitive`” reveals the entire target frame.
Replace it with a production cue:

> Combine the facts so a to-infinitive identifies the one option that remained
> available.

Back: `Arbitration was the only option to remain on the table.` Add the more
idiomatic equivalent **the only option remaining on the table** under
`Also OK:` rather than implying that the infinitive is obligatory.

### M16 — C06-038 / REL-041: the Front supplies sentence-relative **which**

The card should retrieve the whole-clause marker:

> Turn the second sentence into a comma-delimited relative comment on the
> entire first statement.
> `The council postponed the vote for a third time. This frustrated local
> residents.`

Back: the current **which frustrated** sentence. State explicitly that
sentence-relative **which**, not **that**, owns this construction.

### M17 — C06-040 / REL-043: the Front prints the complete target construction

“Use the formal `the terms of which` construction” gives away both connector
and internal noun phrase. Ask for a deliberately formal inanimate-possessive
relative instead:

> Combine the statements in one sentence using a formal alternative to
> **whose**.

Back: `The treaty, several of the terms of which ...`. Keep `several of whose
terms` as the lighter accepted form.

### M18 — Missing inventory/card: adjective-phrase postmodifiers

Module 06 has finite, participial, and infinitival modifiers but no productive
adjective-phrase postmodifier. Add one bounded item/card, preferably the
high-value indefinite-pronoun order:

> The review found something ___ enough to justify a second investigation.
> (adjective meaning worthy of attention)

Back: **something significant enough ...**, not `significant something`.
Alternatively use a completed-marker retrieval card for `someone responsible
for + NP`. Do not duplicate adjective-selected complements from Module 03;
the Module 06 decision is the modifier's position after the noun/pronoun.

### M19 — Missing inventory/card: prepositional-phrase postmodifiers

Add one item/card for `noun + PP` as an identifying modifier, such as:

> Which phrase identifies the intended analysts: the analysts generally, or
> only the analysts **in the regional offices**?

Back should teach that `in the regional offices` postmodifies **analysts**.
Keep the decision on noun attachment; do not reteach the lexical or semantic
choice of **in**, which belongs elsewhere.

### M20 — C06-030 / REL-030, C06-032 / REL-032, and C06-033 / REL-033:
infinitive interpretation is incomplete

The three cards exemplify different interpretations but do not teach the
decision:

- `the first person to arrive`: the noun is the infinitive's understood
  subject;
- `someone to ask` / `tasks to finish`: the noun fills an object or
  prepositional gap despite the infinitive's active form; and
- `the next applicant to be interviewed`: an overt passive infinitive makes
  the affected role explicit.

Revise the family into one discriminator plus the existing productive
contexts. Add the passive sentence to `C06-030` or `C06-033`; no additional
card is required. In `C06-032`, explicitly paraphrase **someone to ask** as
“someone whom she can ask,” because the isolated phrase can otherwise also be
read as a person who will ask.

## Should fix

- **C06-001 / REL-001:** the card is accurate, but the completed Back should
  call **which** a fully standard defining alternative in AmE, not merely an
  incidental `Also OK:`. This keeps it consistent with `C06-036`.
- **C06-024 / REL-024:** prefer the tighter repair `The committee interviewed
  the director's adviser, who had reported the fraud.` The current semicolon
  rewrite is grammatical but changes more structure than the attachment
  decision requires.
- **C06-027 / REL-027:** `The bridge currently being repaired ...` is clearer
  and more idiomatic than bare `The bridge being repaired ...`; retain
  **being** only for a genuinely ongoing passive.
- **C06-030 / REL-030:** the explanation says “ordinals, superlatives, and
  words such as first, last, next, and only,” although **first/last/next** are
  themselves ordinal or ordering expressions. Use “ordinals, superlatives,
  and **only**,” then list examples.
- **C06-034 / REL-037:** retain this as the sole `where` versus `in which`
  register comparison; `C06-011` should remain the basic location application
  and `C06-012` the stranding/fronting decision.
- **C06-035 / REL-038 and C06-036 / REL-039:** these acceptance-aware cards
  are sound. Keep both completed alternatives and never turn either into a
  one-answer blank. For contemporary AmE, defining **which** is grammatical
  even when a house style prefers **that**.
- **C06-038 / REL-041:** after M16, add brief productive variants on the Back:
  `which means ...`, `which is why ...`, and `which made it necessary to ...`.
  They remain one decision because **which** comments on the whole preceding
  proposition in every variant.
- **C06-039 / REL-042:** retain separately from `C06-038`; recognition of the
  sentence-relative pattern and repair of non-defining **that** are different
  retrieval actions.
- Across the module, label **fronting** “formal” or “formal written” rather
  than implying that stranding is merely conversational. Preposition stranding
  is standard in neutral contemporary AmE writing as well as speech.

## Authoritative checks used for disputed forms

- [Cambridge, Relative pronouns](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/relative-pronouns/)
  confirms that **that** is defining-only; zero is defining-object-only;
  fronted prepositions take **whom/which**, not **that**; **where/when/why**
  alternate with preposition + **which**; and whole-clause relatives use
  **which**.
- [Merriam-Webster, Which vs. That](https://www.merriam-webster.com/grammar/when-to-use-that-and-which)
  confirms for contemporary AmE that restrictive **which** is grammatical
  even though some editorial styles prefer **that**.
- [Merriam-Webster, Who vs. Whom](https://www.merriam-webster.com/grammar/who-vs-whom-grammar-usage)
  confirms subject **who**, object **whom**, standard neutral object **who**,
  and the special durability of **whom** after a preposition.
- [Cambridge, Whose](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/relative-clauses-whose)
  confirms possessive **whose** with people, animals, and things.
- [Cambridge, Defining and non-defining relative clauses](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/defining-and-non-)
  confirms the reference distinction, required marker in non-defining
  relatives, and prohibition of non-defining **that**.
- [Cambridge, Noun phrases: dependent words](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/noun-phrases-dependent-words)
  identifies PP and clause postmodifiers after a noun head and distinguishes
  them from noun-selected complements.
- [Cambridge, Adjective phrase functions](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/adjective-phrases-functions)
  confirms that adjective phrases follow indefinite pronouns such as
  **someone** and **something**.
- [British Council, Noun phrases](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/noun-phrases)
  independently lists PP, V-ing, relative-clause, and to-infinitive
  postmodifiers, including `something to read`.
