## Must-fix cards

### M1 — C03-002 / ADJ-002: the lexical anchors are missing from the Front

`Maya is ___ the ethics ...` permits *curious about*, *fascinated by*,
*engaged with*, and other normal answers; `The ethical debate is ___ Maya`
likewise permits many adjectives. Keep the target anchors visible and retrieve
only their connection:

> 1. Maya is **interested** ___ the ethics of artificial intelligence.  
> 2. The ethical debate is **interesting** ___ Maya.

The current Back can then remain.

### M2 — C03-003 / ADJ-003: open Front plus duplicated decision

Both blanks omit **concerned**, so *worried about* and *focused on* are also
ordinary completions. Change the Front to:

> 1. Residents are **concerned** ___ whether ...  
> 2. The engineering review is **concerned** ___ the dam's structural
> integrity.

Use this as the sole `concerned about / concerned with` contrast, and merge the
inventory decision currently duplicated by `ADJ-004`.

### M3 — C03-004 / ADJ-004: exact duplicate of C03-003 and also open

This card retrieves the same worry-versus-subject-matter decision as C03-003,
with the adjective again omitted. Consolidate `ADJ-003` and `ADJ-004` into one
contrast item/card and retire C03-004. If both inventory rows must remain,
rewrite C03-004 as a completed recognition/context card for only
`concerned with + subject`; do not test `concerned about` again.

### M4 — C03-005 / ADJ-005: false `for` versus `about` boundary

`concerned for her` and `concerned about her` are both natural for welfare.
The semantic clue therefore does not close the answer, and the card overstates
a distinction. Cambridge explicitly gives **concerned about/for your health**.
Keep **concerned** visible and ask for both accepted forms:

> After three days without contact, her colleagues were deeply **concerned
> about/for her**.

Explain that **for** can foreground sympathetic concern for someone's welfare,
while **about** is also fully normal; do not teach **about** as limited to an
impersonal issue.

### M5 — C03-011 / ADJ-013: open Front plus duplicated decision

`The contractor is ___ inspecting ...` also accepts *tasked with* and
*accountable for*; `is ___ the authority` accepts *accountable to*. Keep
**responsible** printed:

> 1. The contractor is **responsible** ___ inspecting the bridge ...  
> 2. The contractor is **responsible** ___ the regional transport authority.

Use this as the sole `responsible for / responsible to` contrast.

### M6 — C03-012 / ADJ-014: exact duplicate of C03-011 and open

This reverses the same two roles and again removes the anchor. Merge
`ADJ-013`/`ADJ-014` around C03-011 and retire C03-012. If retained, make it a
completed context for `responsible to + authority` only, with **responsible**
visible and no second `responsible for` prompt.

### M7 — C03-022 / ADJ-024: `appropriate to` remains a normal answer

The broad Front includes “situation,” but AmE also uses **appropriate to** for
a matching relation, for example *appropriate to the crime/circumstances*.
Replace the Front with a bounded audience/use prompt:

> `appropriate ___ + intended audience or use`

Keep the two existing **for** examples, then add:

> **Also OK in a different matching frame:** `appropriate to the
> circumstances / appropriate to the crime`.

Do not say or imply that **to** is generally wrong.

### M8 — C03-024 / ADJ-026: duplicate `available to / available for` contrast

C03-023 already retrieves access versus intended use. Merge `ADJ-025` and
`ADJ-026` into that one genuine contrast and retire C03-024. Alternatively,
keep C03-024 only as `available for + appointment/event` and remove its second
access sentence and all repeated `available to` teaching.

### M9 — C03-025 / ADJ-027: common `beneficial for` is omitted

**Beneficial to** is the excellent neutral target, but **beneficial for** is
also common contemporary English with beneficiaries and outcomes. The Front's
person/system/outcome range does not exclude it. Make the task explicit:

> Complete with the neutral default, then name the common alternative:
> `beneficial ___ + recipient`.

Back:

> **beneficial to + recipient**  
> **Also OK:** **beneficial for** is common in the same broad benefit
> contexts; do not force a categorical meaning contrast.

### M10 — C03-026 / ADJ-028: common `harmful for` is omitted

**Harmful to** is the standard target, but *harmful for children/some people*
is common AmE, including current CDC usage. The Front allows either. Use the
same bounded design as M9:

> Complete with the neutral default, then name the common alternative:
> `harmful ___ + affected person/system/environment`.

Back:

> **harmful to + affected target**  
> **Also OK:** **harmful for** is common, especially with affected groups.

### M11 — C03-028 / ADJ-030: duplicate `useful for / useful to` contrast

C03-027 already retrieves purpose versus beneficiary. Merge
`ADJ-029`/`ADJ-030` around one contrast card and retire C03-028. If retained,
make C03-028 a one-decision `useful to + beneficiary` context and remove the
second `useful for` sentence.

### M12 — C03-031 / ADJ-034: accepted `comparable with` is missing

Cambridge gives **comparable to/with** for this exact sense. The Front therefore
has two accepted answers. Replace it with:

> `comparable ___ + item used as a standard`  
> Give both accepted prepositions.

Back:

> **comparable to/with + item**  
> Both are standard; **to** is a natural default in the existing examples.

### M13 — C03-036 / ADJ-039: bad contrast pairing and repeated `independent of`

Dependence and independence are antonyms, not competing complement frames for
one intended meaning, and C03-037 already owns `independent of`. Rewrite
C03-036 as a focused `dependent on + factor/wh-clause` card, retain
`dependent upon` as the formal alternative, and remove sentence 2 plus the
`independent of` explanation. C03-037 can remain the independent item.

### M14 — C03-040 / ADJ-043: `pleased about` is a normal competing completion

The Front's “result, progress, or performance” permits both **pleased with**
and **pleased about**. Cambridge lists **pleased about**, **pleased with**,
**pleased that**, and **pleased to**. Replace the bare chunk with a bounded
paired context:

> 1. The auditors were pleased ___ the quality of the final report.
>    (evaluation)  
> 2. Staff were pleased ___ the announcement of additional leave.
>    (reaction to news)

Teach **with** as the normal evaluated-object frame and **about** for an
event/news, while stating that overlap remains. Do not present the boundary as
absolute.

### M15 — C03-045 / ADJ-048: `angry at/over` also fit an issue or action

Cambridge gives *angry about* but also the natural example *angry at the lack
of parking spaces*. The current Back incorrectly confines **at** to a person.
Keep **about** as the neutral issue frame, but add:

> **Also OK:** **angry at/over + issue/action** can be natural; **angry
> with/at + person** names the target.

The Front should ask for the neutral default plus accepted alternatives, or
use an exact completed contrast rather than a single blank.

### M16 — C03-048 / ADJ-052: `annoyed at/by/over` remain ordinary answers

For situations and actions, **annoyed at**, **annoyed by**, and sometimes
**annoyed over** are normal, so `annoyed ___ + issue, event, or action` is not
closed. Replace it with a genuine cause/target contrast:

> 1. Employees were **annoyed by/at** the last-minute change. (cause)  
> 2. Employees were **annoyed with/at** the manager. (person targeted)

If `about` is retained, label it as one accepted issue frame rather than the
exclusive answer.

### M17 — C03-052 / ADJ-056: the Front does not distinguish `of` from `by`

The Back itself admits **frightened by + cause**, while the Front's “person,
thing, or activity” can denote either an enduring object of fear or an
immediate cause. Turn it into a bounded contrast:

> 1. The child is frightened **of unfamiliar dogs**. (general object of fear)  
> 2. The child was frightened **by a sudden explosion**. (event that caused
> the reaction)

Keep `frightened of + NP/V-ing` versus `frightened by + immediate cause`
explicit, without claiming every context has a sharp boundary.

### M18 — C03-058 / ADJ-062: duplicate gratitude contrast and wrong Back

This duplicates C03-057's `grateful for / grateful to` decision. Worse, Front
2 says *grateful for having received their trust and cooperation*, but Back 2
answers a different sentence: *grateful for being able to learn from their
experiences*. Merge `ADJ-061`/`ADJ-062` around C03-057 and retire C03-058. If
it is retained, the Back must reproduce the exact printed completion:

> It is **grateful for having received their trust and cooperation**.

### M19 — C03-061 / ADJ-065: exact duplicate of the known-for/as decision

C03-060 and C03-061 merely reverse `known for + reason` and
`known as + role`. Merge `ADJ-064`/`ADJ-065` into one contrast item/card and
retire C03-061. If retained, isolate `known as + role/category` and remove the
second `known for` prompt.

### M20 — C03-064 / ADJ-068: the Front blanks the entire likelihood frame

`The bill ___ pass` permits *will probably pass*, *is expected to pass*,
*may pass*, and more. Keep **likely** visible and retrieve only its
complement:

> 1. The bill is **likely** ___ pass with bipartisan support.  
> 2. It is **likely** ___ the bill will pass with bipartisan support.

Use this as the sole `subject + be likely to / It is likely that` contrast.

### M21 — C03-065 / ADJ-069: exact duplicate of C03-064 and open

This repeats the same two likelihood frames in reverse order and again blanks
the target construction. Merge `ADJ-068`/`ADJ-069` around the corrected

print **likely**, and do not ask for the raised-subject counterpart again.

### M22 — C03-069 / ADJ-073: exact duplicate of C03-051

### M23 — C03-070 / ADJ-074: exact duplicate of C03-053

## Module-level coverage blockers

These have no current card or inventory ID, so they are not part of the 24
card-level defects above.

1. **Add `liable to + NP/verb` or an explicit `likely to / liable to`
   decision.** Contemporary references treat probabilistic **liable to** as
   likelihood/tendency, commonly with an adverse outcome (*liable to make
   matters worse; liable to flooding*). A useful new contrast should teach
   **likely to** as general probability and **liable to** as risk/tendency,
   without claiming that every use of **liable** is negative. Legal
   `liable for + damages/costs` is a separate sense and should be a separate
   item if included.
2. **Inventory the productive evaluative `for/of + person + to-inf` family.**
   C03-076 covers the performer in `It is easy for new staff to ...`, but the
   module has no bounded decision such as `It is important for her to ...`
   versus `It was kind of her to ...`. This is the clearest missing instance
   of the plan's “adjective + object/control patterns where natural” family.

### Residual must-fix cards

#### R1 — C03-007 / ADJ-008: broad `knowledgeable` Front omits accepted forms

`knowledgeable ___ + professional or general topic` does not close on
**about**. **Knowledgeable in** is natural for a field or area of expertise,
and **knowledgeable of** is also attested. Narrow the Front to an
about-oriented topic context, or teach the default plus accepted variants.

#### R2 — C03-011 / ADJ-015: merged card and surviving row disagree

The source is now a two-anchor `capable of / able to` contrast, but ADJ-015
still declares only `anchor: capable`, `marker: of`, and
`target: capable of + activity`. Update the surviving row to own both
constructions; ADJ-075 is excluded and cannot carry the `able to` half.

#### R3 — C03-015 / ADJ-019: `proficient at` remains a valid completion

`proficient ___ + field, language, or activity` permits **in** for a
field/language and **at** for an activity or task. The inventory already names
`proficient at` as the confusable form. Retrieve the bounded `in` versus `at`
choice, or ask explicitly for the neutral default and accepted alternative.

#### R4 — C03-033 / ADJ-040: `independent from` is accepted in part of the Front

The broad class `influence, institution, or resource` admits
**independent from**, especially for separation or institutional relations.
Narrow the card to the neutral **independent of** default and acknowledge
**from**, or retrieve a bounded meaning/register decision.

#### R5 — C03-034 / ADJ-041: `satisfied by` is not closed out

The broad `result, service, or decision` class can support passive-cause
**satisfied by**, which the live inventory itself lists as confusable. Keep
**with** as the evaluated-object default, but narrow the Front or contrast it
with **by + cause/means**.

#### R6 — C03-037 / ADJ-044: the printed sentence also accepts `by`

`Voters were disappointed ___ the government's response ...` naturally
accepts **by** as well as the taught **with**. The Back also gives **in** but
does not tell the learner that **by** is valid. Add **by + cause** or rewrite
the prompt so it explicitly asks for the evaluated-object default and names
the accepted cause frame.

#### R7 — C03-047 / ADJ-055: merged card and surviving row disagree

The source retrieves `afraid of / afraid to`, while ADJ-055 still declares
only `anchor: afraid`, `marker: of`, and `target: afraid of + thing/activity`;

#### R8 — C03-049 / ADJ-057: merged card and surviving row disagree

#### R9 — C03-051 / ADJ-059: `person` leaves `proud for` materially available

`proud ___ + person, achievement, or action` admits **proud for + person**
when the speaker is happy on that person's behalf. Remove `person` from this
one-answer chunk, or contrast **proud of someone/their achievement** with
**proud for someone after their success**.

#### R10 — C03-057 / ADJ-067: the intended semantic contrast is not retrieved

Both printed anchors take **to**, so a learner can fill both blanks correctly
without knowing `related = connection` versus `relevant = direct
pertinence`. Blank **related/relevant** and require each once in the bounded
sentences; leave **to** visible.

#### R11 — C03-060 / ADJ-071: broad `action` Front also admits `about`

contrast.

#### R12 — C03-065 / ADJ-079: the Front contains the whole answer

blank **whether/how** in two bounded sentences and require each once.

#### R13 — C03-068 / ADJ-082: the Front gives away the contrast mapping
