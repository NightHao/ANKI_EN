most of its lexical frames are accurate, useful contemporary AmE. In

## Must-fix before validation

### M1 — C02-028 is answer-revealing recognition, not retrieval

**Card/item:** `C02-028` / `VERB-PREP-IMPACT`.

The Front prints both `impact small businesses` and `impact on small
businesses`, then asks which is transitive. The learner can answer by noticing
the printed word **on**; no usable connection has to be retrieved. Replace it
with a bounded production contrast, for example:

1. `The new zoning rules may ___ small businesses directly.`  
   *(use **impact**)*
2. `The report examines how the new zoning rules may impact ___ small
   businesses.`

Back: `impact small businesses` / `impact on small businesses`, with the note
that both the transitive and intransitive-plus-`on` frames are established.
Merriam-Webster records both frames in contemporary use:
[impact, verb](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/impact).

### M2 — C02-034 uses the recipient and request in an unnatural order

**Card/item:** `C02-034` / `VERB-PREP-APPLY`.

`Nora applied for a research fellowship to the National Science Foundation`
is markedly less natural than the standard combined frame. Change the Front
and Back to:

> Nora applied ___ the National Science Foundation ___ a research fellowship.

> Nora **applied to the National Science Foundation for a research
> fellowship**.

Use `apply to + organization + for + thing requested`. Cambridge explicitly
gives `apply to sb/sth (for sth)` and the example “applied to a charitable
organization for a grant”:
[apply](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/apply).

### M3 — C02-037 has a normal zero-connector competitor

**Card/item:** `C02-037` / `VERB-PREP-SPEND`.

The printed sentence accepts both:

- `spent $2 million on upgrading its systems`; and
- `spent $2 million upgrading its systems`.

The Back names only the first. Cambridge records both `spend something doing
something` and `spend something on doing something`, and supplies money
examples for both:
[spend](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/spend).

Make the decision explicit with two different complement shapes:

1. `The agency spent $2 million ___ cybersecurity upgrades.`
2. `The agency spent six months ___ the new system. (use **test**)`

Back: `on cybersecurity upgrades` / `testing the new system`. If a `V-ing`
money example is retained, the Back must name both accepted forms rather than
grading zero as wrong.

### M4 — C02-041 does not distinguish `agree on` from `agree to`

**Card/item:** `C02-041` / `VERB-PREP-AGREE-ON`.

`After three rounds of bargaining, both sides agreed ___ a two-year contract`
allows both **on** (jointly settled it) and **to** (accepted it). The gloss
cannot make the unseen intention objective.

Print both completed forms and ask for the perspective, or use a paired,
meaning-labeled contrast:

1. `After discussing several dates, both sides agreed **on** May 15.`  
   *(joint choice)*
2. `The union proposed a two-year contract, and management agreed **to** it.`  
   *(acceptance)*

Keep `C02-040` (`agree with`) and `C02-042` (`agree to`) as separate sense
carriers; the split required by the inventory review is correct.

### M5 — C02-043 omits common `argue over`

**Card/item:** `C02-043` / `VERB-PREP-ARGUE`.

The topic blank in `argued ___ data ownership ___ the department chair`
accepts both **about** and **over**. Add:

> Also OK: `argued over data ownership with the department chair`.

Alternatively, print `about/over` as the accepted topic slot and retrieve only
the person connector **with**. Oxford lists `argue with somebody about/over
something`:
[argue](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/argue).

### M6 — C02-061 also permits `look into`

**Card/item:** `C02-061` / `VERB-PREP-LOOK`.

`The review panel will look ___ how the subsidy affects rural households`
accepts **at** and the very natural investigative **into**. Replace the
analytical clause with an unmistakable visual target, for example:

> Please look ___ the trend line in Figure 3.

Back: `look at the trend line`. If the analytical sense is retained, contrast
completed `look at` (examine/consider) with `look into` (investigate) rather
than forcing one into an open blank.

### M7 — C02-073 overstates exclusivity in AmE

**Card/item:** `C02-073` / `VERB-PREP-GRADUATE`.

`graduated from the University of Michigan` is the safest and most widely
accepted form, but AmE also uses transitive `graduated college/school`.
Merriam-Webster says both are frequent while noting that the transitive form
draws criticism:
[graduated or graduated from](https://www.merriam-webster.com/grammar/graduated-or-was-graduated).

Keep the target, but revise the Back:

> **Graduate from + institution** is the most widely accepted form.
>
> Also heard in AmE, especially with *high school/college*: `She graduated
> college in 2024.` Some readers object to this transitive form, so use
> **from** in careful general writing.

The Front should ask for the safest general-writing form, not “the required
form.”

### M8 — C02-077 does not bind the lexical verb

**Card/item:** `C02-077` / `VERB-PREP-CARE`.

Both blanks can be completed by many unrelated verbs (`support`, `assist`,
`serve`, `value`, `respect`). The target verb appears only on the Back. Change
the instruction to:

> Complete both statements with the required form of **care**.

The `care for` caregiving versus `care about` concern distinction itself is
sound. Cambridge supports both senses:
[care for](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/care-for),
[care about](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/care-about).

### M9 — C02-081 leaves optional `that` as an ungradeable blank

**Card/item:** `C02-081` / `VERB-TOINF-DECIDE`.

`The board decided ___ the vote should be postponed` accepts **that** or zero.
Do not use an empty blank for an optional marker. Print the second form as
`decided (that) the vote should be postponed`, then ask which frame allows a
different clause subject; or explicitly accept both **that** and zero on the
Back.

### M10 — C02-083 leaves optional `that` as an ungradeable blank

**Card/item:** `C02-083` / `VERB-TOINF-HOPE`.

Both `I hope that Maya can present` and `I hope Maya can present` are standard.
Print `(that)` or list zero as an accepted alternative. Keep the genuine
subject-control distinction: `hope to present` has the same understood subject
as **hope**, while the finite clause names **Maya**.

### M11 — C02-084 leaves optional `that` as an ungradeable blank

**Card/item:** `C02-084` / `VERB-TOINF-PROMISE`.

`The contractor promised ___ the damage would be repaired` likewise accepts
**that** or zero. Print `(that)` and retrieve the clause-versus-subject-controlled
infinitive distinction, not the optional marker.

### M12 — C02-090 forces `that` and overlaps C02-126

**Card/item:** `C02-090` / `VERB-VING-SUGGEST`.

`suggested ___ the agency extend` permits both **that** and zero; Cambridge
explicitly gives `I suggest you plan ...`:
[word patterns: suggest](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/word-patterns-suggest).
The same mandative `suggest (that) + subject + base verb` is then tested again
by `C02-126`.

Narrow C02-090 to its own item:

> The consultant suggested ___ the pilot for six months. (use **extend**)

> `suggested extending`

Add the concise trap `not suggest to extend`. Let C02-126 own the
person-specific mandative/`advise + person + to-infinitive` contrast, and state
there that **that** may be omitted.

### M13 — C02-092 and C02-116 are reciprocal duplicates

**Cards/items:**

- `C02-092` / `VERB-VING-KEEP`
- `C02-116` / `VERB-OBJECT-COMPLEMENT-KEEP`

Each card tests both `keep + V-ing` and `keep + object + adjective`; only the
order and examples change. They are two cards for the same contrast.

Preferred repair: merge the two inventory decisions into one contrast item
and keep one card. If both inventory items must survive, make each card atomic:

- C02-092 retrieves only `keep + V-ing` for continued/repeated activity.
- C02-116 retrieves only `keep + object + adjective` for a maintained state.

Do not retain two reciprocal two-part contrasts.

### M14 — C02-115 leaves optional `that` as an ungradeable blank

**Card/item:** `C02-115` / `VERB-OBJECT-COMPLEMENT-FIND`.

Both `found that the portal was difficult` and `found the portal was
difficult` occur with a content clause. Print `(that)` in sentence 2 or list
zero as accepted. The useful contrast is `find + object + adjective` versus
`find (that) + finite clause`.

Cambridge notes that reporting `that` is often omitted and lists **find** among
the verbs that take a reporting clause:
[verb patterns: that-clauses](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/verb-patterns-verb-that-).

### M15 — C02-121 and C02-123 duplicate the same `say/tell` decision

**Cards/items:**

- `C02-121` / `VERB-REPORT-SAY-TELL`
- `C02-123` / `VERB-REPORT-TELL-THAT`

Both teach `say + (that-)content` versus `tell + person + (that-)content`.
`C02-123` additionally puts optional **that** in a blank, so `said the test
results were normal` is a normal unlisted answer.

Merge these inventory items and retain one card. The retained Back should say:

- `say + (that-)clause`;
- `tell + person + (that-)clause`; and
- **that** is often omitted in both.

Cambridge gives `He told us that ...`, rejects `He told that ...`, and explains
the frequent zero-**that** option:
[verb patterns: that-clauses](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/verb-patterns-verb-that-).

## Should-fix

- **C02-008 / `VERB-OBJECT-EXPLAIN` and C02-124 /
  `VERB-REPORT-EXPLAIN-WH`:** they are not exact duplicates, but C02-008's
  second sentence already teaches `explain to + recipient + wh-clause`, while
  C02-124 teaches `explain + wh-clause` and repeats recipient placement on the
  Back. Keep C02-008 strictly on `explain + thing + to + person`; let C02-124
  own the wh-clause. This preserves the valuable recipient trap without
  teaching the same extension twice.
- **C02-017 / `VERB-OBJECT-COMPRISE`:** the grammar is evidence-based, but add
  the register warning that **be comprised of** remains criticized by some
  readers. Merriam-Webster records it as established and now very frequent,
  while recommending **composed of** as the safer alternative when criticism
  matters:
  [comprise](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/comprise).
- **C02-048 / `VERB-PREP-PREVENT`:** both lines take **from**, so the card is
  reinforcement rather than a contrast. Prefer a repair (`prevented attackers
  to access ...` → `prevented attackers from accessing ...`) or retype it as a
  chunk/context in both source and inventory.
- **C02-068 / `VERB-PREP-COMPARE`:** the overlap note is accurate and should
  remain. Cambridge records both `compared to/with` in analytical comparisons:
  [compare](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/compare).
  A slightly stronger Front would ask for the usage tendency (direct analysis
  versus likeness), not merely “which preposition is possible,” whose answer
  is visibly “both.”
- **C02-071 / `VERB-PREP-INSIST`:** add formal **insist upon + NP/V-ing** as an
  accepted variant if the Front remains a production blank.
- **C02-085 / `VERB-TOINF-REFUSE` and C02-087 /
  `VERB-TOINF-AFFORD`:** these are labeled `contrast`, but both halves retrieve
  the same complement. Retype/narrow them to lexical chunk/context cards unless
  a genuinely different competing frame is introduced.
- **C02-090, C02-125, C02-126:** after M12, keep the anchor boundary explicit:
  C02-090 owns `suggest + V-ing`; C02-125 owns `recommend + V-ing / recommend
  that`; C02-126 owns `suggest (that) + subject + mandative base / advise +
  person + to-infinitive`. Do not repeat a full neighboring card on each Back.
- **C02-100 / `VERB-ALTER-GO-ON`:** `went on summarizing` is grammatical, but
  `continued summarizing` is more transparent contemporary AmE. Retain the
  target if desired, but keep the explanation focused on same activity versus
  next activity rather than implying equal frequency.
