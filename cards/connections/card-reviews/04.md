# Independent card audit — Module 04 Noun Patterns

Scope: every card in `04-noun-patterns.md`, checked against the complete
Module 04 rows in `coverage.tsv`, `CONNECTION_DECK_PLAN.md`,
`CARD_AUTHORING.md`, and the earlier `inventory-reviews/03-04.md`. This is a
review only; no source, inventory, builder, or status was changed.

## Verdict

**Do not validate Module 04 yet.** Structural mapping is exact and most prose
and examples are natural contemporary AmE, but **31 of 72 cards** have a
blocking learning-design or closure defect. The main problems are:

- four reciprocal pairs spend two cards on the same decision: **difference**,
  **responsibility**, **influence**, and **experience**;
- several related chunk/contrast families repeat one side of a decision or
  present overlapping prepositions as if a semantic cue selected only one;
- `decision about whether` omits normal `decision on whether`;
- `difficulty in + V-ing` competes with both zero and, under a different
  reading, `with + V-ing`; and
- some completed-chunk designs still contain a blank whose exact printed frame
  permits another common AmE completion.

The new `way to do / way of doing` recognition card is sound and should remain.

## Must-fix cards

### M1 — C04-001 / NOUN-001: the third `reason` blank has accepted alternatives

`The report explains the reason ___ the clinic closed` accepts **why** and
**that**, and zero is also possible in ordinary AmE (`the reason the clinic
closed`). The Back acknowledges only **that**, after presenting **why** as the
answer.

Replace sentence 3 with a recognition prompt rather than a forced blank:

> Which are standard before the explanatory clause?  
> `the reason why the clinic closed`  
> `the reason that the clinic closed`  
> `the reason the clinic closed`

Keep the `reason for` and `reason to` retrievals, but explicitly teach that the
three explanatory variants overlap.

### M2 — C04-006 / NOUN-006 and C04-048 / NOUN-053: reciprocal duplicate

Both cards retrieve the same `influence on + target / influence over +
person, body, or decision` tendency in opposite order. Cambridge separately
records **influence on** as an effect and **influence over** as power,
especially control, but the roles can overlap in real contexts.

Merge the two inventory items and retain one card. A safer Front is:

> Match the conventional emphasis, then state the overlap.  
> 1. `the comments' influence on the final vote` (observed effect)  
> 2. `the chair's influence over the committee` (power)

The Back should say these are useful tendencies, not categorical semantic
opposites.

### M3 — C04-007 / NOUN-007: `solution for` also fits the broad Front

`a solution ___ + problem` does not make **to** the only contemporary AmE
answer. **A solution for the problem** is common, even though **solution to
the problem** is the conventional learner default. The current note limits
**for** to a user or purpose and is too categorical.

Use a completed default-recognition card:

> `a solution to the housing shortage`
>
> What is the conventional noun frame, and what common variant may occur?

Back:

> Prefer `solution to + problem` as the standard neutral frame.  
> Also OK: `solution for + problem` occurs in contemporary AmE; `for` also
> commonly names an intended user or purpose.

### M4 — C04-009 / NOUN-009: noun `answer for` is wrongly reduced to the verb

The Back says `answer for + action` is a verb accountability frame, but noun
**answer for** also occurs with a recipient or a problem: `an answer for every
problem`, `I have an answer for you`. The Front's `question or problem` range
therefore does not close on **to**.

Replace with a completed default-recognition card for `answer to +
question/problem`, and add:

> Also OK in other noun frames: `an answer for someone` and, in some
> contexts, `an answer for a problem`. Do not confuse these with the verb
> `answer for + action`.

### M5 — C04-015 / NOUN-019 and C04-016 / NOUN-020: exact reciprocal duplicate

Both cards test `difference between + compared items / difference in +
feature`, merely reversing the order and vocabulary. Merge the inventory rows
and keep one two-context contrast card. C04-015 is the cleaner base. Retire
C04-016.

### M6 — C04-026 / NOUN-031, C04-027 / NOUN-032, and C04-028 / NOUN-033:
the `concern` family overlaps and repeats itself

C04-027 repeats the **about** frame already taught by C04-026. More
importantly, `concern about the displaced families` is fully natural in
sentence 1, and `concern over the shortage` is fully natural in sentence 2.
The cue does not uniquely select **for** versus **about**. Merriam-Webster
records **concern about** for worries, **concern for** for sympathetic regard,
and **concern over** for anxiety; these are tendencies with overlap.

Merge the three items/cards into one acceptance-aware contrast:

> Which forms are normal, and what does each tend to foreground?  
> 1. `concern for/about the displaced families`  
> 2. `concern about/over the housing shortage`

Back:

> `concern for + person/group` can foreground care for welfare; `concern
> about + person/issue` is broadly normal worry; `concern over + issue` is
> common, especially in formal reporting. Do not force an exclusive choice.

### M7 — C04-029 / NOUN-034 and C04-030 / NOUN-035: `relationship with`
is taught twice

C04-030 already teaches `relationship between + two named participants /
relationship with + one other participant`, so C04-029 is a complete subset
of it. Merge the items and retain only C04-030. This is one coherent
one-participant-versus-two-participant decision.

### M8 — C04-031 / NOUN-036, C04-032 / NOUN-037, and C04-033 / NOUN-038:
duplication plus false `with/to` separation

C04-033 repeats the **with** frame from C04-031. C04-032 then presents
**connection to** as selected by topic/system/network/destination, although
Cambridge gives both **connection with** and **connection to** for a joined or
felt relationship and supplies overlapping human and nonhuman examples.

Consolidate C04-031 and C04-033 into one `between` versus one-participant
card. Retain a second completed recognition card for `connection with/to`,
explicitly saying the two often overlap:

> `a connection between A and B` names both sides.  
> `a connection with/to an entity, system, person, or topic` names the other
> endpoint; lexical preference and emphasis vary.

Do not keep a blank that requires **to** while **with** remains ordinary.

### M9 — C04-035 / NOUN-040 and C04-036 / NOUN-041: overlapping comparison
frames are still presented as fillable choices

Both cards acknowledge overlap, but each Front contains
`a comparison ___ + item` and asks the learner to retrieve one preposition.
Cambridge records **comparison with** and **comparison to** in analytical
comparison examples, as well as `comparison between`.

Merge into one recognition card:

> `comparison of A with/to B`  
> `comparison between A and B`

Explain that **with** is a useful analytical default and **to** often supports
likeness, but contemporary usage overlaps. Keep the existing C04-036 analogy
examples only if the complete `comparison of A to B` frame is printed; they do
not exemplify a bare immediate `comparison to + item` complement.

### M10 — C04-037 / NOUN-042 and C04-038 / NOUN-043: overlap plus non-atomic
`advantage to`

C04-037 and C04-038 both teach the activity meaning and correctly admit that
`advantages of conducting` and `advantages to conducting` overlap. C04-038
also combines that construction with the separate recipient meaning
`advantage to employees`, so the card has two decisions.

Merge the activity rows/cards into one acceptance-recognition card:

> Both are standard:  
> `the advantage(s) of doing something`  
> `the advantage(s) to doing something`

Either exclude the low-value recipient subdecision or give it its own
inventory item and completed card, with **advantage for + recipient** also
recognized. Cambridge's AmE entry gives `advantage for students`; do not
teach **to** as the sole recipient connector.

### M11 — C04-040 / NOUN-045 and C04-041 / NOUN-046: open `benefit`
source/recipient blanks

Cambridge records **benefits of foreign travel**, **benefit from school**, and
**benefit to most Americans**; **for someone's benefit** is also standard.
Consequently, the broad Fronts `benefit ___ + thing/activity` and
`benefit ___ + recipient` do not uniquely select **of** and **to**.

Merge into one completed source/recipient recognition card:

> `the benefits of the policy` — benefits associated with it  
> `benefit from the policy` — benefit obtained from it  
> `a benefit to residents` — a helpful effect received by them  
> `for residents' benefit` — intended to help them

If atomicity requires two cards, split source and recipient meanings, but do
not leave either as a one-answer blank.

### M12 — C04-043 / NOUN-048: normal `cost for` remains available

`cost to + person/group` is a good burden frame, but `cost for + person/group`
is also common when the price faced by that group is meant. The Front's
generic “person, group, or system bearing the burden” does not exclude it.

Prefer one completed `cost of + source/action / cost to + affected party`
recognition card, or revise C04-043 to:

> Retrieve the completed burden frame: `a substantial cost to patients`.

Add:

> `cost for + person/group` is also normal for a price or expense applicable
> to them; it is not a generally wrong substitute.

### M13 — C04-044 / NOUN-049 and C04-045 / NOUN-050: exact reciprocal duplicate

Both cards test `responsibility for + duty/outcome / responsibility to +
person, group, or authority` in reverse order. Cambridge gives both frames in
exactly those roles. Merge the inventory rows and retain C04-044; retire
C04-045.

### M14 — C04-046 / NOUN-051 and C04-047 / NOUN-052: the `control` blanks
remain open

Both Backs correctly say `control over` and `control of` overlap, but each
Front still asks for one preposition. `control of the process`, `control over
the system`, and similar alternatives remain natural under the printed role
cues.

Merge into one completed recognition card:

> `control over prices/the outcome` often foregrounds power or influence.  
> `control of the organization/system` often foregrounds possession or
> management.  
> Both prepositions can overlap when either construal is available.

### M15 — C04-054 / NOUN-060: `involvement in/with` is not closed by the
entity cue

`involvement in a lobbying group` can describe membership or participation,
while `involvement with a lobbying group` describes association. The printed
second sentence therefore accepts **in** under a common reading.

Use completed examples and ask for the meaning:

> `her involvement in drafting the legislation` — participation in an
> activity  
> `her involvement with a lobbying group` — association

Add that `involvement in a group` is also possible when participation or
membership is intended.

### M16 — C04-055 / NOUN-061 and C04-056 / NOUN-062: reciprocal duplicate
and incomplete `experience` system

The two cards reverse the same `experience of / experience with` decision.
They also omit common **experience in**. Cambridge explicitly gives
`experience of working`, `experience in working`, and the lived-event reading
`the experience of pain`; its evidence does not support a categorical
`of = lived through / with = practical` binary.

Merge into one recognition card with three useful tendencies:

> `experience of recovering from surgery` — the lived event/process  
> `experience with statistical software` — hands-on familiarity with a tool  
> `experience in statistical analysis/working with children` — skill or
> activity domain

State that **of**, **in**, and **with** overlap in practical-experience
contexts. Retire the reciprocal second card.

### M17 — C04-065 / NOUN-073: `decision on whether` is missing

The exact sentence `The board postponed its decision ___ whether to close ...`
accepts **about** and **on**. Cambridge lists `decision about whether`, while
Collins gives `decision on whether`; both are established.

Replace sentence 1 with:

> The board postponed its **decision about/on whether to close** the branch.

Keep the contrast with `decision to close`, which correctly names the chosen
action. The item target, connector field, and Back must all add **on whether**.

### M18 — C04-069 / NOUN-077: zero and `with + V-ing` defeat the forced
`in/with` choice

The Back itself admits common zero `difficulty recruiting`, but the Front
orders the learner to choose only **in** or **with**. Cambridge treats
`have difficulty doing` and `have difficulty in doing` as alternatives.
Moreover, `difficulty with recruiting specialists` is grammatical when
recruiting is construed as the problematic process or topic.

Replace the forced contrast with recognition:

> Standard activity frames:  
> `have difficulty recruiting`  
> `have difficulty in recruiting`
>
> Thing/topic frame:  
> `have difficulty with the new software`

Add that `difficulty with + V-ing` is possible under a “problem with the
activity/process” reading, so the contrast is a tendency, not an exclusive
form rule.

## Should-fix cards

- **C04-004 / NOUN-004 and C04-005 / NOUN-005:** the merged source/target
  architecture is good, but **effect(s) from** and **impact from** can name a
  causal source in contemporary usage. Print the source phrase as completed
  if the learning decision is only source versus affected target, or mention
  **from** as a causal alternative. Do not imply every source must use **of**.
- **C04-023 / NOUN-028:** `opinion about + person/thing` can also express an
  evaluation. Make `opinion of` a completed conventional frame and call
  `about/on` issue-oriented tendencies, not a strict person-versus-topic rule.
- **C04-039 / NOUN-044:** `a disadvantage to choosing X` and
  `a disadvantage for a group` are normal. Keep `disadvantage of + thing/
  V-ing` as a completed chunk or add the alternatives instead of leaving the
  broad blank.
- **C04-042 / NOUN-047:** `cost of + thing/action` is strong, but the Front's
  generic pattern is less useful than the two natural existing examples.
  Prefer a completed chunk if C04-042 is not merged with C04-043.
- **C04-051 / NOUN-057:** **contribution toward + goal/cause** is a normal
  AmE alternative to **contribution to**. Add it under `Also OK:` or narrow
  the Front to a completed `contribution to` chunk.
- **C04-052 / NOUN-058:** `investment into` occurs in contemporary business
  usage, although `investment in` is the safer learner default. Phrase the
  card as default retrieval, not categorical rejection.
- **C04-059 / NOUN-065:** `awareness about` occurs, though `awareness of` is
  the standard neutral collocation. The current card is usable; label **of**
  as the preferred default.
- **C04-062 / NOUN-070:** the content-versus-relative analysis is accurate.
  Add the useful omission diagnostic: noun-complement **that** is normally
  retained (`the claim that incomes fell`), while object-relative **that** can
  be omitted (`the claim the minister repeated`). Cambridge's noun-phrase
  grammar identifies `the claim that he was ...` as a complement and an
  object-gap clause as a postmodifier.
- **C04-068 / NOUN-076:** `risk from + source/activity` is possible when the
  danger caused by that source is meant. Keep `risk of + V-ing` as the
  probability/activity default, but do not imply **from** is impossible.
- **C04-070 / NOUN-078:** the `problem with + V-ing` examples are natural and
  the zero `have no problem + V-ing` note is valuable. Add that `problem in +
  V-ing` can occur in a formal “difficulty encountered in the process”
  reading; the current completed examples should remain the production model.

## Evidence for disputed claims

- [Cambridge: influence](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/influence)
  records both `influence on` and `influence over`; its dedicated collocation
  entries distinguish effect from power/control.
- [Cambridge: responsibility](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/responsibility)
  gives `responsibility for` a duty and `responsibility to` a person or group.
- [Cambridge: experience](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/experience)
  gives both `experience of working` and `experience in working`; its
  [experience-of collocation entry](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/experience-of)
  also distinguishes lived experience without making the practical uses
  exclusive.
- [Merriam-Webster: concern](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/concern)
  supplies current noun examples with `about`, `for`, and `over`.
- [Cambridge: connection](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/connection)
  gives overlapping `connection with` and `connection to` examples as well as
  the two-sided `connection between`.
- [Cambridge: comparison](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/comparison)
  records `comparison with`, `comparison to`, and `comparison between` in
  overlapping comparison environments.
- [Cambridge: advantage](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/advantage)
  gives `advantages of doing` and AmE `advantage for` a recipient; the learner
  entry also records `advantages to` a thing.
- [Cambridge: benefit](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/benefit)
  gives noun examples with `of`, `from`, and `to`, plus `for someone's

- [Cambridge: decision](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/decision)
