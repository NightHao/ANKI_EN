# 05 Conjunctions and Clause Linkers

## C05-001

<!-- card
item: LINK-001
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete the answer to **Why was the outdoor hearing postponed?** Use a linker
followed by a full clause.

The outdoor hearing was postponed ___ several witnesses could not travel
through the flooded district.

### Back

The outdoor hearing was postponed **because several witnesses could not travel
through the flooded district**.

Use `because + subject + finite verb` when the reason is the main answer to
**why**.

**Since/as** are grammatical if the reason is backgrounded, but they are not
the best direct answer to the printed **why** question.

## C05-002

<!-- card

### Front

Complete the matched causes with **because** or **because of**.

1. The clinic canceled its outreach visit ___ the road was unsafe.
2. The clinic canceled its outreach visit ___ hazardous road conditions.
3. The clinic canceled its outreach visit ___ the only access road being
   flooded. (`V-ing` clause with its own expressed subject)

### Back

1. The clinic canceled its outreach visit **because the road was unsafe**.
2. The clinic canceled its outreach visit **because of hazardous road
   conditions**.
3. The clinic canceled its outreach visit **because of the only access road
   being flooded**.

Use `because + finite clause`, but `because of + NP/V-ing`. Sentence 3 has its
own embedded subject, **the only access road**.

### Front

Which **since** introduces a background reason, and which marks a starting
time?

1. **Since all applicants already have the timetable, I will focus on the
   assessment criteria.**
2. **The archive has been closed since March.**

### Back

1. Initial **since + finite clause** introduces a background reason.
2. **Since + time point** marks the beginning of a continuing period.

`Since + finite clause` can present a reason as known background. `Since +
time point` marks the beginning of a continuing period.

-->

### Front

**As the laboratory already has suitable equipment, no additional purchase is
necessary.**

What relation does initial **as** express here?

### Back

Initial `as + finite clause` introduces a **background reason** here. Use
**because** when the reason itself needs strong emphasis. Temporal **as** has a
separate card with a context that blocks this causal reading.

## C05-005

<!-- card
item: LINK-005
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`result or situation + due to + NP/V-ing`

### Back

Use **due to** before a noun phrase or `V-ing` clause to give a
neutral-to-formal cause.

- The rail service was suspended **due to a signaling failure**.
- Several applications were rejected **due to lacking essential
  documentation**.

The second example shows a licensed `V-ing` complement, but
**because the applicants lacked essential documentation** is usually more
idiomatic in ordinary prose.

### Front

`main clause + owing to + NP/V-ing`

### Back

Use **owing to** before a noun phrase or `V-ing` clause to express a cause in
formal writing.

- The hearing was adjourned **owing to the chair's sudden illness**.
- The contractor incurred a penalty **owing to repeatedly missing safety
  deadlines**.

## C05-010

<!-- card
item: LINK-010
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Punctuate the logical result in two standard ways with **therefore**.

1. The sample was too small ___ therefore ___ the findings remain
   inconclusive. (one sentence)
2. The sample was too small ___ Therefore ___ the findings remain
   inconclusive. (two sentences)

### Back

1. **The sample was too small; therefore, the findings remain inconclusive.**
2. **The sample was too small. Therefore, the findings remain inconclusive.**

**Therefore** is a conjunctive adverb, not a coordinating conjunction. Use a
semicolon or period before it and normally a comma after it; a comma alone
would create a comma splice.

### Back

1. **The river exceeded the threshold. As a result, the footbridge was

## C05-014

### Front

Put the completed **though** clause in both licensed positions.

1. ___ the apartment is compact, it has ample natural light.
   (clause-initial)
2. The apartment has ample natural light, ___ it is compact.
   (clause-final subordinate clause)

### Back

1. **Though the apartment is compact, it has ample natural light**.
2. **The apartment has ample natural light, though it is compact**.

`Though + finite clause` is a neutral-to-conversational alternative to
**although** and can appear before or after the main clause.

**Also OK:** **Although** fits both completed sentences, with a slightly more
formal tone.

Add a one-word conversational afterthought meaning “despite that.”

The new office is farther from the station. The neighborhood is much quieter,
___.

### Back

**The new office is farther from the station. The neighborhood is much

Complete with **though** or **if** after printed **Even**; use each once.

1. Even ___ the reservoir is currently full, restrictions remain in place.
   (known fact)
2. Even ___ the reservoir becomes full next month, restrictions will remain in

### Back

### Front

Complete the matched concessions with the structure indicated.

1. ___ widespread opposition, the council approved the redevelopment plan.
   (NP)
2. ___ hundreds of residents objecting, the council approved the plan.
   (`V-ing` clause with its own expressed subject)
3. ___ hundreds of residents objected, the council approved the plan.

### Back

## C05-029

-->

### Front

Complete the negative and affirmative conditions.

1. The scholarship will lapse ___ the recipient submits the annual report.
   (only non-submission causes it to lapse)
2. The scholarship will lapse ___ the recipient withdraws from the program.
   (withdrawal causes it to lapse)

### Back

1. **The scholarship will lapse unless the recipient submits the annual
   report**.
2. **The scholarship will lapse if the recipient withdraws from the
   program**.

**Unless** means “if not”; **if** introduces the stated affirmative condition.

**Also OK for sentence 1:** **The scholarship will lapse if the recipient does
not submit the annual report.**

For sentence 2, **when** becomes possible only if withdrawal is treated as
expected rather than as an open condition.

### Front

Using **unless**, **stated**, and **otherwise**, give both standard word orders
for the conventional passive formula.

### Back

Both are standard:

- **Unless otherwise stated, all interviews will be recorded for quality
  assurance.**
- **Unless stated otherwise, all interviews will be recorded for quality

another rule or instruction says otherwise.” Do not infer that **interviews**
is the thing “stated.”

### Front

Complete the real and explicitly unreal comparisons.

1. Lena speaks as if she ___ the project well; she has managed it for two
   years. (**know**)
2. A visitor speaks as if he ___ the project, but he has never worked here.
   (**run**)

### Back

1. **Lena speaks as if she knows the project well; she has managed it for two
   years.**
2. **A visitor speaks as if he ran the project, but he has never worked
   here.**

The present **knows** is compatible with a real comparison. The past form
**ran** marks modal remoteness—an unreal or distancing comparison—not past
time in this context. A past form after **as if/as though** is not always
counterfactual; context determines the reading.

The speaker can choose a remote form to distance themself even when a
proposition might be true; the surrounding facts make the intended reading
salient but do not make the other form ungrammatical.

### Back

### Back
