# 01 Prepositions and Prepositional Phrases

## C01-001

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-AT-CLOCK
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

For a **precise clock time**, complete the sentence:

The overnight maintenance window begins ___ midnight and ends at 3:30 a.m.

### Back

The overnight maintenance window begins **at midnight** and ends at 3:30 a.m.

Use **at** for a precise time point, including **noon** and **midnight**.

## C01-002

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-AT-NIGHT
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Choose **at night** or **during the night**.

1. The observatory is open to visitors ___ . (the usual nightly period)
2. I woke twice ___ before the interview. (within that particular night)

### Back

1. The observatory is open to visitors **at night**.
2. I woke twice **during the night** before the interview.

Use **at night** for the normal general period. In contemporary AmE,
**during the night** is the natural production choice for an event within one
particular night.

Also OK in limited contexts: **in the night**, especially when the particular
night is already established.

## C01-003

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-AT-MOMENT
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete the fixed frame: `at the ___` = now.

### Back

**at the moment**

**At the moment** means “now” in neutral conversation; keep **the** in this
fixed expression.

- The clinic cannot accept new patients **at the moment**.
- Fuel prices are relatively stable **at the moment**.

## C01-008

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-IN-MONTH
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Choose **in** or **by**. The intended meaning is “at some point during that
month,” not “by that October deadline.”

The city expects to complete the coastal resilience study ___ October 2027.

### Back

The city expects to complete the coastal resilience study **in October 2027**.

Use **in** with a month, year, or season. Use **on** for a specific date.

-->

### Front

Choose **in** or **for**.

1. The replacement sensors will arrive ___ two weeks. (two weeks from now)

### Back

1. The replacement sensors will arrive **in two weeks**.

-->

### Front

Fix if wrong:

The research ethics committee will reconsider the application in next week.

### Back

The research ethics committee will reconsider the application **next week**.

## C01-011

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-DURING-NP
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Choose **during** or **for**. The blank locates the interruption within the
event; it does not state its duration.

Several participants lost their internet connection ___ the webinar.

### Back

Several participants lost their internet connection **during the webinar**.

Use **during + noun phrase** for an event or bounded period. A finite-clause
linker such as **while + subject + verb** is a separate Module 05 decision.

## C01-012

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-OVER-DURATION
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Choose **over** or **during**. Which form foregrounds the six months as the
span across which a trend developed?

Public confidence improved steadily ___ the following six months.

### Back

Public confidence improved steadily **over the following six months**.

Use **over + period** for change, development, or repeated activity spread
across that period. **During** can locate an event within a period but does
not itself foreground development across the span.

Also OK in the printed sentence: **during the following six months**.

## C01-013

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-THROUGHOUT
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Choose **throughout** or **during** by meaning.

1. Emergency staff remained on duty ___ the entire storm. (at every stage)
2. Two brief power failures occurred ___ the storm. (at unspecified points)

### Back

1. Emergency staff remained on duty **throughout the entire storm**.
2. Two brief power failures occurred **during the storm**.

**Throughout** means during all parts of a period. **During** places something
within the period without claiming that it continued for all of it.

Also OK for 1: **during the entire storm**; **entire** itself supplies the
whole-period meaning.

## C01-015

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-FROM-TO
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete the internal connection:

`from + start ___ + end`

### Back

**from + start to + end**

Use **from + start + to + end** for a complete range.

- The community health center is open **from 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.**
- Applications increased **from 240 to 315** in one year.

Also OK in suitable ranges: **between 8:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m.** and **from
8:00 a.m. until 6:00 p.m.**

## C01-016

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-BY-DEADLINE
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

1. Please submit the revised risk assessment ___ 5:00 p.m. (deadline)
2. A compliance officer will remain available ___ 5:00 p.m. (continuation)

### Back

1. Please submit the revised risk assessment **by 5:00 p.m.**
2. A compliance officer will remain available **until 5:00 p.m.**

**By** means no later than the deadline; completion may be earlier. **Until**
means an action or state continues up to the endpoint.

Also OK for 1 with the stricter meaning “earlier than 5:00”: **before 5:00
p.m.**

## C01-017

<!-- card
item: PREP-TIME-THROUGH-ENDPOINT
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Choose **through** or **until**.

1. The exhibition will remain open ___ Sunday. (Sunday is included)
2. The exhibition will remain open ___ Sunday morning. (closing endpoint)

### Back

1. The exhibition will remain open **through Sunday**.
2. The exhibition will remain open **until Sunday morning**.

In AmE, **through + endpoint** explicitly includes the endpoint. **Until**
marks continuation up to the stated stopping point.

Also OK for 1 with the same intended endpoint: **throughout Sunday** or
**until the end of Sunday**.

### Front

### Back

## C01-028

<!-- card
item: PREP-PLACE-SCHOOL-AME
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Which form foregrounds each named viewpoint: **in school** or **at school**?

1. Lina is still ___; she plans to graduate next year. (enrollment)
2. Lina is ___ now, helping with the science fair. (site/activity)

### Back

1. Lina is still **in school**; she plans to graduate next year.
2. Lina is **at school** now, helping with the science fair.

In AmE, **in school** commonly emphasizes enrollment or the period of
education; **at school** emphasizes presence or activity at the site.
Attendance contexts often permit either viewpoint.

Also OK when the other viewpoint is intended: **at school** can describe an
enrolled student's attendance, and **in school** can describe participation
in schooling.

## C01-029

<!-- card

-->

### Front

Which form foregrounds the named viewpoint: **at** or **in**?

1. I cannot access the secure server remotely; I need to be ___ the office.
   (workplace as the required site)
2. The package is ___ the office, on the shelf behind my desk. (literal

### Back

office** for 2 are both grammatical in ordinary AmE.

## C01-033

<!-- card
item: PREP-PLACE-ABOVE-OVER
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Choose **above** or **over**. Which foregrounds neutral higher position, and
which foregrounds covering/alignment?

1. The emergency exit sign is ___ the doorway, at a higher position.
2. A protective canopy extends ___ the doorway and covers the entrance.

### Back

1. The emergency exit sign is **above the doorway**.
2. A protective canopy extends **over the doorway** and covers the entrance.

**Above** is neutral for a higher position. **Over** often adds direct
alignment, covering, or movement. For many ordinary higher-position
descriptions, **above** and **over** overlap.

Also OK for 1 in ordinary location contexts: **over the doorway**.
Also OK for 2: **above the doorway**; **over** more directly foregrounds the
canopy's covering and alignment with the entrance.
