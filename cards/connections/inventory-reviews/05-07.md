# Independent inventory review — Subdecks 05–07

## Verdict

**Revise before source authoring.** The slice has a strong, correctly routed
core: all three subdecks have visible boundaries, the central
`because/because of`, `despite/although`, relative-placement, reduced-relative,
purpose-infinitive, and dangling-modifier decisions have homes, and the
excluded rows generally preserve the one-home contract.  It is not yet ready
to author as-is because the linker-headed reduced-clause grid is incomplete,
several inventory rows are too broad to yield a closed-answer card, and one
07 item is better owned by comparison/degrees.

## Must fix before authoring

1. **LINK-023 omits a licensed reduced form.** `though + reduced complement`
   lists `V-ing`, `AdjP`, `PP`, and predicative NP but not
   `past-participle-clause`. `Though injured, ...` is a normal reduced
   concessive construction, subject to the same recoverable-subject condition.
   Add `past-participle-clause` or split the row so each card tests one shape.

2. **The required reduced-linker grid is incomplete.** The plan explicitly
   treats reduced clauses as a controlled system, not only `although + V-ing`.
   Add proposed rows (or a deliberately evidenced deferred row) for:

   - `while + past-participle-clause` and `while + AdjP/PP` (for example,
     *While located downtown...*, *while in college...*), with an understood
     subject tied to the main clause;
   - `when + predicative-NP` (*When a student, ...*) if retained in the
     intended C1 scope; and
   - `if + V-ing-clause` (*If traveling abroad, ...*), with the shared-subject
     restriction.

   Do not solve this by expanding LINK-023/050/053 into a single many-answer
   blank. Each shape needs a narrow context or a clearly bounded contrast.

3. **LINK-054 is linguistically over-restrictive.** `so that + subject + can
   or will + verb` excludes common `could`, `may`, `might`, `would`, and some
   natural non-modal purpose clauses. Change the target/evidence note to
   `so that + subject + modal/appropriate finite verb`; author contexts that
   distinguish it from the shared-subject infinitive. LINK-055 should likewise
   state its modal/register constraint without making an exclusive blank when
   both formal alternatives fit.

4. **REL-023 has an incorrect antecedent claim.** `all of which` refers to a
   recoverable plural set, not to a singular preceding proposition. Replace
   “plural set or proposition” with “plural set”; use `which` (REL-041) for a
   whole-clause comment. Otherwise a card would teach an ungrammatical answer.

5. **REL-004 exposes an impossible confusable.** A zero relative cannot be a
   subject relative. Replace `zero relative` in its confusables with `that`
   and reserve zero for REL-007/008 object-relative contexts. Make the target
   visibly `thing + which/that + finite verb` so authoring cannot accidentally
   accept an omitted subject marker.

6. **REL-005 is not atomic enough.** `noun + that + verb or clause` combines
   subject and object relatives, whose zero-marker boundaries differ. Split
   into subject-`that` and object-`that` rows, or make it a repair/contrast
   explicitly tied to REL-008/009. The current wording cannot produce one
   objectively gradeable Front.

7. **NF-018 violates one-home routing.** Its central learning decision is a
   comparison baseline (`compared with/to`), which the master routing sends to
   09 when comparison is central. Mark this row excluded/deferred in 07 and
   propose the bounded comparison item in 09, unless the 07 card instead tests
   only the free-supplementive attachment/understood-subject issue.

8. **Perfect passive non-finites are missing.** NF-013/014 cover `having +
   past participle`, but not `having been + past participle` (*Having been
   informed of the delay, ...*). Add a proposed 07 row with the affected
   main-clause subject and a non-dangling repair contrast. This is a practical
   C1 passive/perfect form directly named in the audit scope.

## Should fix

- **LINK-018–024 and LINK-035–038 need an operational shared-subject test.**
  “Recoverable” is too loose for card construction. Notes should say that the
  omitted subject must normally be the main-clause subject (or the passive
  subject must be the entity affected), and require a finite clause when that
  relationship is not recoverable. This matters especially for LINK-021
  (`although + PP`) and LINK-036 (`if + PP`): use idiomatic bounded forms such
  as *although under pressure* / *if in doubt*, not arbitrary PP reductions.

- **LINK-058 should say modal remoteness, not simply unreal past.** A past form
  after `as if/as though` can signal an unreal or distancing comparison; the
  row should not imply every past form is counterfactual. Give an explicitly
  unreal context for the intended contrast.

- **LINK-010 and LINK-060 need punctuation precision.** `therefore` and
  `however` may follow a period or semicolon, normally with a comma after the
  connector; neither joins independent clauses with a comma alone. Keep this
  as a repair or fully printed punctuation contrast, not a connector blank.

- **REL-006 and REL-021 need defining/non-defining constraints in the notes.**
  `who/whom/that/zero` alternatives are not interchangeable across those
  environments. State that `that` and zero are defining-only; fronted
  preposition uses `whom/which`, whereas neutral stranding can use `who/which`
  in the appropriate relative type.

- **REL-038/039 are acceptance-aware rather than ordinary contrast cards.**
  Because both forms can be right in a defining relative, require either a
  completed-chunk recognition Front or explicit `Also OK:` policy. Do not make
  a one-blank card that grades `who` versus `that`, or `which` versus `that`,
  as uniquely correct without a house-style context.

- **REL-040 needs genuine reference contrast, not punctuation decoration.**
  Keep it only if the pair changes the identified referent; otherwise defer it
  rather than teaching a comma rule detached from meaning.

- **NF-009/NF-010 need a semantic separation.** Both use `main clause,
  V-ing phrase`. NF-009 should retain only same-agent accompanying/sequence
  uses; NF-010 should require a result entailed by the whole prior clause.
  If an example permits either reading, it fails closed-answer review.

- **NF-015/NF-016 need attachment safeguards.** A postposed past participle
  often reads as a reduced relative on the nearest noun. NF-016 should use
  only cases that indisputably supplement the clause subject, or be converted
  to a repair that contrasts noun-head and clause-head attachment.

- **NF-017 (`Given + NP`) is a useful fixed supplementive phrase, but it must
  not teach a general free-participle rule.** Keep it as a bounded chunk/context
  item and make its basis/condition meaning explicit.

## Missing high-value families

1. The linker-headed reductions identified under Must fix: especially
   `though + past participle`, `while + past participle/AdjP/PP`, and
   `if + V-ing`.
2. An **absolute construction** with its own explicit subject, such as
   `Weather permitting, ...` or `All things considered, ...`, should be
   considered for 07. It fills the gap between free shared-subject participles
   (NF-008–016) and `with + NP + non-finite` (NF-019–021). Include only one
   conventional, closed-answer form; otherwise mark it deliberately excluded.
3. A compact **passive perfect participle** item (`having been + past
   participle`) is required to make the perfect/passive coverage real rather
   than implied by NF-013 and NF-015 separately.

## Duplicate or merge candidates

- **LINK-013/014/016 versus LINK-017:** retain all only if 013/014/016 test
  register or factual strength and 017 tests the NP/V-ing versus finite-clause
  complement boundary. The best closed-answer core is a single
  `despite/in spite of + NP/V-ing` versus `although + finite clause` contrast;
  `though` and `even though` need context/recognition cards, not competing
  blanks.
- **LINK-018–022 and LINK-023:** use LINK-023 as a routing/overview boundary
  or remove it after individual `though` rows exist. Its current multi-shape
  target duplicates the individual `although` decisions and cannot itself be
  authored as one atomic card.
- **REL-012/037 and REL-013/014:** preserve the pairs, but avoid authoring two
  indistinguishable register cards. One context-sensitive place/time contrast
  each is sufficient if it teaches neutral `where/when` versus formal `in/on
  which`.
- **NF-022/NF-024 and NF-023/NF-025:** keep one rule row plus one repair row
  only when the former teaches recognition and the latter requires a genuine
  rewrite. Otherwise merge each pair to avoid repeating the same dangling
  decision.
- **NF-001/002/003:** NF-003 is the strongest shared-purpose versus
  different-subject contrast; NF-001/002 should become narrowly contextual or
  be merged if they would only restate it.

## Safe exclusions and deferrals

- Keep **LINK-065** excluded: broad `even though + reduced complement` would
  overgeneralize a less productive pattern. It should not be used to deny the
  separately licensed finite `even though + clause`.
- Keep **REL-034** deferred until a specific noun-plus-infinitive context
  determines modifier versus noun-content reading. This correctly protects
  module 04 while retaining `the first person to arrive` under REL-030.
- Keep **REL-035/036** excluded from this subdeck: noun content `that` clauses
  belong to 04 and antecedent-less `what` clauses are free relatives.
- Keep **REL-045** and **NF-029–035** as visible routing exclusions. They are
  valuable anti-duplication evidence: noun-head reduced relatives remain 06,
  linker-headed reductions remain 05, preposition-selected `V-ing` remains
  01, and degree-result infinitives remain 09.
