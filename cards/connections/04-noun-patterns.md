# 04 Noun Patterns

## C04-001

<!-- card
item: NOUN-001
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete the first two **reason** frames, then answer the recognition question.

1. The lack of reliable data was the main reason ___ delaying the launch.
   (motive or basis)
2. The new safety evidence gives regulators a reason ___ revise the policy.
   (action justified)
3. Give all three standard possibilities before the explanatory clause:
   `the reason ___ the clinic closed`.

### Back

1. The lack of reliable data was the main **reason for delaying the launch**.
2. The new safety evidence gives regulators a **reason to revise the policy**.
3. All three are standard: **the reason why the clinic closed**,
   **the reason that the clinic closed**, and
   **the reason the clinic closed**.

Use `reason for + NP/V-ing` for a motive or basis and `reason to + base verb`
for a justified action. Before an explanatory clause, **why**, **that**, and
zero are overlapping standard choices. **Reason of** is not the standard frame
for any of these meanings.

## C04-002

<!-- card
item: NOUN-002
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`the/a cause ___ + event or condition`

### Back

Use `the/a cause of + event or condition` for the factor that produces it.

- Researchers are investigating **the cause of the sudden decline in bee
  populations**.
- Chronic sleep deprivation is **a major cause of workplace accidents**.

A **cause** produces an outcome; a **reason** commonly explains or motivates
an action or belief.

## C04-003

<!-- card
item: NOUN-003
type: context

-->

### Front

Complete the noun frame with the preposition that introduces the event that
produced the outcome:

The service interruption was the result ___ a failed software update.

### Back

The service interruption was **the result of a failed software update**.

Use `result of + event/action` after the noun **result** to name what produced
the outcome. The verb frame is different: `A failed update resulted in a
service interruption.`

## C04-004

<!-- card
item: NOUN-004
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete the matched **effect** descriptions. For sentence 1, give both
accepted source connectors.

1. The study examined the effect ___ prolonged screen exposure.
   (the source being evaluated)
2. The study examined the effect of prolonged screen exposure ___ sleep
   quality. (the affected target)

### Back

1. The study examined **the effect of/from prolonged screen exposure**.
2. The study examined **the effect of prolonged screen exposure on sleep
   quality**.

Use `effect of + cause/source` when the source is the thing being evaluated;
`effect from + cause/source` can more directly present causal origin. Use
`effect on + target` to name what is affected. The verb **affect** takes a
direct object: `Screen exposure may affect sleep quality.`

-->

### Front

Match each completed frame to its conventional emphasis, then state whether
the distinction is absolute.

1. `the comments' influence on the final vote`
2. `the chair's influence over the committee`

### Back

1. **Influence on** tends to foreground an observed effect.
2. **Influence over** tends to foreground power to shape a person, body, or
   decision.

These are useful tendencies, not categorical opposites. Real contexts may
support either construal.

## C04-007

<!-- card
item: NOUN-007
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`a solution to the housing shortage`

What is the conventional neutral noun frame, and what common variant may also
occur?

### Back

Prefer `solution to + problem` as the conventional neutral learner default.

- Affordable childcare is not **a complete solution to the labor shortage**.
- The engineering team proposed **a practical solution to the drainage
  problem**.

**Also OK:** `solution for + problem` occurs in contemporary AmE.
`Solution for + person/purpose` also commonly names the intended user or use,
as in `a payment solution for small businesses`.

### Front

`an answer to the housing affordability problem`

What is the conventional neutral noun frame, and where can **for** occur?

### Back

Prefer `the/an answer to + question/problem` for a response or solution.

different accountability pattern.

### Back

### Front

Which completed frames are standard, and what does each structure foreground?

1. `a comparison of A with/to B`
2. `a comparison between A and B`

### Back

## C04-031

<!-- card
item: NOUN-042
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Both forms are standard. What kind of meaning do they share?

1. `the advantage of sharing anonymized data`
2. `the advantage to sharing anonymized data`

### Back

Both `advantage(s) of + NP/V-ing` and `advantage(s) to + NP/V-ing` can name a
benefit associated with a thing, feature, or activity. **Of** is a useful broad
production default; the two forms overlap.

- **The main advantage of remote consultations** is easier access for rural
  patients.
- **One advantage of sharing anonymized data** is that researchers can
  replicate the findings.

Recipient frames such as `an advantage for students` are a separate meaning;
do not infer that **to** is the only possible recipient connector.

## C04-049

<!-- card
item: NOUN-070
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

In each sentence, decide whether **that** introduces the content of the claim
or a relative clause modifying **claim**.

1. Economists challenged the claim that household incomes had fallen.
2. Economists challenged the claim that the minister had repeated.

### Back

1. In **the claim that household incomes had fallen**, **that household
   incomes had fallen** gives the content of the claim. The clause is complete.
2. In **the claim that the minister had repeated**, **that** is a relative
   marker. The minister had repeated **the claim**, so **repeated** has an
   object gap.

Content `that + complete clause` states what was claimed. Relative **that**
introduces a modifying clause in which the noun **claim** has an understood
role.

Omission is a useful diagnostic: content **that** is normally retained
(`the claim that incomes fell`), while object-relative **that** can be omitted
(`the claim the minister repeated`).

1. Both **decision about whether** and **decision on whether** are established.
2. After the vote, the board announced **its decision to close the branch**.

Use `decision about/on whether + clause/to-inf` for the unresolved question
being considered. Use `decision to + base verb` for the action chosen.

### Front

### Back

Use the uncountable noun frame `evidence that + clause` when the clause states

## C04-055

<!-- card
item: NOUN-076
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

Give the broad probability/activity default:

`the/a risk ___ + V-ing activity`

### Back

Use `the/a risk of + V-ing` for the possibility that an activity or event will
have a harmful result.

- Sharing identifiable records creates **a risk of exposing patients'
  private information**.
- The medication can reduce **the risk of developing severe complications**.

`Risk to + person/thing` instead names what is endangered, and
`risk that + clause` states a proposition. `Risk from + source/activity` can
name the source of the danger.

## C04-056

<!-- card
item: NOUN-077
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Which activity frames are standard, and what does the **with** frame tend to
introduce?

1. `have difficulty recruiting specialist physicians`
2. `have difficulty in recruiting specialist physicians`
3. `have difficulty with the new scheduling software`

### Back

Both zero and **in** are standard before an activity:
`have difficulty (in) + V-ing`. Use `difficulty with + NP` for a problematic
thing or topic. `Difficulty with + V-ing` is also possible when the activity
is construed as the problematic process, so this is a tendency rather than an
exclusive form rule.

## C04-057

<!-- card
item: NOUN-078
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

Give the broad neutral production model:

`the/a problem ___ + V-ing activity`

### Back

Use `the/a problem with + V-ing` for a difficulty or objection connected with
an activity.

- **The problem with relying on voluntary disclosure** is that many cases go
  unreported.
- **One problem with expanding the highway** is that it may encourage more
  traffic.

The common frame `have no problem + V-ing` omits **with**: `We had no problem
finding qualified interpreters for the hearing.`

`Problem in + V-ing` can also occur in a more formal
“difficulty encountered in the process” reading; the completed **with** frames
above remain the broad production model.

### Front

Complete the two **permission** frames.

### Back

## C04-059

<!-- card
item: NOUN-080
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Which versions use a standard **method** frame? Select all that apply.

1. The pilot study identified a practical way to reduce response bias.
2. The pilot study identified a practical way of reducing response bias.
3. The pilot study identified a practical way for reduce response bias.

### Back

**1 and 2 are both standard.**

- `a way to + base verb`: **a practical way to reduce response bias**
- `a way of + V-ing`: **a practical way of reducing response bias**

Both frames can describe a method, and they often overlap in meaning. Do not
force a choice between them without a more specific context. Version 3 is
incorrect because **for** cannot be followed directly by the base form
**reduce**.
