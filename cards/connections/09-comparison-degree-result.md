# 09 Comparison, Degree, and Result Patterns

## C09-001

<!-- card
item: CMP-ER-THAN-NP
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`short adjective + -er + than + comparison standard`

### Back

Use an inflected comparative with short adjectives to compare one entity with
a noun-phrase standard.

- The revised battery is **lighter than the previous model**.
- Rural rents remain **lower than urban rents** in most regions.
- The second interview was **shorter than the first one**.

## C09-002

<!-- card
item: CMP-MORE-THAN-NP
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`more + longer adjective + than + comparison standard`

### Back

Use `more + adjective + than` with adjectives that do not normally take
`-er`.

- The new consent form is **more accessible than the original version**.
- Preventive care is **more cost-effective than emergency treatment**.
- The revised schedule is **more flexible than our previous arrangement**.

## C09-003

<!-- card
item: CMP-LESS-THAN-NP
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete the lower-degree comparison without changing the word order:

The updated interface is ___ intuitive than the prototype.

### Back

The updated interface is **less intuitive than the prototype**.

Use `less + adjective + than` for a lower degree of a quality. The
**fewer/less + noun** countability system is tested separately in C09-017
through C09-019.

## C09-004

<!-- card
item: CMP-COMPARATIVE-THAN-CLAUSE
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

The housing reform proved more difficult to implement ___ local officials had
anticipated.

### Back

The housing reform proved **more difficult to implement than local officials
had anticipated**.

Use `comparative + than + finite clause` when the comparison standard has its
own subject and verb.

## C09-005

<!-- card
item: CMP-THAN-ELLIPTICAL-CLAUSE
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete with the auxiliary that replaces the repeated verb **performed**:

This year's applicants performed better in the simulation than last year's
applicants ___.

### Back

This year's applicants performed better in the simulation **than last year's
applicants did**.

Here, **did** stands for **performed in the simulation**. A comparison clause
can omit its repeated predicate but retain the appropriate auxiliary.

## C09-006

<!-- card
item: CMP-THAN-OBJECT-PRONOUN
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Use a one-word neutral AmE completion:

Nora is considerably more experienced in contract negotiations than ___.

### Back

Nora is considerably more experienced in contract negotiations **than me**.

An object-form pronoun after **than** is normal in contemporary neutral AmE.

**Also OK:** **than I am** is the full finite-clause alternative. Do not use
bare **than I** when the understood predicate needs to be explicit.

## C09-007

<!-- card
item: CMP-THAN-EXPECTED
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`comparative + than expected`

### Back

Use **than expected** as a conventional reduced comparison meaning “than
people expected” or “than was expected.”

- The transition was **smoother than expected**.
- Consumer demand recovered **more quickly than expected**.
- The final phase proved **more expensive than expected**.

## C09-008

<!-- card
item: CMP-THAN-BEFORE
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

After the accessibility upgrade, visually impaired users can navigate the
portal more independently ___.

### Back

After the accessibility upgrade, visually impaired users can navigate the
portal **more independently than before**.

Use `comparative + than before` to compare the present degree or performance
with an earlier reference point.

## C09-009

<!-- card
item: CMP-COMPARATIVE-MUCH-FAR
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete with **very** or **much**.

1. The revised forecasting model is ___ more accurate than the old one.
2. The revised forecasting model is ___ accurate in stable conditions.

### Back

1. The revised forecasting model is **much more accurate than the old one**.
2. The revised forecasting model is **very accurate in stable conditions**.

Use **much** or **far** before a comparative to mark a large difference. Use
**very** before the base adjective, not normally before a comparative.

**Also OK for sentence 1:** **far more accurate**.

## C09-010

<!-- card
item: CMP-COMPARATIVE-A-LOT
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`a lot + comparative`

### Back

Use **a lot** before a comparative to express a substantial difference in
neutral or conversational English.

- Hybrid work has become **a lot more common** since the pandemic.
- The revised onboarding process is **a lot easier to follow**.
- Public transit is **a lot cheaper than driving** in the city center.

## C09-011

<!-- card
item: CMP-COMPARATIVE-SLIGHTLY
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete with **slightly** or **far**.

1. The experimental group scored ___ higher, by only two percentage points.
2. The control group took ___ longer, by nearly forty minutes.

### Back

1. The experimental group scored **slightly higher**, by only two percentage
   points.
2. The control group took **far longer**, by nearly forty minutes.

Use **slightly** or **a little** for a small difference. Use **far**, **much**,
or **a lot** for a large difference.

## C09-012

<!-- card
item: CMP-COMPARATIVE-BY-AMOUNT
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Applications rose from 24,000 to 30,000.

1. This year's total was higher ___ last year's total.
2. This year's total was higher ___ 6,000 applications.

### Back

1. This year's total was **higher than last year's total**.
2. This year's total was **higher by 6,000 applications**.

Use **than + standard** to identify what is being compared. Use
**by + amount** to state the size of the difference.

## C09-013

<!-- card
item: CMP-COMPARATIVE-TIMES
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Server A processes 800 requests per second; Server B processes 200.

Complete the precise multiplicative comparison:

Server A is four times ___ fast ___ Server B.

### Back

Server A is **four times as fast as Server B**.

For precise production, use
`N times as + adjective/adverb + as + standard`.

Forms such as **four times faster than** occur in real usage, but the
expression can be interpreted differently. The **four times as ... as** frame
makes the intended ratio clearest.

## C09-014

<!-- card
item: CMP-COMPARATIVE-AND-COMPARATIVE
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`comparative and comparative`

### Back

Repeat a short comparative with **and** to show continuous change.

- Entry-level housing is becoming **scarcer and scarcer**.
- The diagnostic tests grew **faster and faster** as the cache warmed up.
- The negotiations became **harder and harder** to sustain.

## C09-015

<!-- card
item: CMP-MORE-AND-MORE
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

As public services move online, digital accessibility is becoming ___
important.

### Back

As public services move online, digital accessibility is becoming **more and
more important**.

Use `more and more + adjective` to show progressive change when the adjective
normally forms its comparative with **more**. Short adjectives commonly use
`-er and -er`.

## C09-016

<!-- card
item: CMP-DOUBLE-COMPARATIVE
type: repair
status: validated
-->

### Front

Fix if wrong:

After the database was indexed, the search function became more faster.

### Back

After the database was indexed, the search function became **faster**.

A standard comparative normally uses either **-er** or **more**, not both.
Use **much faster** if you need to emphasize a large difference.

**More quickly** is correct because the adverb **quickly** forms its
comparative with **more**.

## C09-020

### Front

`as + adjective + as + comparison standard`

### Back

Use the two-part **as ... as** frame to express equal degree.

- The remote assessment was **as rigorous as the in-person exam**.
- Preventive maintenance is **as important as emergency repair**.
- The refurbished unit is **as energy-efficient as a new building**.

## C09-021

<!-- card

type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete with **efficient** or **efficiently**.

1. The smaller team handled the migration as ___ as the larger team.
2. The smaller team was as ___ as the larger team.

### Back

1. The smaller team handled the migration **as efficiently as the larger
   team**.
2. The smaller team was **as efficient as the larger team**.

## C09-022

<!-- card
item: CMP-NOT-AS-AS
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Express the same lower-degree comparison in both structures:

1. The second proposal is ___ practical ___ the first.
2. The second proposal is ___ practical ___ the first.

Use **not as ... as** in sentence 1 and **less ... than** in sentence 2.

### Back

1. The second proposal is **not as practical as the first**.
2. The second proposal is **less practical than the first**.

Both express a lower degree. Their tone and emphasis can differ, so they are
not interchangeable in every discourse context.

## C09-025

<!-- card
item: CMP-NOT-SO-AS
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

`not so + adjective/adverb + as + comparison standard`

Complete the formal alternative:

The distinction is not ___ clear ___ the official guidance suggests.

### Back

The distinction is **not so clear as the official guidance suggests**.

**Not so ... as** is grammatical, but **not as clear as** is more common in
contemporary AmE and is also correct here.

### Back

- The new reservoir is **twice as deep as the old reservoir**.
- The express service is **twice as frequent as the local service**.

## C09-034

<!-- card
item: CMP-THE-MORE-THE-MORE
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete each relationship.

1. ___ evidence the committee reviews, ___ confident it becomes in its
   recommendation. (two related variables)
2. As the investigation continued, the evidence became ___ complex.
   (progressive change in one quality)

### Back

1. **The more evidence the committee reviews, the more confident it becomes
   in its recommendation.**
2. As the investigation continued, the evidence became **more and more
   complex**.

Use `the + comparative ..., the + comparative ...` to relate two changing
variables. Use **more and more + adjective** for progressive change in one
quality.

## C09-035

<!-- card
item: CMP-THE-MORE-THE-LESS
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Express the inverse relationship:

As commuters receive more reliable real-time information, they experience
less uncertainty.

### Back

**The more reliable real-time information commuters receive, the less
uncertainty they experience.**

Use `the more ..., the less ...` to describe two variables moving in opposite
directions. The structure expresses an association; it does not by itself
prove causation.

## C09-036

<!-- card
item: CMP-THE-SOONER-THE-BETTER
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`The sooner, the better.`

### Back

This elliptical correlative comparison means that acting earlier is
preferable.

- We should disclose the scheduling conflict today; **the sooner, the
  better**.
- Start gathering migration data now. **The sooner, the better.**
- If the symptoms persist, seek medical advice; **the sooner, the better**.

-->

### Front

Complete with **too** or **very**.

1. The raw data is ___ incomplete to support a reliable conclusion.
2. The raw data is ___ incomplete, but the researchers can still identify a
   broad trend.

### Back

1. The raw data is **too incomplete to support a reliable conclusion**.
2. The raw data is **very incomplete, but the researchers can still identify

prevented-result structure.

### Front

1. The sample was ___ small ___ support a reliable subgroup analysis.
   (analysis was not possible)
2. The sample was ___ small ___ the subgroup estimates varied widely.
   (actual result)

### Back

1. The sample was **too small to support a reliable subgroup analysis**.
2. The sample was **so small that the subgroup estimates varied widely**.

`too + adjective + to-infinitive` presents a prevented outcome.
`so + adjective + that-clause` presents an actual result.

### Front

   (the investigation could be reopened)

### Back

## C09-065

<!-- card
item: CMP-COMPARED-WITH-TO
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

`Compared with/to + explicit baseline, main clause`

Which two accepted forms can frame this analytical comparison?

The region invests substantially more in preventive care relative to the
national average.

### Back

1. **Compared with the national average, the region invests substantially
   more in preventive care.**
2. **Compared to the national average, the region invests substantially more
   in preventive care.**

Use **compared with** or **compared to** to frame a statement against an
explicit baseline. **Compared with** is especially common in direct analysis;
**compared to** is also standard and is often favored for likeness. Their
usage overlaps.

### Front

Use **higher than** in sentence 1 and **above** in sentence 2.

## C09-068

<!-- card
item: CMP-NO-MORE-THAN
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Compare the intended emphasis:

1. Despite months of publicity, ___ 40 residents attended.
   (the speaker presents 40 as disappointingly few)
2. Under the fire code, the room may hold ___ 40 people.
   (a neutral formal ceiling)

### Back

1. Despite months of publicity, **no more than 40 residents attended**.
2. Under the fire code, the room may hold **not more than 40 people**.

Both forms set an upper limit. **No more than** often adds a minimizing
implication; **not more than** can state a neutral formal ceiling.

**Also OK:** **at most 40** is a clear neutral alternative. The two target
forms can overlap when context does not establish the intended emphasis.
