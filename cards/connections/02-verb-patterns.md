# 02 Verb Patterns

## C02-001

<!-- card

-->

### Front

Fix if wrong:
Please answer to the final question in the survey.

### Back

Please **answer the final question** in the survey.

## C02-017

<!-- card
item: VERB-OBJECT-COMPRISE
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Express the same whole-to-parts relationship with **comprise** in active and passive form:

1. The curriculum ___ six core courses.
2. The curriculum ___ six core courses. (passive)

### Back

1. The curriculum **comprises six core courses**.
2. The curriculum **is comprised of six core courses**.

Active **whole + comprise + parts** and passive **whole + be comprised of + parts** are both established. Also natural: `The curriculum is composed of six core courses.`

Some readers still criticize **be comprised of**. When that criticism matters, **be composed of** is the safest passive alternative.

### Front

### Back

1. Inadequate maintenance can **result in equipment failure**.

2. The auditor advised the agency ___ the contract. (use **review**)

### Back

1. The auditor **suggested that the agency review the contract**.
2. The auditor **advised the agency to review the contract**.

Formal AmE uses **suggest (that) + subject + base verb**; **that** may be omitted. **Suggest** has no `person + to-infinitive` frame; use **advise + person + to-infinitive** when appropriate.

## C02-129

status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete the past habit and present familiarity:

1. Before relocating, Elena used to ___ to the office five days a week. (use **commute**)
2. She is now used to ___ with colleagues across several time zones. (use **collaborate**)

### Back

1. Before relocating, Elena **used to commute to the office five days a week**.
2. She is now **used to collaborating with colleagues across several time zones**.

**Used to + base verb** describes a past habit or state. **Be/get used to + noun/V-ing** means be or become accustomed to something; here **to** is a preposition.

## C02-130

<!-- card
item: VERB-PARTICLE-RULE-OUT-IN
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete each diagnostic conclusion with **out** or **in**.

1. The scan showed no evidence of a fracture, so the doctor ruled it ___.
2. A highly specific test confirmed the infection, so the doctor ruled it ___.

### Back

1. The doctor **ruled it out**.
2. The doctor **ruled it in**.

**Rule out** means eliminate something as a possibility. **Rule in** means establish or include something as a likely diagnosis or explanation; it is much less common and is used especially in medical and technical contexts.

Both are separable: **rule the condition out/in** and **rule it out/in**, not **rule out/in it**.

Additional patterns:

- **rule out + noun:** We can't rule out a delay.
- **rule out + V-ing:** We can't rule out postponing the launch.
- **rule someone out of + event/role:** Her injury ruled her out of the final.

## C02-131

<!-- card
item: VERB-FUTURE-GONNA
type: repair
status: validated
-->

### Front

Fix if wrong:
I gonna send the revised proposal tonight.

### Back

**I'm gonna send the revised proposal tonight.**

Casual **gonna** is the spoken form of **going to**, but it still requires the
correct form of **be**: **I'm gonna**, **you're gonna**, or **she's gonna**.
Use **gonna + base verb**. Prefer **going to** in formal and most professional
writing.
