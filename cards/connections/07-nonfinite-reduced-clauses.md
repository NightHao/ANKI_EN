# 07 Non-finite and Reduced Clauses

## C07-001

<!-- card
item: NF-001
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

State the agency's purpose with an initial infinitive clause:

The agency expanded evening appointments because it wanted to reduce waiting
times for working parents.

### Back

**To reduce waiting times for working parents, the agency expanded evening
appointments.**

An initial `to + base verb` clause can state the purpose of the main-clause
subject. The agency is understood as the agent of both **reduce** and
**expanded**.

## C07-002

<!-- card
item: NF-002
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete the two purpose frames.

1. ___ improve transparency, the committee began publishing its voting
   records. (compact neutral form)
2. ___ improve transparency, the committee began publishing its voting
   records. (more explicit neutral-to-formal form)

### Back

1. **To improve transparency, the committee began publishing its voting
   records.**
2. **In order to improve transparency, the committee began publishing its
   voting records.**

Both infinitive clauses express the committee's purpose. **In order to** is
more explicit and often slightly more formal; ordinary **to** is usually more
concise.

## C07-003

<!-- card
item: NF-003
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

1. The researchers encrypted the files ___ protect participants' identities.
   (the researchers perform both actions)
2. The researchers encrypted the files ___ external reviewers could not
   identify participants. (the purpose clause has its own subject)

### Back

1. The researchers encrypted the files **to protect participants'
   identities**.
2. The researchers encrypted the files **so that external reviewers could not
   identify participants**.

Use a purpose infinitive when its understood subject is the main-clause
subject. Use `so that + finite clause` to express a separate subject.

## C07-005

<!-- card

-->

### Front

Complete with the fixed stance infinitive built from **clear**:

___, I do not believe the current evidence justifies a nationwide ban.

### Back

**To be clear, I do not believe the current evidence justifies a nationwide
ban.**

Fixed stance infinitives such as **to be clear**, **to be honest**, and
**to put it simply** frame the speaker's comment rather than state the
main-clause subject's purpose.

## C07-006

<!-- card
item: NF-006
type: context
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete with `only to + have + object + past participle`:

The negotiating team spent six months drafting a compromise, ___ it rejected
at the final vote.

### Back

The negotiating team spent six months drafting a compromise, **only to have
it rejected at the final vote**.

`main clause, only to + verb` presents an unexpected later result, often one
that is disappointing or ironic. It does not express the original purpose.

## C07-007

<!-- card
item: NF-007
type: chunk

-->

### Front

`To make matters worse, + negative development`

### Back

Use this fixed comment clause to introduce an additional development that
makes an already difficult situation worse.

- The clinic lost its main supplier. **To make matters worse, two nurses
  resigned the same week.**
- Housing costs rose sharply; **to make matters worse, wages remained
  stagnant**.
- The database failed during the audit. **To make matters worse, the latest
  backup was corrupted.**

### Front

Combine the simultaneous actions with an initial V-ing clause:

Maya was reviewing the field notes. She noticed a contradiction in the
interview timeline.

### Back

**Reviewing the field notes, Maya noticed a contradiction in the interview
timeline.**

An initial V-ing clause can describe an action simultaneous with or closely

subject.

### Front

Complete with a V-ing form of **speak**:

The mediator moved between the two conference rooms, ___ separately with each
delegation.

### Back

The mediator moved between the two conference rooms, **speaking separately

A postposed V-ing clause can add an accompanying or closely following action

### Front

Complete with a result V-ing form of **leave**:

The court invalidated the licensing rule, ___ hundreds of pending applications
without a clear legal framework.

### Back

The court invalidated the licensing rule, **leaving hundreds of pending
applications without a clear legal framework**.

A postposed V-ing clause can express a result of the whole preceding event.
The invalidation—not merely **the court**—produced the stated consequence.

## C07-011

item: NF-011

-->

### Front

Complete with a V-ing form of **know**:

___ the coastal road would remain closed for months, local businesses created
an alternative delivery route.

### Back

**Knowing that the coastal road would remain closed for months, local
businesses created an alternative delivery route.**

`Knowing/Understanding + content clause` can present a reason available to
the main-clause subject. The businesses possessed the knowledge and acted on
it.

### Front

### Back

**Not knowing how long the outage would last, the hospital activated its

### Front

Use a perfect participial clause to show that the audit was completed before
the analysts published their recommendations:

___ the audit, the analysts published their recommendations.

### Back

**Having completed the audit, the analysts published their recommendations.**

`Having + past participle` marks a completed action before the main event.
The analysts are the understood subject of both actions.

### Front

Complete with a negative perfect participial form of **receive**:

___ a response by Friday, the research team postponed the field trial.

### Back

**Not having received a response by Friday, the research team postponed the
field trial.**

`Not having + past participle` states that an expected earlier action or
experience did not occur. The research team is the understood subject of
**not having received**.

## C07-015

<!-- card
item: NF-PERFECT-PASSIVE
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Complete with an active or passive perfect participial clause built from
**inform**, as the roles require.

1. ___ the regional offices about the security risk, the agency disabled the
   portal. (the agency performed the earlier action)
2. ___ of the security risk, the regional offices disabled the portal.
   (the offices received the earlier action)

### Back

1. **Having informed the regional offices about the security risk, the agency
   disabled the portal.**
2. **Having been informed of the security risk, the regional offices disabled
   the portal.**

Use `having + past participle` for an earlier active action. Use
`having been + past participle` when the main-clause subject had already
received the earlier action.

### Front

Complete with a past-participle form of **support**:

The revised protocol remained in force, ___ by evidence from three independent
trials.

### Back

The revised protocol remained in force, **supported by evidence from three
independent trials**.

A postposed past-participle clause can add a passive state or characterization

protocol**, not a nearby noun.

## C07-018

<!-- card
item: NF-017
type: context

-->

### Front

Read the completed sentence:

**Given the limited evidence currently available, a temporary restriction
would be more proportionate than a permanent ban.**

What relation does `Given + NP` express here: accepted basis, physical
transfer, or later result?

### Back

**Accepted basis.**

**Given the limited evidence currently available, a temporary restriction
would be more proportionate than a permanent ban.**

`Given + NP` presents an accepted fact or condition as the basis for a
conclusion. It is a bounded supplementive phrase, not a general free
participle rule.

## C07-022

<!-- card
item: NF-ABSOLUTE-WEATHER-PERMITTING
type: chunk
status: validated
-->

### Front

`Weather permitting, + planned event`

### Back

This conventional absolute construction means “if the weather permits.” It
has its own subject, **weather**, rather than borrowing the main-clause
subject.

- **Weather permitting, the restoration work will begin on Monday.**
- The field team will survey the northern wetlands tomorrow, **weather
  permitting**.
- **Weather permitting, residents can attend the consultation outdoors.**

## C07-027

<!-- card
item: NF-026
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

1. The minister left the chamber, ___ questions from reporters.
   (the minister performed both actions)
2. The minister left the chamber, ___ reporters continued to ask questions.
   (the second action has a different subject)

### Back

1. The minister left the chamber, **avoiding questions from reporters**.
2. The minister left the chamber, **and reporters continued to ask
   questions**.

Use a participial supplement when its understood subject is clear and shared.
Use a finite coordinated clause when a separate subject or tense must be
expressed.

## C07-028

<!-- card
item: NF-027
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Use a perfect participial clause in sentence 1 and a finite **after** clause in
sentence 2.

1. ___ the consultation, the committee revised the proposal.
   (the committee completed both actions)
2. ___ the residents had completed the consultation, the committee revised
   the proposal. (the earlier clause has a different subject)

### Back

1. **Having completed the consultation, the committee revised the proposal.**
2. **After the residents had completed the consultation, the committee
   revised the proposal.**

Use a perfect participle for compact prior action by the main-clause subject.
Use a finite linker clause when the earlier action has its own subject.

## C07-029

<!-- card
item: NF-028
type: contrast
status: validated
-->

### Front

Use a passive participial form of **support** in sentence 1 and a finite
**because** clause in sentence 2.

1. ___ by multiple studies, the treatment remains part of the national
   guidelines. (the treatment is the affected subject)
2. ___ multiple studies support the treatment, it remains part of the
   national guidelines. (state the causal relation explicitly)

### Back

1. **Supported by multiple studies, the treatment remains part of the national
   guidelines.**
2. **Because multiple studies support the treatment, it remains part of the
   national guidelines.**

Use a past-participle supplement when its passive subject is shared and the
relation is clear. Use a finite **because** clause when the cause and its
subject should be explicit.
