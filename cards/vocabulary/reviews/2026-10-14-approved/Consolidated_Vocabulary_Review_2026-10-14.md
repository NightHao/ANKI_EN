#### magnificent

**ADJECTIVE**

1. Extremely beautiful, impressive, or excellent.

#### spectacular

**ADJECTIVE**

1. Extremely impressive or striking, especially to look at; also remarkable in scale or result.

   “The fireworks were spectacular.”

**Everyday situations: what you can say**

| Situation | Natural sentences | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| You are describing the view from your hotel. | “The view was magnificent.” / “The view was spectacular.” | Both are enthusiastic compliments; either works. |

**Difference / overlap**

Both mean extremely impressive and often work in the same sentence: “The view was magnificent/spectacular.” Learn common combinations rather than forcing a strict distinction. Spectacular also describes dramatic results, including a spectacular failure; magnificent normally expresses praise.

**Common patterns**

**induce**  
verb · /ɪnˈdus/ · 🔊

**ACTIVE**

#### entice

**VERB**

1. Attract someone by offering something appealing, often encouraging an action.

   “The discount enticed me to try the service.”

#### induce

**VERB**

1. Cause a state or effect; also influence someone to act, often through an incentive. Usually more formal than cause or persuade.

   “The medication can induce sleep.”

2. Influence someone to do something, often through an incentive; formal.

   “The offer induced several employees to stay.”

**Everyday situations: what you can say**

| Situation | Natural sentences | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| An appealing offer makes someone stay. | “The offer enticed him to stay.” / “The offer induced him to stay.” | Both work. Entice highlights appeal; induce sounds more formal. |
| Something causes a physical effect. | “The medication can induce sleep.” | Induce has a cause-an-effect sense; entice does not have this construction. |

**Difference / overlap**

Both can describe influencing someone to act: “The offer enticed/induced him to stay.” Entice highlights an appealing offer; induce is more formal and also describes causing an effect: “The medication can induce sleep.” These are useful contexts, not mutually exclusive categories.

**Common patterns**

- **entice:** entice someone to + verb; entice someone into + V-ing; entice someone with + offer
- **induce:** induce sleep/labor; induce someone to + verb

**Synonyms**

- **entice:** tempt — NEAR-INTERCHANGEABLE in appealing-offer contexts
- **induce:** cause — NEAR-INTERCHANGEABLE for effects; persuade — NEAR-INTERCHANGEABLE in some action contexts

**Antonyms**

- **entice:** deter — CONTEXTUAL OPPOSITE
- **induce:** prevent — CONTEXTUAL OPPOSITE for effects

**ACTIVE**

#### deprive

**VERB**

1. Take away or deny something needed, valued, or deserved.

   “The noise deprived us of sleep.”

#### deprecate

**VERB**

1. In software, mark a feature as discouraged or no longer recommended; also formally express disapproval.

   “We deprecated the old endpoint in favor of the new API.”

2. Express disapproval; formal and less common in everyday conversation.

   “The writer deprecated the use of violence.”

#### depreciate

**VERB**

1. Lose monetary value; in accounting, reduce an asset's recorded value over time.

   “Cars usually depreciate as they get older.”

2. Reduce an asset’s recorded value over time in accounting.

   “The company depreciates its equipment over five years.”

**Everyday situations: what you can say**

| Situation | Natural sentences | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| Noise keeps you from sleeping. | “The noise deprived me of sleep.” | Deprive someone of something. |
| An old API is no longer recommended. | “We deprecated the old API.” | Discouraged use; not necessarily removed. |
| A car loses value. | “Cars depreciate as they get older.” | Depreciate describes value or accounting. |

**Difference / overlap**

These three verbs are not synonyms. Deprive means deny something needed or valued; deprecate means discourage software use or formally disapprove; depreciate concerns monetary value or accounting. “The noise deprived me of sleep.” “We deprecated the old API.” “Cars depreciate.” A deprecated feature can still work; the status does not itself mean removal.

**Common patterns**

- **deprive:** deprive someone of + noun; be deprived of + noun
- **deprecate:** deprecate an API/feature; a deprecated method
- **depreciate:** depreciate in value; depreciate equipment over five years

**Synonyms**

- **deprive:** deny — RELATED; deny someone something
- **deprecate:** discourage — RELATED for software; disapprove of — NEAR-INTERCHANGEABLE for general disapproval
- **depreciate:** lose value — NEAR-INTERCHANGEABLE for market value

**Antonyms**

- **deprive:** provide — CONTEXTUAL OPPOSITE; provide someone with something
- **deprecate:** endorse — CONTEXTUAL OPPOSITE of disapproval
- **depreciate:** appreciate — DIRECT OPPOSITE for market value

#### responsible for

**ADJECTIVE PHRASE**

1. Having a duty, causing something, or bearing responsibility for a result.

   “I'm responsible for reviewing pull requests.”

2. Be the cause of something.

   “A faulty sensor was responsible for the shutdown.”

3. Bear responsibility or blame for a result.

   “You’ll be held responsible for any mistakes.”

#### accountable for

**ADJECTIVE PHRASE**

1. Responsible for actions or results and expected to explain or justify them.

   “I'm accountable for the release outcome.”

#### answerable to

**ADJECTIVE PHRASE**

1. Required to explain or justify your actions to a person or organization.

   “The director is answerable to the board.”

#### in charge of

**PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE**

1. Managing, directing, or having control over something.

   “Maya is in charge of the project.”

**Everyday situations: what you can say**

| Situation | Natural sentences | What to notice |
|---|---|---|

Responsible also describes causes: “A faulty sensor was responsible for the failure”; accountable would be unusual for a sensor.

- **accountable for:** be accountable for + noun/V-ing; be accountable to + person/body; hold someone accountable
- **answerable to:** be answerable to + person/body; be answerable for + action/result

#### sentence

**VERB**

1. officially impose a punishment in court.

**NOUN**

1. a punishment imposed by a court.

#### condemn

**VERB**

1. Express strong disapproval of someone or something.

   “The judge condemned his behavior.”

2. Formally sentence someone to a punishment, especially death.

**VERB**

**VERB**

|---|---|---|
