## P01: stand clear of + hazard

### Front

stand clear of + hazard

### Back

Use this to tell someone to move away from a hazard or leave room for something to move. It is common in safety announcements.

- Please stand clear of the closing doors.
- Stand clear of the loading area while the truck is reversing.
- Visitors must stand clear of the machinery.

## P02: stay clear of + person/thing/problem

### Front

stay clear of + person/thing/problem

### Back

Use this to tell someone to keep away from a danger or avoid involvement with a person, activity, or situation.

- Stay clear of the edge of the platform.
- I try to stay clear of office gossip.
- We should stay clear of investments we don’t understand.

## P04: Can I get it without + ingredient?

### Front

Can I get it without + ingredient?

### Back

Use this to ask for food or a drink with a particular ingredient omitted. Replace it with the item’s name when needed.

- Can I get it without onions?
- Can I get the sandwich without mayonnaise?
- Can I get the salad without cheese?

## P05: What does + item + come with?

### Front

What does + item + come with?

### Back

Use this to ask which accompaniments, accessories, or extras are included.

- What does the steak come with?
- What does this meal come with?
- What does the laptop come with?

## P06: What order number are you on?

### Front

What order number are you on?

### Back

Use this to ask which numbered order staff have reached in a queue. It asks about their progress, not specifically whether your own order is ready.

- What order number are you on right now?
- What number are you on? My order is 44.
- What order number are you on? We’ve been waiting for about twenty minutes.

## P08: I haven’t gotten my + item + yet.

### Front

I haven’t gotten my + item + yet.

### Back

Use this as a neutral reminder that something you expected has not arrived or been given to you. Gotten is standard American English here.

- I haven’t gotten my change yet.
- I haven’t gotten my refund yet.
- I haven’t gotten my boarding pass yet.

## P09: How much is it per + unit?

### Front

How much is it per + unit?

### Back

Use this to ask the price for each unit of weight, time, quantity, or distance.

- How much is it per pound?
- How much is it per hour?
- How much is the rental per day?

## P10: Is this charged by weight?

### Front

Is this charged by weight?

### Back

Use this to ask whether the price depends on how heavy something is, rather than being a fixed price per item or portion.

- Is this charged by weight?
- Is the salad charged by weight or by the container?
- Are these snacks charged by weight?

## P11: check bags through to + destination

### Front

check bags through to + destination

### Back

Use this when checked luggage is sent onward to a later or final destination. Ask the airline to confirm the arrangement for your particular itinerary.

- Will our bags be checked through to Taipei?
- Can you check this bag through to Los Angeles?
- Our bags were checked through to the final destination.

## P12: be coated in/with + substance

### Front

be coated in/with + substance

### Back

Use this when a substance forms a layer on a surface. In and with often overlap naturally.

- The cookies are coated in chocolate.
- The pan was coated with a thin layer of oil.
- The floor was coated in dust.

## P13: be packed in + liquid/container

### Front

be packed in + liquid/container

### Back

Use this to describe the liquid surrounding packaged food or the container used to hold an item. The complement determines which meaning is intended.

- The sardines are packed in olive oil.
- The peaches are packed in syrup.
- The samples were packed in insulated boxes.

## P14: be served + adjective / with + accompaniment

### Front

be served + adjective / with + accompaniment

### Back

Use served + adjective for a food’s condition when presented, and served with + noun for an accompaniment.

- The soup is served hot.
- The salmon is served with rice and vegetables.
- The dessert is served chilled.

## P15: turn into + result

### Front

turn into + result

### Back

Use this for a change in which something becomes a different thing or situation. Use turn + object + into + result when someone or something causes the change.

- Water turns into steam when it boils.
- The casual conversation turned into a heated debate.
- The renovation turned an empty warehouse into a community center.

## P18: That’s a valid concern.

### Front

That’s a valid concern.

### Back

Use this to acknowledge that someone has a legitimate reason to worry. It does not necessarily mean you agree with their proposed solution.

- That’s a valid concern. We should test the security implications before release.
- That’s a valid concern, especially if the role offers little job stability.
- That’s a valid concern. Let’s discuss how we could reduce the risk.

## P20: I’m not saying + clause

### Front

I’m not saying + clause

### Back

Use this to clarify what you are not claiming, often before stating your actual point.

- I’m not saying the idea is wrong. I’m saying we need more evidence.
- I’m not saying we should abandon the project, but we should reconsider the timeline.
- I’m not saying remote work suits everyone. It works well for me.

## P21: shift toward + focus/direction

### Front

shift toward + focus/direction

### Back

Use this for a gradual change in emphasis, activity, or preference. Shift + object + toward + noun/V-ing describes deliberately causing that change.

- My role is shifting toward system design and technical leadership.
- The company is shifting toward subscription-based services.
- We shifted our attention toward improving reliability.

## P24: If anything, + clause

### Front

If anything, + clause

### Back

Use this to suggest that the opposite interpretation, or a stronger version of your point, is more accurate.

- The change hasn’t simplified the process. If anything, it has made it more confusing.
- I don’t think remote work has reduced my productivity. If anything, I get more done.
- The trip didn’t make me less interested in living abroad. If anything, it strengthened that interest.

## P25: I’m not entirely convinced that + clause

### Front

I’m not entirely convinced that + clause

### Back

Use this to express measured doubt about a claim without rejecting it completely.

- I’m not entirely convinced that a larger team would solve the problem.
- I’m not entirely convinced that the salary increase would justify the longer commute.
- I’m not entirely convinced that this benchmark reflects real-world performance.

## P26: get + benefit/amount + out of + experience

### Front

get + benefit/amount + out of + experience

### Back

Use this to describe the benefit, enjoyment, or value gained from an activity or experience. Get the most out of emphasizes gaining as much as possible.

- I got a lot out of the design review.
- What do you hope to get out of this course?
- I want to get as much out of the trip as I can.

## P28: subject + do/does/did + base verb

### Front

subject + do/does/did + base verb

### Back

Use a stressed do, does, or did to emphasize an affirmative statement, often when correcting an assumption or acknowledging a point. The main verb stays in its base form.

- I do think AI will change parts of our work.
- She does understand the risks; she just disagrees about their likelihood.
- We did test the migration, but we missed that edge case.
