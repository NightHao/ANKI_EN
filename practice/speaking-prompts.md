# Speaking prompts

Use these prompts after studying a chapter. Record one answer, transcribe it, repair
only repeated problems, and answer again.

## Chapter prompts

1. **Time and tense:** Describe a skill you started learning and explain how your
   ability has changed.
2. **Future and conditionals:** Explain one realistic plan and what you would do if
   that plan became impossible.
3. **Modals and attitude:** Give advice to someone moving to your city and explain
   what difficulties they might face.
4. **Passive and reported meaning:** Describe a recent announcement or decision and
   report how people reacted.
5. **Questions and conversation control:** Explain what makes an interviewer or
   conversation partner easy to talk to.
6. **Verb patterns:** Discuss a habit you stopped, started, or tried to change.
7. **Nouns, articles, and quantity:** Describe the amount and type of information you
   consume online.
8. **Relative and reduced clauses:** Describe a person who influenced you and a piece
   of advice given by that person.
9. **Adjectives, adverbs, and comparison:** Compare two places where you have lived,
   worked, or studied.
10. **Linking ideas:** Discuss whether remote work is beneficial despite its
    disadvantages.
11. **Prepositions:** Describe a normal weekday with precise time, place, movement,
    and verb-preposition chunks.
12. **Phrasal verbs:** Tell a story about a plan that broke down, was put off, or
    eventually worked out.

## Three answer shapes

### Part 1: 20–30 seconds

```text
direct answer → one reason → one small example
```

### Part 2: 90–120 seconds

```text
context → sequence → important detail → result → reflection
```

### Part 3: 45–75 seconds

```text
position → reason → example → qualification or contrast → conclusion
```

## Review checklist

Do not count “advanced structures.” Ask:

- Is each event placed clearly in time?
- Did each complex sentence express a real relationship?
- Are articles and noun forms stable?
- Did I use complete chunks rather than translate prepositions?
- Can a listener follow my answer without reconstructing it?
- Did pauses come from thinking about content rather than rebuilding basic grammar?
