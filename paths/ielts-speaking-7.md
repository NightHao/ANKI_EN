# IELTS Speaking 7 grammar path

IELTS Speaking does not reward rare grammar for its own sake. At Band 7, the official
descriptor expects a range of structures used flexibly, frequent error-free sentences,
and effective simple and complex sentences despite some remaining errors.

Grammar is one of four equally weighted speaking criteria. Fluency and coherence,
lexical resource, and pronunciation matter just as much. See the
[official Speaking Band Descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf).

## Phase 1 — Make the sentence engine reliable

Use the [diagnostic](../diagnostic.md) first. Prioritise:

- Units 1–18: time and tense;
- Units 49–52: questions and response control;
- Units 69–76 and 79: nouns and articles;
- Units 84–91: reference and quantity;
- Units 100–101 and 109–110: adverbs and word order;
- Units 121–128: high-frequency time, place, and movement phrases.

Goal: produce ordinary sentences without repeated basic errors.

## Phase 2 — Build usable range

Study and speak with:

- Units 19–25 and 38–41: future choices and conditionals;
- Units 26–37: possibility, deduction, obligation, advice, and politeness;
- Units 42–48: passive and reported meaning;
- Units 53–68: verb patterns and compact clauses;
- Units 92–97: full and reduced relative clauses;
- Units 102–108: degree and comparison;
- Units 113–120: contrast, conditions, and time relationships.

Goal: combine structures because the meaning requires them, not because they look
advanced.

## Phase 3 — Sound natural under pressure

Use:

- [Natural speaking](natural-speaking.md) for focus, clarification, and turn structure;
- Units 129–136 for reliable collocations with prepositions;
- Units 137–145 for common phrasal-verb patterns;
- [Speaking prompts](../practice/speaking-prompts.md) for timed production.

Goal: maintain a long answer while choosing grammar without obvious effort.

## Weekly practice loop

Choose three target units, not fifteen.

1. Study one unit on three different days.
2. Create two true personal examples per unit.
3. Answer one Part 1 question for 30 seconds.
4. Answer one Part 2 or Part 3 question for 60–120 seconds.
5. Transcribe one answer exactly.
6. Mark only repeated errors and repair them using the relevant unit.
7. Record the same answer again without reading a script.

## Readiness check

You are approaching the grammar requirement for Band 7 when:

- simple and complex sentences are both accurate often enough to sound dependable;
- tense shifts remain clear during stories and hypothetical answers;
- relative, conditional, modal, and noun-clause structures appear naturally;
- you can paraphrase around a missing word without abandoning the sentence;
- self-correction is quick and does not destroy coherence;
- advanced-looking forms are optional rather than memorised decorations.

Finishing every file is not the goal. Flexible, accurate production is.
